"Shinjuku" on the Booking: Which Station Will You Actually Use?

💡 The quick answer

Pick Shinjuku-sanchome for most trips, because it costs less than the JR side and its subway lines reach Shibuya, Ikebukuro and Ginza without a change.

Why we say this ↓

Three hotels all say Shinjuku. They sit by three different stations, and that changes your mornings.

Answer A · For an early train out

Near JR Shinjuku

from ≈¥22,000 (rated 8+)

The Narita Express leaves from JR Shinjuku, with the first departure around 5:55 and a ride of about 1 hour 20 minutes to Narita Airport (¥3,250 standard seat). Rooms close to the station cost the most: on our date the well-rated options started around ¥22,000 to ¥27,000. One budget exception, Apartment Hotel Shinjuku (about 450 m away, about ¥12,500 with fees), sits between JR Shinjuku and Shinjuku-sanchome.

Suits: travellers with an early airport train or limited express and a big suitcase.

Answer B · For shopping and direct subway trips

Near Shinjuku-sanchome

from ≈¥15,000

A separate subway station on the east side with three lines: the Marunouchi Line toward Ginza and Tokyo Station, the Fukutoshin Line to Shibuya and Ikebukuro (with through trains to Yokohama), and the Toei Shinjuku Line. Its exits run straight into Isetan. On our date Hotel Tateshina came to about ¥15,300 and Hotel Rocks about ¥17,000 for two.

Suits: shoppers and anyone heading to Shibuya, Ikebukuro or Ginza most days.

Answer C · For the lowest price

Near Higashi-shinjuku

from ≈¥13,000

One stop north of Shinjuku-sanchome on the Fukutoshin Line, plus the Toei Oedo Line. On our date Sakura Cross Hotel Shinjuku East (rated 8.3) was about ¥13,700 and Shinjuku Granbell (8.0) about ¥14,600. Two catches: express trains on the Fukutoshin Line pass through without stopping, and for Roppongi on the Oedo Line you change at Tochomae.

Suits: budget-minded travellers happy to ride local trains and add one change on some days.

Our verdict

We suggest: pick Shinjuku-sanchome for most trips, because it costs less than the JR side and its subway lines reach Shibuya, Ikebukuro and Ginza without a change. Pay for JR Shinjuku only on nights before an early Narita Express or limited express. Choose Higashi-shinjuku if saving about ¥2,000 a night matters more than one extra stop.

One more thing: the station café commuters swear by

Right by JR Shinjuku's East Exit, in the basement of Lumine Est, is BERG, a tiny beer-and-coffee counter that has fed commuters since 1970. It opens at 7:00, so you can have rye bread, sausages and a coffee (or a morning beer, like some regulars) before the crowds. Tabelog put it on its 2025 list of the 100 best cafés in east Japan, and most visitors walk straight past it.

Quick answers

Which Shinjuku station is best for a hotel?

It depends on your trips. JR Shinjuku is best for early trains out of the city such as the Narita Express. Shinjuku-sanchome suits shopping and subway trips to Shibuya, Ikebukuro and Ginza. Higashi-shinjuku is usually cheapest and has the Oedo and Fukutoshin lines.

Is Higashi-shinjuku a good place to stay?

Yes for budget and subway access, with a catch: express and commuter-express trains on the Fukutoshin Line pass through without stopping, so you ride local trains from here. For Roppongi on the Oedo Line you change at Tochomae.

Is Shinjuku-sanchome the same as Shinjuku Station?

No. Shinjuku-sanchome is a separate subway station on Shinjuku's east side, served by the Marunouchi, Fukutoshin and Toei Shinjuku lines, with exits straight into Isetan department store. Hotels near it cost about ¥15,000 a night on our date, against over ¥22,000 right by JR Shinjuku.

How long is the Narita Express from Shinjuku?

About 1 hour 20 minutes to Narita Airport, with a standard reserved seat at ¥3,250. The first departure from Shinjuku is around 5:55.
