Asakusa, Tokyo · Things to do

Senso-ji Before Breakfast or After Dinner?

Same temple, three different visits. What you can actually see at each time.

Early morningDaytimeAfter dinner

Senso-ji's grounds never close, so both quiet times work, but they show you different things. Come early if you want to step inside the main hall: it opens at 6:30 from October to March (6:00 from April). Come after dinner for photos: the gates, the pagoda and the main hall stay lit until about 23:00, but the hall itself is shut after 17:00. Midday is for Nakamise's shops, and for crowds.

Checked 23 November 2026 · hours from GO TOKYO, japan-guide.com, the Asakusa tourist centre and MISOJYU

Answer A · Before breakfast

Early morning: inside the main hall

hall from 6:30 (Oct to Mar)

The grounds are open all night, so you can walk through Kaminarimon and Nakamise before the stalls open. The main hall opens at 6:30 in the colder months and 6:00 from April to September. Then walk to MISOJYU, a miso soup shop at 1-7-5 Asakusa, 3 minutes from Exit 1 of Asakusa Station on the Ginza Line. It serves a morning set (miso soup, a rice ball, half a seasoned egg and pickles) for ¥850 from 8:00 to 10:00.

Answer B · Daytime

Daytime: Nakamise open, and full

250 m · about 90 stalls

Daytime is the only time you get both things: the main hall open and the Nakamise stalls trading. Nakamise runs about 250 m from Kaminarimon to Hozomon, with around 90 stalls selling snacks, sweets and souvenirs. This is also the most crowded part of the visit, so expect to move slowly from Kaminarimon to Hozomon.

Answer C · After dinner

After dinner: lit up, quieter, hall closed

lights until ≈23:00

Kaminarimon, Hozomon, the five-storied pagoda and the main hall are lit every day from sunset until about 23:00. Our short photo route: start on the free 8th-floor terrace of the Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center (open to 22:00), opposite Kaminarimon, for the view down Nakamise. Cross to Kaminarimon, walk Nakamise to see the painted shutters on the closed stalls, then go through Hozomon to the pagoda and the front of the main hall.

Our verdict

We suggest: come after dinner if photos are the point: you get the lights, quieter paths and the shutter murals. Choose the morning instead if you want to go inside the main hall without crowds: arrive at 6:30 from October to March (6:00 from April), then have breakfast at MISOJYU from 8:00. Come in the daytime only if the Nakamise shops matter to you. If you're staying in Asakusa, you can do both quiet visits on one day.

One more thing: stay for rakugo after dark

If you pick the evening, walk five minutes west to Asakusa Engei Hall, one of only four theatres in Tokyo that stage rakugo (comic storytelling) every day of the year. You buy a ticket at the door on the day and can drift in and out between acts. Most of it is in Japanese, but the mix of storytellers, manzai pairs and magic is easy to enjoy even without the words.

Asakusa Engei Hall, 1-43-12 Asakusa, about 1 min from Tsukuba Express Asakusa Exit A1. Open daily, roughly 11:30–20:45; general admission about ¥3,500, cash. Sources: GO TOKYO (Tokyo Convention & Visitors Bureau) and NAVITIME.

Quick answers

What time does Senso-ji open?

The grounds are always open and entry is free. The main hall opens at 6:00 from April to September and at 6:30 from October to March, and closes at 17:00.

Is Senso-ji lit up at night?

Yes. Kaminarimon, Hozomon, the five-storied pagoda and the main hall are lit every day from sunset until about 23:00, and the closed Nakamise shops show painted shutters. You can walk the grounds at night, but the main hall itself is closed after 17:00.

Where can I eat breakfast near Senso-ji?

MISOJYU, a miso soup shop at 1-7-5 Asakusa (3 minutes from Ginza Line Asakusa Station Exit 1), serves a morning set for ¥850 from 8:00 to 10:00: miso soup, a rice ball, half a seasoned egg and pickles.

How long is Nakamise shopping street?

About 250 m, from Kaminarimon to Hozomon, with around 90 stalls selling snacks and souvenirs. It is the busiest walk at Senso-ji when the shops are open.
