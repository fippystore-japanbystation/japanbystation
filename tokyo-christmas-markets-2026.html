Tokyo Christmas Markets 2026: Dates, Entry, Food and Which One

💡 The quick answer

Seven of the nine big Christmas markets in and around Tokyo are free to walk into this year; you pay only for what you eat and drink.

Nine markets in and around Tokyo, each with its official 2026 dates, hours, entry, mug, what to eat, the nearest station and the quietest time to go.

Seven of the nine big Christmas markets in and around Tokyo are free to walk into this year; you pay only for what you eat and drink. The two that charge are the Tokyo Christmas Market, which opens at Shiba Park on 4 December under Tokyo Tower and is the most German of them all, and Yokohama Red Brick. For one evening we'd pair Shiba Park with the free markets at Tokyo Tower and Azabudai Hills, all in one corner of Minato. Every market on this page ends on 25 December.

Tokyo Christmas Market: Shiba Park and Shin-Toyosu

The market that began in Hibiya Park in 2015 is the closest thing in Tokyo to a German Christmas market: wooden huts, a 14 m Christmas pyramid from the woodcarving village of Seiffen, Glühwein, nutcrackers and a stage. In 2026 it has two venues with two moods: Shiba Park ("the forest", with Tokyo Tower behind it) and Shin-Toyosu ("the sea", on the waterfront).

  • Dates and hours: Shiba Park 4–25 Dec, 11:00–22:00 (opening day from 16:00), last entry 21:00, last orders 21:30. Shin-Toyosu 20 Nov–25 Dec, 11:00–21:30, last entry 20:30 (temporarily closed from 26 Nov).
  • Entry: adults ¥1,000 on weekdays, ¥1,500 at weekends (Shin-Toyosu also on public holidays) until 23 Dec; ¥2,000 on 24–25 Dec. Elementary school children half, preschoolers free. Re-entry the same day is allowed; the organisers ask you to keep your visit to about 3 hours.
  • How to book: timed tickets in 30-minute slots on KKday (linked from the official site) or at FamilyMart. Advance tickets can't be cancelled or changed. On crowded days same-day entry may be limited, so book weekends ahead.
  • The mug: each advance adult ticket includes the venue's own mug, ultramarine at Shin-Toyosu and deep "kermes" red at Shiba Park. Without one, hot drinks come in a deposit mug: keep it and the deposit isn't refunded. Rinse your mug at the sinks and refill it for the price of the drink only.
  • Getting there: Shiba Park is 1 minute from Onarimon Station, Exit A6 (Toei Mita Line). It is not Shibakoen Station. Other options: Kamiyacho Exit 3 (10 min), Daimon Exit A6 (10 min), JR Hamamatsucho North Exit (12 min). Shin-Toyosu is 2 minutes from Shin-toyosu Station on the Yurikamome, or take the Tokyo BRT from Shimbashi (about 10 min) to Toyosu Shijo-mae, then walk 2 minutes.

What to eat and drink

The official Shin-Toyosu menu lists Glühwein in three colours (red, rosé and white), honey-and-apple Glühwein, Kinderpunsch and plenty of hot chocolate for non-drinkers. For food: sausage platters, schnitzel with porcini sauce, Flammkuchen, Hokkaido venison steak, stew served in a bread bowl, a whole cheese fondue in bread, cheese-topped lángos and face-sized pretzels, plus German beers from Hofbräu and Berlin's BRLO. Shiba Park's own menu wasn't published when we checked. Prices aren't listed online; every stall takes cash or cashless.

Quietest time: a weekday afternoon. Weekend evenings are when same-day entry can be limited, and 24–25 December cost the most.

Tokyo Tower Christmas Market: free, under the tower

On the plaza in front of Tokyo Tower's main entrance, 13 November–25 December, 12:00–22:00 (last orders 21:30), free to enter. It's small, eight huts, but you eat with the lit-up tower directly overhead. Expect grilled sausages, pretzels, pasta, churros, hot wine and hot chocolate, plus a stall of Christmas goods. Nearest stations: Kamiyacho (Hibiya Line), Akabanebashi (Oedo Line) and Onarimon, which makes it an easy add-on to Shiba Park.

Azabudai Hills: the roomy one

Azabudai Hills fills its Central Square (the arena and lawn) with 19 shops, 15 of them food and drink, under a glowing arched gate, 21 November–25 December, 11:00–21:00, free entry. With almost twice Roppongi's number of stalls, it suits couples and anyone who wants dinner with their Glühwein. Kamiyacho Station is the closest.

Roppongi Hills: the 20th edition

Roppongi Hills' market at O-YANE Plaza is one of Japan's longest-running, and 2026 is its 20th year: about ten shops recreating a corner of Stuttgart's market, led by Käthe Wohlfahrt's German ornaments, with Glühwein, sausages, stollen and fresh pretzels. 21 November–25 December, 11:00–21:00, free. There are anniversary-only items and, on weekdays, lunch sets. After 17:00, walk down Keyakizaka under the "Snow & Blue" lights (until 22:00). Roppongi Station, Hibiya Line Exit 1C, is a minute away.

Skytree Town: the picture-book one

Up on the 4th-floor Sky Arena of Tokyo Skytree Town, 5 November–25 December, 11:00–22:00 (last orders 21:00), with 15 European-style huts and a picture-book theme. No admission ticket is listed, and it goes ahead in rain. Drinks come in boot-shaped mugs, five new Grimm-inspired designs this year, and the food leans cute and filling: Santa's sliced stollen (a Solamachi classic), a 67 cm spiral Weisswurst, smoked bone-in chicken, a new strawberry-and-custard dessert pizza and a strawberry Baumkuchen. Nearest stations: Oshiage and Tokyo Skytree.

Arriving on the Tokaido Shinkansen? Show the paper "EX seat slip" at the listed hut and answer a survey for one free drink (JR Central's #Tokyo ZokuZoku campaign).

Ueno Park: Tokyo Christmas Advent

The Tokyo edition of Fukuoka's winter event takes over Ueno Park, free to enter, 6 November–25 December: Hakamagoshi Plaza 12:00–23:00, and the Shinobazu Pond area since 18 November (weekdays 15:00–22:00, weekends and holidays 12:00–22:00). More than 30 huts serve Christmas food, hot wine and hot chocolate, including a first KitKat café, and there are Tokyo-only mugs, even a "rain-day" and a "snow-day" mug sold only on those days. An outdoor circus and ticketed concerts at the Shinobazu open-air stage run in December. Nearest: JR Ueno and Keisei Ueno.

Marunouchi and Ebisu: markets you walk through

Marunouchi Street Park turns Naka-dori's 1.2 km of lit trees into a street with tables and ten market stalls and kitchen cars (12 November–25 December), and from 27 November Gyoko-dori, the avenue between Tokyo Station and the Imperial Palace, adds a tree and 15 hut-style food stalls: butter chicken curry, coarse sausages, cocoa. Free, and right outside Tokyo Station.

Ebisu Garden Place runs a small Christmas Marche around its 10 m tree and the giant Baccarat chandelier: Clock Square from 14 November, Chateau Square from 27 November, both to 25 December, weekdays 17:00–20:00, weekends 12:00–20:00. The Westin Tokyo's food truck serves borscht, coconut chicken curry, hot wine and hot chocolate. Ebisu Station is about 5 minutes away; the chandelier stays lit until 11 January 2027.

Yokohama Red Brick: the big harbour night

The biggest of them all (about 1.21 million visitors last year, by the venue's count) runs 20 November–25 December: 11:00–21:00 until 4 December, then 11:00–22:00 (last orders 30 minutes before). About 60 stalls, an 11 m live spruce with a different light show every 15 minutes, a 30 m light roof and a 20 m light tunnel, with the harbour behind it. New this year: live-kitchen huts (sausages grilled to order, prosciutto sliced off the leg, Krapfen doughnuts fried in front of you), and white Glühwein, a German trend, at about 20 stalls.

  • Entry: from ¥500, depending on the day and ticket type; part of the site is free. A web-only priority entry ticket cuts the wait at the gate, and a new combo adds a one-way Seabus ride.
  • The mug: the yearly original mug comes in white and red; this year's theme is "the Christmas sky". A Noritake-designed premium mug comes only with the priority ticket or the Premium Lounge.
  • Getting there: Bashamichi or Nihon-odori (Minatomirai Line), about 6 minutes on foot; Sakuragicho about 15.

Three markets in one evening (from 4 December)

Shiba Park, Tokyo Tower and Azabudai Hills sit in one corner of Minato, so you can do all three without a long ride.

  1. 16:00 Shiba Park from Onarimon Exit A6. Use your timed ticket, get the red mug, and have your first Glühwein while it's still light.
  2. 17:30 Tokyo Tower market on the plaza in front of the tower: a pretzel or churros with the tower lit up overhead.
  3. 18:30 Azabudai Hills (Kamiyacho side): dinner from the 15 food stalls under the arched lights.
  4. 20:00 Still going? One stop on the Hibiya Line from Kamiyacho to Roppongi for Keyakizaka's lights (on until 22:00); Roppongi Hills' stalls close at 21:00.

Before 4 December, swap Shiba Park for Roppongi Hills. Check walking routes on your map app; the Tokyo Christmas Market's last entry is 21:00.

Which one for you

Our picks

  • One market only: Shiba Park, for the pyramid and the red mug.
  • No ticket, no planning: Tokyo Tower plus Azabudai Hills.
  • Families: Skytree Town (cute food, boot mugs) or Ueno (the biggest free site).
  • Couples: Yokohama Red Brick at dusk, or Ebisu's chandelier with a hot wine.
  • Short on time near Tokyo Station: Marunouchi's Gyoko-dori huts.

Think twice

  • 24–25 December at the paid markets: ¥2,000 and at their busiest.
  • Shin-Toyosu: closed temporarily from 26 November; check before you travel out to the bay.
  • Ebisu on weekdays: the marche opens only 17:00–20:00.
  • Expecting European-sized markets: Roppongi and Tokyo Tower have about ten huts or fewer.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Every market here ends on 25 December. Land on the 26th and they're gone until next November.
  • Tokyo Christmas Market tickets can't be changed once bought, and the mug comes only with advance adult tickets, while stocks last.
  • Cash: the Tokyo Christmas Market takes cash and cashless at every stall; at smaller markets it varies, so carry some yen.
  • Hotels: December weekends fill early near Roppongi; most listings offer free cancellation, so you can hold a room now and decide later.

One more thing: the two mugs that stack

The Tokyo Christmas Market's two 2026 mugs are designed as a pair: put one on top of the other and they form a small Christmas pyramid, with figures drawn around both. If Shin-Toyosu reopens, collectors can do both venues; if not, Shiba Park's red one alone still gets you refills at the price of the drink.

Quick answers

Is the Tokyo Christmas Market free in 2026?

No. At Shiba Park (4–25 Dec) adults pay ¥1,000 on weekdays and ¥1,500 at weekends until 23 December, and ¥2,000 on 24 and 25 December; elementary school children pay half and preschool children are free. The same prices apply at Shin-Toyosu, which was closed temporarily on 26 November 2026. Re-entry on the same day is allowed.

Which Tokyo Christmas markets are free?

Roppongi Hills (O-YANE Plaza), Azabudai Hills (Central Square), Tokyo Tower (front plaza), Ueno Park's Tokyo Christmas Advent, Marunouchi Street Park and the Ebisu Garden Place Christmas Marche are free to enter, and Skytree Town lists no ticket. You only pay for food, drinks and mugs.

Do I need to book tickets for Tokyo Christmas markets?

Only for the paid ones. The Tokyo Christmas Market sells timed tickets in 30-minute slots on KKday and at FamilyMart; the organisers say same-day entry may be limited when it's crowded, and only advance adult tickets include the venue's mug. Yokohama Red Brick sells a web-only priority entry ticket. The free markets need no booking.

How much is a Christmas market mug in Tokyo?

It depends on the market. At the Tokyo Christmas Market the mug comes free with an advance adult ticket; otherwise drinks come in a deposit mug that you may keep by giving up the deposit. Skytree Town serves drinks in boot-shaped mugs (five designs in 2026), and Yokohama Red Brick sells a yearly original mug in white and red.
