Lake Kawaguchi by Bike: Is Half a Day Enough?

💡 The quick answer

Pick the plan by your train home. On the 14:58, ride to the Kawaguchiko Ohashi Bridge and back on Q-BIKE (about ¥2,200 for two hours) and have lunch near the station.

Why we say this ↓

Your train back to Tokyo decides how far you can ride. So pick the train first, then the route.

Short rideNorth shoreSkip the bike

Half a day gets you one stretch of shore and lunch. Not the whole lake: that's about 20 km around. On the 14:58 Fuji Excursion back? We'd ride from the station to the Kawaguchiko Ohashi Bridge and back, then eat. Catching the 16:48 instead? Then you have time to ride the north shore to Oishi Park and have lunch there. And if it's windy, wet or tight on time, leave the bike. The Red Line bus gets to Oishi Park in 27 minutes and comes every 15 minutes.

Answer A · Short ride

Station to the bridge and back, then lunch

About 2 hours · ¥2,200

Q-BIKE is Fujikyu Railway's bike share. In the Kawaguchiko area it costs ¥1,100 per 60 minutes, paid in its app by credit card (you need a phone that can receive SMS), so two hours is ¥2,200. Ride from the station to the Kawaguchiko Ohashi Bridge and Ubuyagasaki at its north end, about 1.6 km away in a straight line. The town's tourism association lists both as Fuji viewpoints, and the bridge has a footpath where you can stop.

Set the turnaround by the train, not by the view. For the 14:58 Fuji Excursion, we would be back at a station-area port by about 13:00, then eat. Kosaku's Kawaguchi-ko branch, about 1.2 km from the station, is open 11:00–20:00 (last order 19:30) and takes cards, per Tabelog. Return the bike in the Kawaguchiko area: Q-BIKE charges for any bike left in another area until it is collected.

Answer B · Longer ride

North shore to Oishi Park, lunch on the way

About 3.4 km each way · ¥3,300 max

Oishi Park is on the north shore, about 3.4 km from the station in a straight line (further by road around the east shore). The tourism association calls the north shore the side where Fuji's ridge shows all the way down. For lunch, Hoto Fudo's north main store at Kawaguchi 707 opens 11:00–19:00 (or until the noodles run out), every day, per Yamanashi's tourism site, with its hoto at ¥1,210.

Hours decide this one. The app is capped at ¥3,300 a day; the paper ¥4,400 day pass from the station baggage storage starts at 10:00 and the bike must be back by 17:00. On the 8:30 Fuji Excursion from Shinjuku you arrive at 10:26, so you are riding by about 10:45. That fits the 16:48 train comfortably. For the 14:58, the ride, lunch and the way back become a race, so take Answer A. Fun Japan suggests an electric-assist bike if hills or distance worry you.

Answer C · Skip the bike

Red Line bus to the north shore, then walk

¥1,500 day pass · 27 min

Fujikyu's Red Line sightseeing bus leaves Kawaguchiko Station every 15 minutes from 9:00. It reaches the Music and Forest Museum stop in 19 minutes and Oishi Park in 27. The tourism association recommends the brick promenade between Kawaguchiko Circle Hall and the Museum of Art, where Fuji is in view the whole way; that is a walk, not a ride. A 1-Day Pass is ¥1,500.

The bus also removes the deadline stress. Buses back from Oishi Park leave every 15 minutes and take 27 minutes: the 14:12 reaches the station at 14:39, in time for the 14:58 train. Lunch at Hoto Fudo's north store fits on the way, near the Music and Forest Museum area. Choose this when the wind is up, the road is wet or you have only three hours.

Our verdict

We suggest: pick the plan by your train home. On the 14:58, ride to the Kawaguchiko Ohashi Bridge and back on Q-BIKE (about ¥2,200 for two hours) and have lunch near the station. On the 16:48 or later, ride the north shore to Oishi Park and eat at Hoto Fudo's north store. If the wind or rain makes riding unpleasant, or you only have three hours, take the Red Line bus and walk the promenade instead. The answer changes if you have no credit card for the app: then the ¥4,400 station day pass, available from 10:00, is your only bike, and the bus may be the better buy.

One more thing: the "THE Mount Fuji" stop

Four minutes before Oishi Park, the Red Line stops at Nagasaki Park entrance. The town's tourism association calls Nagasaki Park the place for the classic picture, just the lake and the mountain: it sits on a small peninsula, nothing blocks the view, and the far shore is flat with no tall buildings, so you see the longest stretch of Fuji's ridge on both sides.

Quick answers

How much does it cost to rent a bike at Kawaguchiko Station?

Q-BIKE, Fujikyu Railway's bike share, charges ¥1,100 for the first 60 minutes and ¥1,100 for each further hour in the Kawaguchiko area, capped at ¥3,300 a day, paid in its app by credit card. A ¥4,400 day pass is sold at the Kawaguchiko Station baggage storage (10:00–17:00) and the bike must be back by 17:00.

How long does it take to bike around Lake Kawaguchiko?

Longer than half a day once you stop for photos and lunch. The lake is about 20 km around, and Yamanashi's official Lake Kawaguchiko Tour Course, flat and marked with a blue line on the road, is about 26 km. With an afternoon train, we suggest an out-and-back instead: the Ohashi Bridge for the 14:58, or Oishi Park (about 3.4 km) for the 16:48.

How do I get to Oishi Park without a bike?

Take Fujikyu's Red Line sightseeing bus from Kawaguchiko Station: every 15 minutes from 9:00, 27 minutes to Kawaguchiko Natural Life Center (Oishi Park). A 1-day pass is ¥1,500. Buses back leave Oishi Park every 15 minutes until 17:57, with a last one at 18:27.

Where can I eat lunch on the north shore of Lake Kawaguchi?

Hoto Fudo's Kawaguchi-ko north main store (Kawaguchi 707) is open 11:00–19:00, or until the noodles run out, with no closing day, per Yamanashi's official tourism site. Its signature hoto was listed at ¥1,210. If you'd rather not ride, the Red Line bus reaches Oishi Park in 27 minutes.
