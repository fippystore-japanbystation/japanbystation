Minoh Falls to Katsuo-ji: Walk On, or Take the Bus?

💡 The quick answer

Walk, but flip the direction. There has been no bus between Minoh Falls and Katsuo-ji since Minoh City's weekend trial bus ended in March 2025, so the choice is the trail (about 80 minutes), the road, or a taxi.

The waterfall and the temple of a thousand daruma are about 80 minutes apart on foot, and no bus joins them. Here's the order that makes the day easy.

Our pick: ride Hankyu Bus 30 up to Katsuo-ji from Minoh-Kayano Station, see the daruma temple while you're fresh, then walk downhill past the falls to Hankyu Minoh Station. You climb nothing you don't have to, and you never wait for a bus on a mountain road.

Who this walk suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Walkers who want forest, a waterfall and a temple in one day out of Osaka
  • Anyone with an IC card (ICOCA, Suica): the bus is now cashless
  • Autumn visitors: Katsuo-ji's leaves usually peak mid-November to early December

Think twice

  • Strollers and wheelchairs: the trail has steps and forest paths
  • Late starters: the last bus leaves Katsuo-ji at 17:05, and the trail is dark early in winter
  • Rainy days: stone steps and rocks get slippery

Three ways between the falls and the temple

A · Walk the trail (about 80 minutes). Minoh is the western start of the Tokai Nature Trail. The Forestry Agency gives about 50 minutes from the falls up to the Minoh Visitor Center, then about 30 minutes to Katsuo-ji. Expect stairs above the falls and a forest path after that. A thinning project has closed Nature Study Route No. 4 and its branch until 26 February 2027, but the main Tokai Nature Trail is not on the closure list, so stay on the signed main trail.

B · Walk the road (about 3.5 km). Prefectural Route 43 climbs from the falls area to the temple. It is paved and signposted, but you share it with cars, and it is uphill all the way from the falls. We'd only take it if the trail is wet.

C · Taxi. osaka-info.jp gives about 15 minutes by taxi from Hankyu Minoh Station to Katsuo-ji. We found no official fare. It's the easy fix for tired legs or a late start.

Our suggestion: do it in reverse. Bus 30 up to Katsuo-ji, then walk A downhill. You get the temple before the midday crowds and the waterfall as the finale.

Getting there

To Katsuo-ji: take the Midosuji Line north. Since March 2024 its trains run through on the Kita-Osaka Kyuko extension to Minoh-Kayano, so from Namba, Umeda or Shin-Osaka you don't change. Bus 30 leaves from the station's bus terminal (tickets from staff at stop 8), takes about 20 minutes and costs ¥800. Since 1 October 2026 it takes no cash: tap an IC card (no top-ups on board) or buy a ticket by credit card at stop 8. Buses up run about every 30 minutes from 9:00 to 15:00, more often on weekend mornings. Buses from Senri-Chuo and Kita-Senri no longer go to Katsuo-ji.

Home from Minoh Falls: about 40 minutes down the riverside path to Hankyu Minoh Station, then the Minoh Line to Ishibashi Handai-mae and the Takarazuka Line to Osaka-umeda. From Kyoto, Hankyu takes about 1 hour 6 to 12 minutes from Kyoto-Kawaramachi, changing at Juso and Ishibashi Handai-mae, for about ¥540.

Katsuo-ji, the temple of winning daruma

Katsuo-ji opens 8:00–17:00 on weekdays, Sundays and holidays and 8:00–18:00 on Saturdays (last entry 30 minutes before), and entry is ¥500. Daruma sit everywhere: on walls, lanterns, rocks and roofs, left by people who came here before an exam, an operation or a match. The temple sells its own Kachi-Daruma ("winning daruma"), and it frames it as a vow to beat your own weakness rather than a wish. The official site recommends web tickets to skip the queue at the gate in busy weeks.

Eat like a local

  • Momiji tempura: maple leaves fried in sweet batter, the snack of Minoh's falls road. Hisakuni Kousendo (1-1-40 Minoh, a few minutes from Hankyu Minoh Station) is open 9:30–18:00 and closed on Thursdays. In November and December it sells the tempura by phone reservation only, so on an autumn day try the stalls along the path, or its café annex Kobeni for maple soft-serve.
  • Lunch: eat near Minoh Station after the walk. Katsuo-ji is up a mountain road, and we'd carry water and a snack for the trail.
  • A beer on the way home: our Minoh Falls afternoon guide has the brewery taproom one stop down the line.

A half-day route, uphill by bus, downhill on foot

  1. 8:30 Midosuji Line to Minoh-Kayano (through trains from Namba, Umeda and Shin-Osaka).
  2. 9:00 First bus 30 from Minoh-Kayano, tap your IC card.
  3. 9:20 Katsuo-ji: the daruma, the pond and the leaves before the tour buses.
  4. 10:40 Leave on the Tokai Nature Trail towards the Minoh Visitor Center (about 30 min).
  5. 11:10 Down through the forest to Minoh Falls (about 50 min).
  6. 12:15 The 33 m falls, then the riverside path down to Hankyu Minoh Station (about 40 min) and lunch.

📅 Good to know before you go

  • Last bus 17:05: buses up run 9:00 to 15:00, and the last one down from Katsuo-ji leaves at 17:05.
  • Cash won't work on bus 30 since 1 October 2026. IC cards can't be topped up on board.
  • Autumn: leaves usually peak from mid-November to early December, the busiest weeks of the year here; the temple suggests web tickets.
  • Trail closures: side routes (Nature Study Route No. 4 and its branch) are closed until 26 February 2027 for forest thinning. Follow the main Tokai Nature Trail signs.

Nearby, if you have more time

  • Minoh Falls on an autumn afternoon: the falls alone from Umeda, with the evening light-up.
  • Cup Noodles Museum Ikeda: on the Hankyu Takarazuka Line you ride home on.
  • Itami Sky Park: planes landing overhead, also north of Osaka.

One more thing: give your daruma one eye, not two

At Katsuo-ji you don't just buy a Kachi-Daruma. You write your goal on it, pass it through the incense smoke, and paint in its right eye (the one on your left as you face it) to seal the promise. The second eye waits until the goal is reached, and people bring the finished daruma back to the temple's shelves. Pick a goal you'll actually work on: that's the whole point here.

Quick answers

Is there a bus from Minoh Falls to Katsuo-ji?

Not in 2026. Minoh City's weekend sightseeing bus between the falls and Katsuo-ji was a trial that ended on 30 March 2025, and no regular bus stops at the falls. Walk the trail (about 80 minutes), take a taxi, or reach Katsuo-ji by bus 30 from Minoh-Kayano Station.

How long is the walk from Minoh Falls to Katsuo-ji?

About 80 minutes on the Tokai Nature Trail, using the Forestry Agency's times: about 50 minutes from the falls to the Minoh Visitor Center, then about 30 minutes to Katsuo-ji. It climbs steps and forest paths, so wear proper shoes and allow more in rain.

How much is the bus to Katsuo-ji and can I pay cash?

Bus 30 from Minoh-Kayano costs ¥800 for adults and ¥400 for children and takes about 20 minutes. Since 1 October 2026 it is cashless: tap an IC card such as ICOCA or Suica (you cannot top up on board), or buy a ticket by card from staff at stop 8. The last bus back leaves Katsuo-ji at 17:05.

What are Katsuo-ji's opening hours and entry fee?

8:00–17:00 on weekdays, Sundays and holidays, and 8:00–18:00 on Saturdays, with last entry 30 minutes before closing. Entry is ¥500 for high-school age and up, ¥400 for elementary and junior-high pupils.
