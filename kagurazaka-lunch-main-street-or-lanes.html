Kagurazaka Lunch: Main Street or Back Lanes?

You have one lunch break in Kagurazaka. Here is how to see the neighbourhood without spending it hunting for a table.

Main streetBack lanesWalk first

With little time, eat on the main street. Tonkatsu Kagurazaka Sakura on Kagurazaka-dori is open all day and has weekday lunch sets from ¥990. For the alley atmosphere Kagurazaka is known for, book a table at Kagurazaka Oishinbo, an old house on a stone-paved lane (last lunch order 14:00, closed Sundays). If your schedule is flexible, walk the lanes first and eat later. Le Bretagne serves its lunch menu after 14:00 too, and is closed on Mondays.

Answer A · Main street, no planning

A weekday set on Kagurazaka-dori

from ¥990 · L.O. 22:00

Tonkatsu Kagurazaka Sakura's main store is at Kagurazaka 5-1-1, on the main street about 150 m (2 minutes) from Bishamonten temple. It is open every day from 11:00 to 23:00 with last orders at 22:00, so there is no lunch cut-off to race. The weekday-only lunch menu lists prices with tax: a coarse-ground minced cutlet set at ¥990, a pork loin cutlet set at ¥1,100 and a fillet set at ¥1,380. Iidabashi Station's B3 exit is a 4-minute walk.

Answer B · Back lanes, booked

Yuba lunch in an old house on a stone alley

from ¥1,500 · L.O. 14:00

Kagurazaka Oishinbo, at Kagurazaka 4-8, describes itself as an old house with a black board fence on a stone-paved lane. Google Maps puts it 230 m, about 3 minutes, from Bishamonten. Lunch runs 11:30 to 15:00 with last orders at 14:00, and it is closed on Sundays. Its signature lunch, yuba-meshi (tofu-skin rice), starts at ¥1,500. There are 50 seats: a counter, tatami and sunken-table seating, and private rooms for 2 up to 20 people. It takes reservations by phone or online. If you book seats only and don't turn up, it charges ¥5,000 per person.

Answer C · Walk first, eat later

A lane walk ending at galettes after 2 p.m.

≈¥1,680 · closed Mon

From Iidabashi Station, walk up Kagurazaka-dori to Bishamonten (280 m, about 5 minutes). Hyogo Yokocho begins at a narrow passage across the street from the temple. It is a pedestrian-only stone lane, at its entrance barely wide enough for one person. Kakurenbo Yokocho, a small maze of lanes, lies between Kagurazaka Naka-dori and Honda Yokocho. Both are public walkways, but people live there, so GLTJP asks visitors to keep their voices down. Stay in the lanes and don't step into doorways or gardens. Finish at Le Bretagne (Kagurazaka 4-2), a Breton crêperie 140 m from Bishamonten. It opens at 11:30, serves its lunch menu after 14:00 too, and stays open into the evening. Lunch averages ¥1,680 with a drink or dessert. It has 38 seats and is closed on Mondays.

Our verdict

We suggest: walk the lanes first and eat a late lunch. Start at Bishamonten, wander Hyogo Yokocho and Kakurenbo Yokocho, then sit down at Le Bretagne after 14:00, when you no longer need to watch the clock. This changes by day and by time. On a Monday, Le Bretagne is closed, so eat at Tonkatsu Sakura on the main street instead. If your break is fixed between 11:30 and 14:00 and you want the alley setting, book Oishinbo (not Sundays). If you have less than an hour, skip the hunt and take the ¥990 weekday set on Kagurazaka-dori.

One more thing: the glass shrine at the top of the hill

After lunch, walk to the top of Kagurazaka to Akagi Shrine. It is a centuries-old shrine that architect Kengo Kuma rebuilt in 2010 as a calm glass-and-wood hall, so it looks nothing like the shrines on most itineraries. It is a short walk from Exit 1 of Kagurazaka Station, free to enter, and has its own café if lunch ran short.

Akagi Shrine, 1-10 Akagimotomachi, Shinjuku-ku; near Kagurazaka Station (Tozai line) Exit 1. Sources: GO TOKYO, Time Out Tokyo, Lonely Planet.

Quick answers

Where can I eat lunch on Kagurazaka's main street without a long search?

Tonkatsu Kagurazaka Sakura (Kagurazaka 5-1-1) is on Kagurazaka-dori, about 150 m from Bishamonten. It is open every day 11:00–23:00 (last order 22:00), and its weekday-only lunch menu starts at ¥990 for a minced cutlet set, with a pork loin cutlet set at ¥1,100.

Do I need to book lunch in Kagurazaka's back lanes?

For a set time or a private room, yes. Kagurazaka Oishinbo, an old house on a stone-paved alley at Kagurazaka 4-8, serves lunch 11:30–15:00 (last order 14:00), is closed on Sundays, and takes reservations by phone or online. It has counter seats, tatami rooms and private rooms for 2 to 20 people. A seat-only booking you don't show up for is charged ¥5,000 per person.

Are Kagurazaka's alleys like Hyogo Yokocho open to visitors?

Yes, they are pedestrian lanes you can walk through, but they include private homes. the Japan travel guide GLTJP asks visitors to keep noise to a minimum. Hyogo Yokocho's narrow entrance is across Kagurazaka-dori from Bishamonten; Kakurenbo Yokocho lies between Kagurazaka Naka-dori and Honda Yokocho.

Where can I still get lunch in Kagurazaka after 2 p.m.?

Le Bretagne (Kagurazaka 4-2), a Breton crêperie about 140 m from Bishamonten, serves its lunch menu after 14:00 too. It opens at 11:30, runs into the evening, and is closed on Mondays. Lunch averages ¥1,680 with a drink or dessert.
