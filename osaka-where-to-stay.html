Namba

Food, neon and the airport train at your door.

1

What it's like

This is the Osaka on postcards: neon over the canal, takoyaki on every corner and streets that stay busy late. The airport train from Kansai ends right here, so you can be eating dinner within an hour of landing.

2

What's nearby

Starts at the Dotonbori canal and runs north. Shopping, rain or shine.

≈8 minon foot

Kuromon Market

Osaka's kitchen. Grilled scallops and wagyu skewers. Go before noon.

≈8 minon foot

Namba Yasaka Shrine

A 12-metre lion head said to swallow bad luck. Free to visit.

Umeda

Kyoto in 29 minutes, straight from the station.

1

What it's like

Umeda is modern Osaka, built around JR Osaka Station, the city's biggest hub. Think department stores, rooftop views and underground food halls. If Kyoto, Nara or Universal Studios are on your list, this is the easiest base: direct trains leave from here. Not sure which "Umeda" station or which side? See Osaka Station vs Umeda.

2

Tennoji

30 minutes from the airport, no change.

1

What it's like

Tennoji is where old Osaka meets a 300-metre skyscraper. One side of the station has Abeno Harukas and big malls. Walk the other way and you are in Shinsekai, a retro maze of fried-skewer bars under Tsutenkaku Tower. Hotels here usually cost a little less than in Namba or Umeda.

2

What's nearby

2–3 minon foot

Abeno Harukas

A 300 m tower with an observation deck and a huge department store.

5–10 minon foot

Tennoji Zoo

One of Japan's oldest zoos. Adults pay ¥500.

≈15 minon foot

Shinsekai & Tsutenkaku

Loud signs, cheap eats and kushikatsu, the deep-fried skewers Osaka is known for.

Also worth knowing

Universal CityHotels 5 minutes from the park gate. Worth it for two park days; for one, Umeda is 15 minutes away.

One more thing: a 1920s street under a skyscraper

If you stay in Umeda, go down to the basement of the Umeda Sky Building to Takimi Koji, a food alley built to look like an Osaka street from the early Showa era, with cobblestones, tiled roofs, old posters and a small shrine. Around 20 restaurants serve okonomiyaki, kushikatsu and other Osaka staples, and you can wander it for free after the sunset from the rooftop.
