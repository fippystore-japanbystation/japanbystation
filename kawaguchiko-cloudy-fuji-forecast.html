Answer A · Flexible date

Move it, but only if the bookings let you

Decide the evening before

Look at two things the evening before: the weather forecast and the Fujisan Watcher page of the Yamanashi Tourism Organization, which streams live cameras pointed at Fuji from Lake Kawaguchiko, Fujiyoshida, Lake Shojiko, Lake Yamanakako and more. The forecast tells you about tomorrow; the cameras show you what cloud the mountain is wearing right now.

Then check what each booking lets you do. JR tickets: JR East lets you change an unused ticket once, free, and a reserved seat only before the train leaves. Highway bus: on kosokubus.com you can't move a booking to a new date; you cancel (day buses until 17:00 the day before) and book again, and the fee depends on the bus company. Hotel: on Booking.com, Toyoko Inn Fuji Kawaguchiko Ohashi showed a twin with breakfast for Wed 16 Dec at ¥14,910 with free cancellation before 15 Dec, while its ¥13,561 offer was prepaid with no changes. When Fuji is the reason for the trip, the flexible rate is the one to book.

Answer B · Keep the day trip

Go anyway: kimono art, a garden and hoto

≈¥4,200 at the lake

The bus from Shinjuku takes about 100 minutes to Kawaguchiko Station, per the museum's access page. From there, the Red Line sightseeing bus reaches the Itchiku Kubota Art Museum in about 20 minutes. It shows Kubota's dyed kimono landscapes in buildings set in a garden of lava stone and running water, so it is worth the trip on a grey day. December to March it opens 10:00–16:30 (last entry 16:00), adults ¥1,500, and its calendar shows no closed day in December 2026.

For lunch, Hoto Fudo's main store on the north shore serves the local thick noodles in miso broth, 11:00–19:00 every day or until the noodles run out, with Fudo hoto at ¥1,210 (Yamanashi's official tourism site; about 15 minutes by bus from the station). The Mt. Fuji North Area 1Day Pass (¥1,500, in the Jorudan app) covers the Red, Green and Blue sightseeing lines; museum, hoto and pass come to about ¥4,200 per person, plus the trip from Tokyo. If the cloud lifts in the afternoon, you are already on the north shore, the side that faces Fuji.

Answer C · Keep the overnight

Keep the night: two chances instead of one

2-day bus pass ¥2,000

A night at the lake turns one roll of the dice into two: this afternoon and tomorrow morning. Do Answer B on day one, then check the live cameras when you wake up. If Fuji is out, go to the lakeshore before checkout. If it isn't, you lose nothing: the hotel and the hoto were part of the plan anyway.

Two small savings make the second chance cheap. The Fujikyu bus 2DAYPASS is ¥2,000, only ¥500 more than the one-day pass, and covers the same three sightseeing lines both days. And a hotel with breakfast, like the ¥14,910 twin above, means you can be out at first light without hunting for food. If the forecast says cloud for both days, book a room with an onsen bath, so the evening is worth it even if you never see the mountain.

One more thing: matcha by the waterfall

Inside the Itchiku Kubota Art Museum, the tea room Itchiku-an serves matcha with a seasonal sweet while you look out at the "Ryumon" waterfall in the garden. The room has a floor of Ryukyu limestone and walls of Okinawan plaster, with pieces Kubota collected in India, Africa and Southeast Asia. The garden, built by Kyoto garden designer Yasuo Kitayama from Fuji lava, limestone, plants and spring water, gives you something to look at on a day when the mountain doesn't.

Quick answers

Should I cancel my Kawaguchiko day trip if the forecast is cloudy?

Only if you have a free day to move it to. Check the forecast and Yamanashi's official Fuji live cameras the evening before. An unused JR reserved ticket can be changed once for free before the train leaves, and many hotels allow free cancellation until the day before. If you have no other day, keep the trip and plan indoor stops.

Where can I see if Mount Fuji is visible right now?

The Yamanashi Tourism Organization runs a 'Fujisan Watcher' page with live cameras looking at Fuji from Lake Kawaguchiko, Fujiyoshida, Lake Shojiko, Lake Yamanakako and other points, including the view printed on the ¥1,000 note.

What can I do at Lake Kawaguchi when Fuji is hidden?

We suggest the Itchiku Kubota Art Museum (¥1,500; December to March 10:00–16:30, last entry 16:00; about 20 minutes from Kawaguchiko Station on the Red Line bus) and hoto noodles at Hoto Fudo's main store (11:00–19:00, open every day, Fudo hoto ¥1,210).

Can I change a highway bus ticket to Kawaguchiko to another day?

On kosokubus.com you cannot move a booking to another date: you cancel and book again. Day buses can be cancelled until 17:00 the day before, and the fee depends on the bus company, so check it on your booking before you buy.
