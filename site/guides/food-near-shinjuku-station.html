Food Near Shinjuku Station: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner and Late Night by Exit

Seven places within a few minutes of the gates, from a 6:20 soba counter to ramen that never closes.

BreakfastLunch & dinnerLate night

It's 6:30 in the morning, your hotel breakfast costs ¥3,000 and you have a train to catch. Walk two minutes out of the West Exit instead: Kameya has been serving soba to commuters since before the shutters went up. Below are seven places we'd send a friend, grouped by meal and by exit, each rated 4★+ on Google Maps when we checked, with hours and prices from their own listings.

Who this list suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Anyone staying near Shinjuku or changing trains here
  • Solo travellers: almost every pick has counter seats
  • Early risers and night owls: breakfast from 6:20, ramen 24 hours

Think twice

  • Card-only travellers: three picks are cash only
  • Groups of five or more: most counters seat 8–20 people in total
  • Vegetarians: only Sawamura lists vegan options officially
We suggest: soba at Kameya for breakfast, tsukemen at Fuunji for lunch, Nakamuraya's curry for a sit-down dinner, and Nagi when everything else has closed. Exits matter more than distance here: which Shinjuku station and exit you use can add ten minutes.

A breakfast that beats the hotel buffet (from 6:20)

  • Kameya (かめや), West Exit, 2 min: a soba counter with eight seats, in the Omoide Yokocho lanes. Order the ten-tama soba, tempura and a raw egg on hot noodles, for about ¥600. It opens at 06:20 and closes at 23:00, Monday to Saturday; closed Sundays. Cash only. It used to run 24 hours, but Tabelog notes that ended in June 2026, so ignore older blogs.
  • Bakery & Restaurant Sawamura, New South Exit, NEWoMan 2F: the calm option. Breakfast runs 07:00–10:00 (last order 9:30) every day, the bakery counter from 07:00 to 21:00. Lunch brings salmon sauté (about ¥2,530) with refills of the house bread. NEWoMan lists vegan options, and cards are fine.

A lunch worth the short queue, and a dinner with a story

  • Fuunji (風雲児), South Exit, 6 min: thick noodles you dip into a separate bowl of broth. Tsukemen is ¥1,000, the tokusei with all the toppings ¥1,300 (official menu). It opens 11:00–15:00 and 17:00–21:00 every day, with fifteen counter seats and a ticket machine, and closes early when the soup runs out, so go at opening. It has made Tabelog's Ramen 100 Best list every year from 2017 to 2025.
  • Manna at Shinjuku Nakamuraya, East Exit, 2 min (Marunouchi Line Exit A6): the Indian-style curry (¥2,200) has been served here since 1927, and the vegetable curry is ¥1,980. Monday to Saturday 11:00–22:00, Sundays and holidays to 21:00; closed 1 January. No reservations, so you wait in line; cards and e-money accepted. The official site says hours are currently shortened, so check before a late visit.
  • Menya Kaijin (麺屋海神), Southeast Exit, 30 seconds: salt ramen made from simmered fish bones (ara-daki shio), from ¥1,000, with an English menu and sixteen seats on the 2nd floor. From October 2026: Monday–Thursday 11:00–15:00 and 16:30–22:00, Friday to 23:00, Saturday 11:00–23:00 straight through, Sundays and holidays 11:00–22:00. Cash only.
  • Keika (桂花), East Exit, 3 min: Kumamoto-style pork-bone ramen with a slick of black garlic oil, in Shinjuku since 1968 (the company started in Kumamoto in 1955). Look for taro-men (太肉麺) with thick braised pork belly on the ticket machine. Open 10:00–23:00 every day, counter only, cash only. Walk out of the East Exit, turn in by Mizuho Bank next to Alta.

Late-night ramen when everything else is shut

Sugoi Niboshi Ramen Nagi, Golden Gai, 6 min from the East Exit (3 min from Shinjuku-sanchome Exit E2): an intensely fishy dried-sardine broth, about ¥1,000, served 24 hours a day. Ten counter seats up a steep staircase on the 2nd floor; buy your ticket at the machine first. If the stairs are full, Keika and Kameya both serve until 23:00.

📅 Good to know before you go

  • Sundays: Kameya is closed, so for an early Sunday breakfast head to Sawamura (07:00).
  • New Year: Manna is closed on 1 January. Menya Kaijin ran shorter hours over the last New Year (30 Dec and 2–3 Jan); the 2026/27 schedule isn't out yet.
  • Cash: Kameya, Menya Kaijin and Keika take no cards. Carry ¥1,000 notes for the ticket machines.
  • Soup runs out: Fuunji closes early when the broth is gone, so evening visits near 21:00 are a gamble.

One more thing: the curry that hid a revolutionary

Nakamuraya's curry has a backstory few diners hear. The Shinjuku City Tourism Association credits Rash Behari Bose, an Indian independence activist the bakery's owners sheltered, with bringing the recipe the restaurant first served in 1927. Nakamuraya still marks 12 June as its "Love and Revolution" Indian curry day. Three floors up in the same building, a small art museum shows work by the artists who gathered at the bakery's salon in the early 1900s; check its hours before you go up.
