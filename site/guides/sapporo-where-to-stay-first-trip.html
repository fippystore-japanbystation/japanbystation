First Trip to Sapporo: Sapporo Station, Odori or Susukino?

Three areas joined by one straight underground walkway. Which end you sleep at decides your mornings, your dinners and how much ice you walk on.

Sapporo StationOdoriSusukino

For most first trips we'd stay in Odori: it sits in the middle, about 10 minutes underground from Sapporo Station and one subway stop from Susukino, next to Odori Park and Nijo Market. Pick Sapporo Station if you have early trains, day trips or big luggage (the airport train is 33 minutes at the fastest). Pick Susukino if dinner and a late drink are the point of the trip.

The short version

Easy wins

  • The centre is small: Sapporo Station to Susukino is about 1.5 km
  • You can do it all underground in a blizzard
  • Airport trains need no reservation, and Suica works

Watch out for

  • JR Sapporo and the subway's Sapporo Station are about 7 minutes apart on foot
  • Ice from mid-December to February
  • Snow Festival week (4–11 Feb 2027): book early
Option A · Sapporo Station

Early trains and big bags

33 min to the airport

JR Rapid Airport trains run six an hour between 9:00 and 16:00, ¥1,230, no reservation needed for most seats (car 4 is reserved). Sleeping by JR Sapporo means no transfer with luggage, and it's the base for day trips by train, such as Otaru. Downside: evenings are calmer here, and the jingisukan grills and bars are 1.5 km south in Susukino. For breakfast, Cafe Yoshimi near the station opens at 7:30, with a morning toast set and drink for ¥580.

Option B · Odori

The middle of everything

Our pick

Chi-Ka-Ho, the underground walkway under Ekimae-dori, links Sapporo Station and Odori in about 520 m, roughly 10 minutes, and is open 5:45 to 24:30. Odori Park runs about 1.5 km east to west, with the TV Tower at its east end. Nijo Market serves seafood bowls from about 7:00, and the Tozai Line takes you four stops to the Curb Market. Downside: the airport train still means a short subway ride or that underground walk with your bags.

Option C · Susukino

Dinner, then a short walk home

3 min by subway

Sapporo's night district. From the airport it's about 58 minutes and ¥1,440 with one change to the Namboku Line. Jingisukan (grilled mutton) is the dinner to have here: Daruma's main branch, about 3 minutes from Susukino Station, is open every day from 17:00 to 5:00 and jingisukan is ¥1,280. Downside: it's one of Japan's best-known entertainment districts and busy until late, so light sleepers should ask for a high floor or stay a few blocks away.

Our verdict

We suggest: Odori for a 3–4 night first trip; you'll walk to almost everything, underground when it snows. Switch to Sapporo Station if you leave on early trains or have day trips most days. Switch to Susukino if you're here for food and nights out, and you're fine with some street noise. In winter, the closer you are to a station entrance, the less ice you walk on.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Sapporo Snow Festival, 4–11 Feb 2027: the sculptures stand in Odori Park and along Susukino's main street (Minami 4 to 7-jo), so those two areas fill first. Many listings offer free cancellation; check the policy before you pay.
  • Winter walking: Sapporo's tourism office says pavements freeze from mid-December to February. Pack boots with grip; convenience stores sell anti-slip spikes.

Nearby, if you have more time

  • Curb Market: Sapporo's central wholesale market, four stops from Odori on the Tozai Line, for a seafood breakfast.
  • Otaru: some Rapid Airport trains run straight through Sapporo to Otaru, about 73 minutes from the airport at the fastest.

One more thing: rice balls at 3 a.m.

Meidai Nigirimeshi, in a corner of Susukino Market, makes onigiri by hand and never closes. Order the soy-sauce style with cod roe and butter, the one Sapporo's tourism office recommends, with a bowl of tonjiru pork soup. It works after a late night out, and just as well before an early train.
