Kamakura From Noon: Temples, Sunset or Both?

Hase's temples close around 16:30. In winter, that's also when the sun sets. Hachimangu stays open until 20:00. Plan in that order.

Three plansOur noon routeLunch & dinner

You can see Kamakura properly from a noon start if you stay in one area and follow the closing times. Take the 11:36 from Tokyo (Kamakura at 12:33, no change), do the Great Buddha and Hasedera first, because both stop letting people in around 16:30–16:45, then ride the Enoden to Inamuragasaki for sunset. Finish at Tsurugaoka Hachimangu, which keeps its gates open until 20:00. Trying to fit in Kita-Kamakura's temples as well is what turns a relaxed afternoon into a rush.

About a 4-minute read · facts checked 24 November 2026

Who a noon start suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • A slow morning in Tokyo, then one well-paced afternoon
  • First visits: the Great Buddha, Hasedera and the coast are all on one short train line
  • Couples who'd rather watch a sunset than tick off six temples
  • Winter trips: the light and the closing times line up neatly

Think twice

  • You want Kita-Kamakura's Zen temples and Hase: that needs a morning start
  • Weekend lunch at the busiest places: expect queues on the Enoden and at Hase
  • You need step-free routes everywhere: Hasedera's upper terrace and the cape path have slopes and steps

Getting there and around

From Tokyo Station, the JR Yokosuka Line runs straight to Kamakura: the 11:36 arrives at 12:33 (57 minutes, ¥1,040 ticket or ¥1,034 by IC card, on our weekday check). From Shinjuku, the Shonan-Shinjuku Line with a change at Ofuna takes about 54 minutes. A JR Pass covers both.

In Kamakura, the little green Enoden does the rest. It's ¥200 from Kamakura to Hase, ¥200 from Hase to Inamuragasaki and ¥220 from Inamuragasaki back to Kamakura. Tap your IC card and you don't need to think about tickets.

Three ways to spend the afternoon

A · Temples first

Hase · arrive by 15:30

Great Buddha + Hasedera

Pick Hase and give it time. The Great Buddha (¥300) lets people in until 16:45 from October to March and 17:15 from April to September. Hasedera (¥400) takes last entry at 16:30 from July to March and 17:00 from April to June. They're 5 minutes apart on foot, 7 minutes from Hase Station. Get there by 15:30 at the latest to enjoy both.

B · Coast first

Sunset · ~16:30 in winter

Beach, cape, dinner by the sea

Walk Yuigahama, ride the Enoden to Inamuragasaki for sunset, then go one stop on to Shichirigahama for dinner at bills (dinner from 17:00, open to 21:00, bookable online). Back to Kamakura or on to Fujisawa by Enoden.

C · One of each

Our pick

Hase, then the cape, then Hachimangu

Temples while they're open, sunset when they close, and Tsurugaoka Hachimangu after dark, since its gates stay open until 20:00. Every move is one Enoden ride or a short walk.

Our verdict: from a noon start, do C. In winter the sun sets around 16:30, just as Hase closes, so the day flows on its own. The answer changes in summer: in late June the sun sets around 19:00, so you have time for B's beach walk too, and dinner by the sea makes sense. On a grey or rainy day, skip the sunset and do A slowly, then Hachimangu and an early dinner.

Our noon route (winter timings)

  1. 11:36 Yokosuka Line from Tokyo Station, no change.
  2. 12:33 Kamakura. Change to the Enoden for Hase (about 7 minutes).
  3. 13:00 Walk 7 minutes to the Great Buddha. Add ¥50 to step inside the statue (until 16:30).
  4. 13:45 Walk 5 minutes to Hasedera. Late lunch at Kaikoan, the temple's own restaurant, looking over Yuigahama (open until 16:00).
  5. 15:45 Enoden two stops to Inamuragasaki, then an 8-minute walk to the cape.
  6. 16:30 Sunset over Shichirigahama and Enoshima.
  7. 17:15 Enoden back to Kamakura, then 10 minutes on foot to Tsurugaoka Hachimangu (open until 20:00), and dinner near the station.

Lunch and dinner on this route

  • Kaikoan (inside Hasedera): the temple's restaurant has wide windows over Yuigahama beach and serves vegetarian curry, vegan pasta and desserts. It's open 10:00 to 16:00, so it works as a late lunch. You need a Hasedera ticket to get in.
  • bills Shichirigahama: one Enoden stop past Inamuragasaki, open every day 7:00 to 21:00, with the dinner menu from 17:00 and terrace seats above the sea. Book on the official site if you want a table straight after sunset.
  • Around Kamakura Station: after Hachimangu, the streets between the shrine and the station are the easiest place to find dinner without another train ride.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Closing times change with the season: Kotoku-in closes at 17:30 from April to September and 17:00 from October to March. Hasedera's later hours run only April to June. Spring and early summer give you the longest afternoons.
  • Mount Fuji from the cape: the government's "Kanto Fujimi 100" list notes Fuji shows best from Inamuragasaki on windy days from early December to late February. That's the season when a noon start pays off most.
  • A night by the sea: if you want the 8:00 Great Buddha without the crowds, stay over. Most hotels on the big booking sites offer free cancellation, so you can hold a room and decide once you've seen the forecast.

Nearby, if you have more time

  • Zeniarai Benten: the shrine where people wash coins in spring water, about 30 minutes on foot from the Great Buddha.
  • Daibutsu hiking trail: the Daibutsu-zaka entrance is about 10 minutes from Kotoku-in, a woodland ridge walk if you start earlier.
  • Enoshima: stay on the Enoden past Shichirigahama for the island itself. Travelling with someone who dislikes stairs? See Enoshima without the stairs.

One more thing: save the big shrine for last

Most day-trippers start at Tsurugaoka Hachimangu because it's closest to the station, then rush to Hase before it closes. Do it the other way round. The temples in Hase stop admitting people around 16:30, but Hachimangu's gates are open from 6:00 to 20:00 every day of the year, and it's free. Walk up the main steps after sunset, when most of the afternoon crowd is on the train home, and you have Kamakura's biggest shrine at a quieter hour.
