Coin Lockers at Shinjuku Station: Prices, Where the Big Ones Are, What If They're Full

The world's busiest station has hundreds of suitcase lockers. Here's which ones are still free at 11am.

Sizes and pricesWhere the big ones areIf they're full

Forty-two large lockers sit inside the gates on the passage to Platforms 5 and 6, where the Narita Express stops, and JR East itself calls that spot less crowded. That's our first stop with a big case at Shinjuku. A large locker costs ¥900–1,000 for the day, you can pay with Suica, and if every door is shut, a staffed counter or same-day hotel delivery takes over.

Who this page suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Arriving on the Narita Express before hotel check-in

Think twice

  • Two large cases each: one large locker fits about one big suitcase
  • More than three days: lockers aren't for long storage
  • Weekend late mornings: large lockers can be gone by then
We suggest: drop a big case in the large lockers on the B1 passage to Platforms 5–6 before you leave the gates (if you'll come back for a train), the East Exit or New South Gate banks if you won't, or book a Multi ecube locker the day before (¥500 extra). All JR East sizes are about 34 cm wide and 57 cm deep; heights from JR East's locker size sheet. The lower prices are the Multi ecube touchscreen lockers, which can cost ¥100–200 more for M and L in busy seasons.

Paying: the Multi ecube touchscreens take Suica or PASMO, QR codes and credit cards, in English, Chinese and Korean, and the same card opens the door later. Some older lockers still want ¥100 coins. Lockers open from the first train to the last.

How long: lockers usually allow up to three days, counted by calendar date. After that, items are usually removed and held, and getting them back costs the locker fee plus a key fee.

Where the big lockers are, by where you're going

  • Arriving on the Narita Express? Stay inside the gates and use the B1 passage to Platforms 5–6: 42 large, 12 medium and 22 small lockers, and JR East's pick for a less crowded spot.
  • Leaving from the South Gate or heading to the Odakyu side? The 2F South Gate area inside the gates is the biggest bank: 76 large and 85 medium lockers.
  • Taking a highway bus from Busta Shinjuku? Outside the New South Gate, by the Suica Penguin Plaza, there are 41 large lockers. Inside the same gate, by the escalator to Platforms 13–14, there are 22 more.
  • Hotel on the East side, Kabukicho or Shinjuku-sanchome? The 1F East Exit, outside the gates where the Hato Bus office used to be, has 64 large lockers.
  • Hotel in West Shinjuku? On the Toei Oedo Line, Shinjuku-nishiguchi has a small bank on B1 near Exit D2, including 7 large lockers that take PASMO.

All full? Plan B, C and D

  1. Book Reserve tomorrow's locker tonight. The Multi ecube site shows availability by size and takes bookings up to a month ahead for ¥500 per locker. JR East warns the screen isn't always exact.
  2. Hand over Use a staffed counter. ecbo cloak shops store a suitcase for about ¥800 a day, or ¥1,000 at JR station locations. It charges per business day, so a bag left from 17:00 to 10:00 the next morning pays for two days.
  3. Send it Let the case go to the hotel. JR East's Multi ecube delivery picks up from lockers inside the East Gate and at the East Exit. Book and pay by 9:30 and drop off by 11:00, and the case reaches hotels in Tokyo's 23 wards and Urayasu from about 18:00. It cost ¥3,100–3,500 per item in JR East's 2024 price list.

📅 Good to know before you go

  • Late morning: JR East says the large lockers can fill up by late morning, so drop the case early or book one.
  • Booking fee: a reserved Multi ecube locker costs ¥500 on top of the normal price.
  • Delivery cutoffs: book by 9:30 and drop off by 11:00 for same-day hotel delivery.
  • Night buses: lockers close with the last train, so collect your case before you head out for dinner.

One more thing: drop the case before you leave the gates

Most travellers walk out of the Narita Express gates first and then start hunting for a locker among the crowds. Instead, turn into the B1 passage to Platforms 5–6 while you're still inside: that's where the 42 large lockers are. The catch: this bank is inside the ticket gates, so it suits you best when you'll come back to Shinjuku to catch a train, such as the Narita Express home. Then you collect the case and walk straight to the platform.
