Asakusa, Tokyo · Getting there

Which "Asakusa Station" Will You Actually Use from Narita?

Asakusa has four stations with the same name. Your airport train only stops at one of them, and it is not the Tsukuba Express.

ArrivalThe luggage walkThree nights

From Narita you arrive at the Toei Asakusa Line station: Keisei's Access Express runs straight through, 70 minutes for ¥1,380 with no change. A hotel advertised "3 minutes from Asakusa Station" may mean the Tsukuba Express station instead, which no Narita train reaches directly. For the TX-side hotel we traced, that is about 670 m with your bags on day one, then a genuinely handy base for the next two mornings.

Answer A · Match your airport train to its station

The Access Express ends up at Toei Asakusa

70 min · ¥1,380 · no change

Our sample Access Express left Narita Terminal 1 at 15:09. At Oshiage it simply continues onto the Toei Asakusa Line (you stay seated) and reaches Asakusa at 16:19. That is the cheapest easy way in, and it puts you at the Toei station near Azuma-bashi bridge and Kaminarimon-dori.

The other airport routes land at different Asakusas. The Skyliner to Keisei-Ueno plus the Ginza Line reaches the Ginza Line's Asakusa station (16:24, ¥2,760); the Skyliner with a change at Aoto reaches the Toei station faster (16:06, ¥2,430). Reaching the Tsukuba Express Asakusa station took two changes on our search, Skyliner, JR Yamanote Line and TX via Akihabara: 16:30 and ¥3,010.

Answer B · The "cheaper" hotel near the Tsukuba Express

From the airport train to a TX-side hotel

≈670 m with bags

Hotel Trend Asakusa Annex lists itself as 3 minutes from the Tsukuba Express Asakusa station and 5 minutes from the Ginza Line. From TX exit A1 the street walk is only about 240 m. But you will not be coming out of TX exit A1: from the Toei station's step-free exit A2b the walk is about 670 m, roughly 9 minutes at a normal pace, before you add suitcases.

And on our dates it was not the cheaper choice. Three nights for 2 adults, 14–17 Dec 2026: Hotel Trend Asakusa Annex was ¥37,500 for a double; SORA Kaminarimon, about 250 m from exit A2b, was ¥36,845, both including taxes. The Ginza Line's Exit 1, which has a lift, is about 460 m from the Annex and about 100 m from SORA Kaminarimon.

Answer C · One arrival walk vs the next two mornings

Where the TX-side hotel earns its keep

Akihabara 5 min · Shibuya 35 min

Morning 2, Akihabara: from the TX station next door, our sample train left at 08:31 and reached Akihabara at 08:36, ¥230, with no change.

Morning 3, Shibuya: walk about 420 m to Tawaramachi on the Ginza Line and ride straight through: 08:31 to 09:06, ¥260. From the main Asakusa Ginza Line station it is 37 minutes, so the TX-side hotel loses nothing here.

Departure day: back to the Toei station for the Access Express. Our sample left Asakusa at 09:21 and reached Narita Terminal 1 at 10:32, ¥1,380, again with no change. That is the same 670 m walk, so the luggage penalty is paid twice.

Our verdict

We suggest: from Narita, plan on the Access Express to the Toei Asakusa station and book within about 300 m of it (Kaminarimon or Azuma-bashi side) when you carry big bags, because the TX-side hotel adds about 670 m on both arrival and departure day and, on our dates, saved nothing. The answer changes when you will use the Tsukuba Express most mornings (Akihabara, Tsukuba) and travel light, or when the TX-side room is clearly cheaper on your own dates.

One more thing: a drink where Asakusa started drinking

Come up at Ginza line Exit 3 (or Toei Asakusa line Exit A5) and the first door you pass is Kamiya Bar, open since 1880 and known as Japan's first Western-style bar. Order its own Denki Bran, a sweet brandy-based mix created here, with a draft beer on the side the way regulars do. It is loud, cheap and full of locals at the long tables, which is exactly the point after a long flight.
