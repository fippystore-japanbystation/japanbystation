Gora to Lake Ashi: Bus or Ropeway?

The ropeway is the view. The bus is the shortcut most visitors never take. Here's when to use each.

Bus or ropewayLunchOur route

Ride the Hakone Ropeway once, for the views over Owakudani's steam vents, and use the bus for everything else. From Gora Station, two short Tozan bus rides reach Moto-Hakone on Lake Ashi in 20 to 30 minutes for ¥910, a short walk from Hakone Shrine's lakeside torii, and the Freepass covers them. Most visitors only know the cable car, ropeway and boat loop; knowing the bus lets you do the views one way and come back fast.

Who the ropeway suits, and who should just take the bus

Ride the ropeway if

  • It's your first Hakone visit and you want Owakudani's steam and the high views
  • You have a Freepass: the ropeway and boat are already paid for
  • The day is clear and calm: the views are the whole point

Take the bus if

  • You mainly want the lake, Hakone Shrine and lunch by the water
  • Time is short: the bus goes straight to Moto-Hakone
  • It's windy or misty, or the ropeway is closed for maintenance

Three ways from Gora to Lake Ashi

A · The ropeway loop

Views · ¥2,000 ropeway

Cable car, ropeway, boat

Cable car up to Sounzan, then the ropeway over Owakudani to Togendai, about 30 minutes end to end with gondolas roughly every minute (¥2,000 one way, covered by the Freepass), then the sightseeing boat across the lake to Moto-Hakone. Allow most of a morning with stops. The ropeway runs until 16:45, or 16:15 in December and January.

B · The bus

20–30 min · ¥910

Gora to Moto-Hakone

The "S" sightseeing-facility bus from Gora to Kowakien (about 4 minutes, ¥250), then the H-line bus to Moto-Hakone-ko (about 14 minutes, ¥660). From Hakone-Yumoto the direct bus from stop 2 takes about 30 minutes for ¥1,210 and runs about every 15 minutes in the daytime. Heading for Togendai instead? Gora to Togendai by bus is about 55 minutes, ¥880.

C · Mix the two

Our pick

Views out, bus back

Ropeway and boat out in the morning, then the H-line bus from Moto-Hakone back down to Yumoto or up towards Gora. You get Owakudani and the lake without sitting through the slow loop twice.

Our verdict: on a first visit with a Freepass, do C: ropeway out, bus back. The answer changes on a windy or misty day, or when the ropeway is shut for maintenance: then take the bus both ways (B) and spend the saved time at the shrine or in an onsen. If you've ridden the ropeway before, the bus alone is the smarter way to the lake.

Eat like a local: lunch at either end

  • Bakery & Table Hakone (Moto-Hakone): right by the Moto-Hakone-ko bus stop, open every day, with a bakery downstairs and a café with lake views upstairs (last café order 16:30). GOOD LUCK TRIP lists the rice-flour curry doughnut as the best-seller. Cards accepted.
  • Tamura Ginkatsutei (Gora): about 2 minutes from Gora Station, and where tofu katsuni, a tofu cutlet simmered with egg, originated. Lunch last order 14:30; closed Wednesdays and Tuesday evenings. Cards accepted.
  • Hakone Gyoza Center (Gora): small and popular for gyoza sets at lunch, last order 14:30. Closed Thursdays and Wednesday afternoons, cash only. A reviewer on its listing suggests writing your name in the notebook by the door.

Our half-day route: views out, bus back

  1. 9:30 From Gora, cable car up to Sounzan and check Hakone Navi's live status once more.
  2. 10:00 Ropeway over Owakudani to Togendai (about 30 minutes), with a stop for the steam vents.
  3. 11:00 Boat across Lake Ashi to Moto-Hakone.
  4. 11:45 Walk about 10 minutes to Hakone Shrine (free) and its lakeside torii.
  5. 12:45 Lunch and a lake view at Bakery & Table, by the bus stop.
  6. 14:00 H-line bus down to Hakone-Yumoto (about 30 minutes) for a bath before the train home.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Planned ropeway maintenance: Owakudani–Sounzan is shut 7–24 December 2026 and Togendai–Owakudani 12 January–26 February 2027 (replacement buses for ticket holders). If the gondola matters, book outside those dates.
  • Wind can stop the ropeway and boats on any day of the year: check Hakone Navi's Transportation Info page before you leave Tokyo, and switch to the bus if they're down.
  • Most Hakone hotels on booking sites show a free-cancellation date: useful when the forecast turns.

One more thing: a ropeway you couldn't ride is money back

Wind stops the ropeway and the lake boats more often than most visitors expect. If that happens on your Freepass days and you never rode it, the unused part is refunded at the station where you finish: on the digital Freepass that's ¥1,640 for an adult 2-day pass, ¥820 if only one section was down, and ¥1,180 more if the boat didn't run either. Ask before you go through the gate home.
