Kyoto Station to Arashiyama: 11 Minutes by JR, or the Slower Way That Drops You at Tenryu-ji

Three ways from Kyoto Station, three different Arashiyama stations, and the order that saves you a crowded walk.

JRSubway + RandenBus

Take the JR Sagano Line. From Kyoto Station it's 11 minutes to Saga-Arashiyama on a rapid train, ¥240, no change, and the bamboo grove is about a 10-minute walk from the exit. The bus costs ¥10 less and takes about four times as long. The one good reason to go slower is the subway plus the little Randen tram: about 45 minutes, but calmer, and it stops on Arashiyama's main street, five minutes from Tenryu-ji.

Which way suits you

JR Sagano Line suits

  • Anyone starting at Kyoto Station who wants the bamboo grove early
  • JR Pass holders
  • Half-day trips: 11 minutes each way

Think twice if

  • You hate standing in a packed train: the Sagano Line is fast but crowded
  • Your hotel is in Gion or Kawaramachi: Hankyu is closer
  • You want to start at Tenryu-ji or Togetsukyo: Randen lands you there
We suggest: JR there, Randen back. Arrive at Saga-Arashiyama, walk the bamboo grove and Tenryu-ji, then leave from Randen Arashiyama on the main street. It's the order Kyoto's own guides recommend, and you never walk back the way you came. Staying near the station? Our Kyoto Station exit guide shows where the JR side is.
Way A · Fastest

JR Sagano Line: 11 minutes, ¥240

to Saga-Arashiyama

The Sagano Line (also called the San-in Line) leaves from the far west end of Kyoto Station; our sample train went from platform 32. Kyoto's tourism site warns that this platform is further away than the other lines, so allow a few extra minutes. Rapid trains reach Saga-Arashiyama in about 11 minutes and locals stop there too.

Where to stand: the front cars fill up first, so the city's own advice is to board from the back of the platform. From Saga-Arashiyama it's about 10 minutes on foot to the bamboo grove (JNTO), about 8 minutes to Togetsukyo Bridge, and about 13 to Tenryu-ji.

Way B · Calmer, lands in the middle

Subway + Randen tram: about 45 minutes, ¥510

to Randen Arashiyama

Kyoto's tourism office suggests this route to spread out the crowds. Take the Karasuma subway line three stops to Karasuma Oike, change one floor down to the Tozai Line, and ride to the end at Uzumasa Tenjingawa: about 20 minutes, ¥260. Go up Exit 3 to Randen-Tenjingawa, in the middle of Sanjo-dori, and take the one-carriage Randen tram to the end at Arashiyama: about 15 minutes, ¥250.

Why bother: Randen Arashiyama sits on the main street, about 5 minutes from Tenryu-ji and 4 from Togetsukyo Bridge. If you'll ride both again the same day, the Kyoto Subway & Randen 1-Day Ticket costs ¥1,300.

Way C · Only if it passes your hotel

City bus 28: about 47 minutes, ¥230

from stop C6

Bus 28 leaves Kyoto Station's bus terminal from stop C6 and runs to Arashiyama Tenryuji-mae in about 47 minutes on our sample search. It saves ¥10 on the train and costs you more than half an hour. Kyoto's tourism site says plainly that the bus from Kyoto Station is crowded and time-consuming, and that bus 11 along Shijo-dori from Kawaramachi tends to be stuck in traffic.

Coming from Gion or Kawaramachi? Skip the bus and take Hankyu from Kyoto-kawaramachi: about 32 minutes, ¥240, changing at Katsura to the short Arashiyama line. Hankyu Arashiyama is across the river: walk over Togetsukyo Bridge into town, about 15 minutes to Tenryu-ji.

A half-day that doesn't double back

  1. 8:15 JR Sagano Line from Kyoto Station, 11 minutes.
  2. 8:30 Walk to the bamboo grove (about 10 minutes); it's open at any hour.
  3. 9:00 Tenryu-ji's garden, from the north gate side. Open from 8:30, garden ¥500.
  4. 10:30 Out onto the main street and down to Togetsukyo Bridge.
  5. 11:30 Randen from Arashiyama Station back towards the city, or Hankyu from across the bridge if you're heading to Kawaramachi.

📅 Good to know before you go

  • Tenryu-ji opens 8:30–17:00 (last entry 16:50); the halls are occasionally closed for ceremonies.
  • Sagano Romantic Train: same-day tickets go on sale from about 8:35 at Torokko Saga, first come, first served; online booking takes up to 8 people.
  • Most Kyoto hotels offer free cancellation on many rooms, so you can hold one and decide later.

One more thing: a hot foot bath on the tram platform

If you leave by Randen, arrive a little early. Randen's Arashiyama Station has a hot-spring foot bath right on the platform area, fed by Arashiyama Onsen. It costs ¥300 with a towel included, and it's open 9:30–18:00 (last entry 17:30; shorter hours in winter). After a morning on foot, ten minutes there before the tram is the best ¥300 in Arashiyama.
