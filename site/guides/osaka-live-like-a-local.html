Our pick · Tenma and Tenjinbashisuji

Tenma

1 stop from Osaka Station

Tenma sits around JR Tenma Station and Ogimachi, north of the city centre. Japan's longest covered shopping street runs straight through it, and the back alleys north of the station, known as Ura-Tenma, are packed with tiny izakaya under hundreds of lanterns. At night it is lively, but it is local-lively: office workers and neighbours, not tour groups.

For food lovers · Tsuruhashi

Tsuruhashi

direct to Namba and Nara

Osaka's Korean neighbourhood, famous for yakiniku. The market lanes start right outside the station, and Miyuki-dori Koreatown is a 10 to 15 minute walk south. Three lines meet here, which makes it a smart base if Nara is on your list.

One more thing: the free ferries locals commute on

Osaka still runs eight free city ferries across its rivers, and people use them to get to work, school and the shops. Ride one, for example the Senbonmatsu ferry in Taisho, which leaves about every 15 minutes, and you are standing among bikes and commuters, not tourists. The ride takes a few minutes, costs nothing, and shows you the water-city side of Osaka that most visitors never see.

Osaka City ferries: 8 routes, free for pedestrians and bicycles; most run from early morning to about 19:00–21:30, no service on 1 January, and they stop in bad weather. Sources: Osaka City Construction Bureau, Taisho Ward Office.
