Kyoto Station Food Halls: The Classic Gift vs What Kyoto Buys

Three department-store basements, five pairs: the box everyone carries home, and the one Kyoto people queue for.

Classic vs localThe three storesBefore the train

If you only have half an hour before the Shinkansen, go down to JR Kyoto Isetan's B1. It's inside the station, and you'll find the famous yatsuhashi makers next to Kyoto sweet shops whose cakes only arrive on certain days. Go on a Saturday after 11:00 and you can carry home what Kyoto people actually line up for.

Five pairs: the classic, and the local pick

1. Yatsuhashi vs Saturday ohagi

Classic: baked yatsuhashi, the crisp cinnamon cracker shaped like a koto harp. All three big makers, Izutsu, Shogoin and Honke Nishio, have counters on JR Kyoto Isetan B1. At Daimaru, Shogoin's box of 48 is ¥1,296. Local: Isetan's B1 lists set days for famous Kyoto shops. Imanishiken's ohagi (rice cakes wrapped in red-bean paste, made with the shop's own lightly sweet bean paste) come in on Saturdays from 11:00, ¥721 for three (store list, November 2026).

2. Matcha everything vs a yokan only Kyoto sells

Classic: matcha sweets. Local: Tsuruya Yoshinobu's Kyoto-only yokan, launched in February 2026. Three bars cost ¥1,274 and five cost ¥2,041. It keeps for about a year and is sold only in Kyoto, including at all three department stores (brand release). It's a sturdy gift for a long flight.

3. Pickles: the famous names

Kyoto tsukemono (pickles) is a whole counter row of its own. Isetan B1 has Kawakatsu, Doi Shibazuke and Daiyasu. Daimaru carries Doi, Nishiri and Daiyasu. At Takashimaya B1, Murakami Juhonten's shibazuke (aubergine pickled purple with red shiso) is ¥600 for 130 g, an easy one to try.

4. Station bento vs a Kyoto restaurant's box

Classic: any ekiben. Local: Isetan B2 carries bento from about 27 long-established restaurant brands, among them Hyotei and Nakamura-ro. Izuu, the Gion saba-zushi house, has a counter here too. A whole roll of its mackerel sushi is ¥4,860 at the main shop. Some say it tastes richer half a day later, which suits a train ride.

5. Ajari-mochi vs the 21st-of-the-month dorayaki

Classic: Mangetsu's ajari-mochi, a chewy bean-paste cake, ¥141 each at Daimaru or ten for ¥1,523 at Takashimaya. Local: Sasaya Iori's dorayaki (¥1,944) is sold at Daimaru Kyoto only on the 20th to 22nd of each month, around Toji's monthly Kobo-san market.

Daimaru and Takashimaya are downtown, near Nishiki Market, and easy to fit into a sightseeing day. Isetan is the one for your last stop before the train.

Ten minutes before the train

Out of time? On the station's Hachijo side, ASTY Square (2F) is right in front of the Shinkansen central gate. It has 551 Horai, Kyoto dango and a deli-and-gift shop open 7:00–21:00. In Kyoto Porta, the underground mall, Kyo-komachi sits next to the JR central gate and opens 8:30–20:00. There are bento and souvenir shops inside the Shinkansen gates too.

📅 Good to know before you shop

  • Tax-free works differently since 1 November 2026. You pay tax first and get it back after customs checks the goods at departure (minimum ¥5,000 before tax per store per day). Takashimaya Kyoto registers purchases at its 7F counter the same day and deducts a fee of 23% of the tax. Food eaten in Japan doesn't count.
  • Set days can move. Isetan warns that arrival days change and some items sell out. Demachi Futaba's mame-mochi (2 for ¥560) gives priority to reservations.
  • Fresh vs baked. Mochi and ohagi are best the same day. Choose baked yatsuhashi or yokan if the box has to survive a week of travel.

One more thing: plan your day around the mochi calendar

Isetan's B1 runs a weekly calendar of Kyoto mochi. Nakamuraken's mugite-mochi (freshly pounded rice cake with kinako to sprinkle on, 3 for ¥1,053) comes in on Thursdays from 11:00 and Sundays from 10:00. Mitamaya's black-syrup dango arrive Thursday to Sunday. Fuka's fu-manju, wrapped in a bamboo leaf, comes six days a week. Time your last station visit to the right day and you'll eat what Kyoto keeps for itself.
