Where to Stay in Osaka If You're Out Late

Osaka's subway stops around midnight. So sleep somewhere you can walk home from.

NambaTenmaLast trains

Planning a few late nights in Osaka? Here's the catch: the city stays up late, but its trains don't. Most subway lines stop around midnight, and a taxi across town adds up fast. So we'd choose the hotel by where you'll be at 1am, not at noon. For most people that means Namba. Want a more local night? Try Tenma.

Namba

walk home from everything

This is where Osaka's nights happen. Stay a few minutes from Namba Station and you can walk to all of it: the canal, the back streets and the late food. No need to watch the clock.

For a local night · North side

Tenma and Umeda

izakaya alleys

Rather drink with office workers than tourists? Stay around JR Tenma. The Ura-Tenma alleys start just north of the station, under hundreds of lanterns. And Umeda is only one stop away, with more izakaya in the basements of the Osaka Ekimae Buildings.

If you do stay elsewhere

Check the last train first

Staying somewhere else? Check your last train before the first drink. Most Osaka Metro lines stop around midnight, some outer stations earlier. Every station posts the time.

Know your way back

Missed it? Taxis wait outside Namba and Umeda. Save your hotel's address in Japanese on your phone, so you can just show the driver.

One more thing: if you miss the last train anyway

Missed the last train anyway? Osaka has a fun plan B: Spa World in Shinsekai. It's a huge hot-spring complex, themed on bathing styles from around the world, and it's open around the clock. Pay the overnight extra, have a long soak, and sleep in the rest areas until the first train. The men's and women's floors swap every month, so you see a different "country" on your next visit.

Spa World, 3-4-24 Ebisu-higashi, Naniwa-ku; about 1 min from Dobutsuen-mae Station Exit 5 (Sakaisuji / Midosuji lines) or JR Shin-Imamiya. 24 hours, with an extra charge overnight; check current fees and the tattoo policy on the official site. Sources: Headout, Expedia, osaka-info.jp.
