Where to Stay in Osaka If You're Out Late

The subway stops around midnight. The fix is simple: sleep where you can walk home.

NambaTenmaLast trains

Osaka stays up late, but its trains don't. Most subway lines stop around midnight, and a taxi across the city adds up fast. If late dinners and bar hopping are part of the plan, we suggest picking your hotel by where you'll be at 1am, not where you'll be at noon.

Namba

walk home from everything

The south side of Osaka is where the night happens. Stay within a few minutes of Namba Station and the canal, the side streets and the late food are all on foot.

For a local night · North side

Tenma and Umeda

izakaya alleys

If you would rather drink with office workers than tourists, stay around JR Tenma. The Ura-Tenma alleys sit just north of the station, under hundreds of lanterns. Umeda, one stop away, has the izakaya basements of the Osaka Ekimae Buildings.

If you do stay elsewhere

Check the last train first

Most Osaka Metro lines stop around midnight, and some outer stations earlier. The time is posted at every station.

Know your way back

Missed it? Taxis queue outside Namba and Umeda. Save your hotel's address in Japanese on your phone to show the driver.

One more thing: if you miss the last train anyway

If the night runs past the last subway, there's a very Osaka plan B: Spa World in Shinsekai, a huge hot-spring complex themed on bathing cultures from around the world, which is open around the clock. Pay the overnight surcharge, soak, and sleep in the rest areas until the first train. The men's and women's floors swap every month, so you see a different "country" on your next visit.

Spa World, 3-4-24 Ebisu-higashi, Naniwa-ku; about 1 min from Dobutsuen-mae Station Exit 5 (Sakaisuji / Midosuji lines) or JR Shin-Imamiya. 24 hours, with an extra charge overnight; check current fees and the tattoo policy on the official site. Sources: Headout, Expedia, osaka-info.jp.
