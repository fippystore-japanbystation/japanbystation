Naha First Trip: Where to Stay and Do You Need a Car?

One monorail line does most of the work in Naha. The car question only starts when you head north.

Three basesThe car questionEat

For most first trips we'd stay near Kokusai-dori, between Prefectural Office and Makishi stations, and skip the car while you're in Naha. The Yui Rail monorail gets you from the airport in 13 to 16 minutes (¥290–¥320) and on to Shuri Castle in 11 more. Rent a car only for the days you head north. Churaumi Aquarium is about two hours by car from the airport, against about three by express bus.

Who a car-free Naha base suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Short trips of 2–4 nights centred on Naha, Shuri and the markets
  • Travellers who want a drink with dinner: Japan's drink-driving rules are very strict
  • Anyone arriving late: the monorail starts right at the airport terminal
  • Rainy days: the market streets off Kokusai-dori are covered

Think twice

  • Your trip is mostly beaches and the north: base yourself up there with a car
  • Families with several suitcases changing hotels often: a car is easier
  • Light sleepers right on Kokusai-dori: pick a side street

Getting around: the monorail

The Yui Rail runs from Naha Airport through the city to Shuri and on to Tedako-Uranishi. Single fares start at ¥250. From the airport it's 11 minutes to Asahibashi (¥290), 13 to Prefectural Office (¥290), 16 to Makishi (¥320), 19 to Omoromachi (¥320) and 27 to Shuri (¥360).

Tap in with Suica, ICOCA, PASMO or any of Japan's national IC cards. You can top them up at Yui Rail stations, but you can't buy a Suica there. The 1-day ticket costs ¥1,000 and the 2-day ¥1,800. Both count 24 or 48 hours from purchase, not calendar days.

Pass maths: airport to Makishi (¥320) plus a Shuri round trip from Makishi (¥290 each way) comes to ¥900, so a 1-day ticket only pays off once you add a fourth ride, such as Omoromachi (¥250). Because it runs for 24 hours, buy it on arrival in the afternoon and it still covers the next morning.

Walk to dinner and the market

Do you need a car?

In Naha: no. The monorail covers the airport, Kokusai-dori, Omoromachi and Shuri. For the north: usually yes. The official Churaumi Aquarium site puts it at about two hours from Naha Airport by car (via the Okinawa Expressway) and about three hours by express bus. Beaches and capes further up the island are slower still without a car.

The neat answer for most trips is to split it: spend your Naha nights car-free, then pick up a rental for the days you go north, or stay up there a night or two and keep the car.

Eat like a local: the Makishi Public Market

Turn off Kokusai-dori halfway along and follow the covered market street to the Makishi Public Market, Naha's kitchen since 1950, rebuilt on the same spot and reopened in March 2023. It's about 10 minutes on foot from Makishi or Miebashi station, open roughly 8:00 to 22:00 (hours vary by shop).

  • Downstairs: 75 shops selling fish from local waters, Okinawan pork and beef, island vegetables, sea grapes and shima rakkyo (small island shallots).
  • Upstairs: 12 eateries serving Okinawan dishes such as soki soba, champuru and rafute (slow-braised pork belly), plus sata andagi doughnuts. The city notes the upstairs restaurants stay open fairly late, so it works for dinner too.
  • Ask for "shiibun": the Okinawan word for the little extra a stallholder sometimes adds to your bag.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Shuri Castle has just reopened: the interior of the rebuilt main hall was due to reopen on 23 November 2026, seven years after the 2019 fire. Expect it to be busy for a while; the halls around it are still being rebuilt.
  • Sundays on Kokusai-dori: about 1.3 km of the street becomes a car-free "transit mall" from 12:00 to 18:00 (cancelled in rain). If your hotel is on the street itself, arrive by monorail and walk, or check in before noon.
  • Car days: if you're renting for the north, book the hotel and the car for the same dates. Most hotels on the big booking sites offer free cancellation, so you can firm up the plan later.

Nearby, if you have more time

  • Shuri Castle: Shuri Station, 11 minutes from Makishi (¥290). The grounds outside the paid area are free to walk.
  • Naminoue Shrine: Naha's seaside shrine, on the coast west of Kokusai-dori.
  • The north by car: Churaumi Aquarium is about two hours' drive; make it a night up there rather than a rushed round trip.

One more thing: have your market fish cooked upstairs

The market's best trick is "mochiage", running since 1990. Buy fish, lobster or meat at a stall on the ground floor, and one of the eateries upstairs will cook it for you as sashimi, grilled or as a steak. You pay for the ingredients at the stall and a cooking fee at the restaurant. Ask the stallholder which upstairs place suits what you bought. They'll point you to the right counter, and many people add a drink or a bowl of noodles while they wait.
