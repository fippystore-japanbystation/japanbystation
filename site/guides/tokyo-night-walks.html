Tokyo After Dark: 4 Night Walks (With Maps)

Four short routes in four big areas, each with its map, its stops in order and somewhere good to eat on the way.

AsakusaShinjukuShibuya

Pick Shinjuku for a free view of the city lights from 202 m, and Asakusa for lit gates and quiet temple paths. Shibuya is for feeling the crowd at the crossing, and Roppongi is the quick one this December: a free Christmas market and a street of lit trees side by side. All four start free, none needs more than about 30 minutes of walking, and each ends near dinner.

Who these walks suit, and who they don't

Good fit

  • A first evening in Tokyo, when you want the city to feel like Tokyo
  • Photographers, and anyone happy to walk slowly
  • Travellers who'd rather spend on dinner than on tickets

Think twice

  • Wanting to go inside temples: Senso-ji's main hall shuts at 17:00
  • Rainy or very hazy nights: the views lose most of their point
  • Late starts: the observatory and MAG8 stop letting people in at 21:30
We suggest: on your first night, do Shinjuku at dusk (free observatory, then Hanazono Shrine and ramen by Golden Gai). Keep Asakusa for a night you're staying nearby, so you can linger until the lights go off at about 23:00.

Route 1: Asakusa lit up (about 10 minutes of walking)

Start: Asakusa Station (Ginza Line), then cross to the Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center opposite Kaminarimon.

  1. 1 Tourist centre terrace. The free 8th-floor terrace (open to 22:00) looks straight down Nakamise to the temple. Start here for the overview.
  2. 2 Kaminarimon. The big gate and its lantern are lit from sunset. Crossing the road takes a minute.
  3. 3 Hozomon. Walk up Nakamise after the stalls close and you see their painted shutters, which daytime visitors never do.
  4. 4 Five-storied pagoda. It glows after dark, and the crowds have gone. The main hall is lit too, but closed inside after 17:00.

Where to eat on this walk: for a big night, Asakusa Imahan on Kokusai-dori has served sukiyaki since 1895, close to the Tsukuba Express Asakusa Station. Last entry is 19:00, so eat first and walk after. Dinner starts at about ¥15,000; it's 4★+ on Google Maps (checked 27 Nov 2026). If you stay the night, our Senso-ji morning or evening guide has the ¥850 miso soup breakfast at MISOJYU from 8:00.

Route 2: Shinjuku's free view (about 30 minutes of walking)

Start: about 10 minutes on foot from the JR Shinjuku West Gate (Tochomae Station on the Oedo Line is closer).

  1. 1 Free observatory. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building's South Observatory sits 202 m up and is open until 22:00, last entry 21:30. Allow 20–30 minutes for the lift queue. After dark, the free TOKYO Night & Light projection plays on the building until 21:45.
  2. 2 East-side streets. Cross under the tracks to Shinjuku-sanchome and wander toward dinner. This is the longest stretch, about 30 minutes of walking in all.
  3. 3 Hanazono Shrine. Shinjuku's guardian shrine sits between Yasukuni-dori and the back of Golden Gai, next to Shinjuku-sanchome Exit E2. It's open 24 hours, it's free, and its red gates take you from neon to lanterns in a few steps.

Where to eat on this walk: from our Shinjuku food guide: Sugoi Niboshi Ramen Nagi, ten seats up a steep Golden Gai staircase, open all night, about ¥1,000 for the intense sardine-broth ramen. Or Keika Ramen by the East Exit, serving Kumamoto pork-bone ramen since 1968, until 23:00. Both were 4★+ on Google Maps when we checked.

Route 3: Shibuya Crossing from 3 angles (about 5 minutes of walking)

Start: inside Shibuya Station, in the 2F passage between JR and the Keio Inokashira Line (Shibuya Mark City).

  1. 1 Mark City passage. Free windows look down on the crossing, next to Taro Okamoto's 30 m mural Myth of Tomorrow. The glass is meshed, so it's better for watching than for photos.
  2. 2 Hachiko Square. About 50 m from the JR Hachiko Exit. Watch one full light cycle (about 2 minutes), then cross with the next wave.
  3. 3 MAG8 rooftop. On top of MAGNET by SHIBUYA109, looking straight down on the scramble: ¥1,800 with one drink, cashless, last entry 21:30.

Where to eat on this walk: from our Shibuya food guide: Torifuku in Nonbei Yokocho, a yakitori counter grilling since 1932 (16:00–21:00, about ¥6,000–8,000). Uoshin up Dogenzaka serves sashimi and sushi piled with sea urchin until 23:00. And if the night runs long, Bura Bura serves abura soba until 7:00.

Route 4: Roppongi at Christmas (about 5 minutes of walking)

Start: Roppongi Station, Hibiya Line Exit 1C, about a minute from Roppongi Hills. This one runs until 25 December 2026.

  1. 1 Christmas market. At O-YANE Plaza, 21 November–25 December 2026, 11:00–21:00, free. 2026 is its 20th year: about ten huts modelled on Stuttgart's market, with Käthe Wohlfahrt's German ornaments.
  2. 2 Keyakizaka. From 17:00 the street's "Snow & Blue" lights are on until 22:00. Walk down it with a hot drink from the market.

Where to eat on this walk: the market itself is dinner. It has Glühwein, sausages, stollen and fresh pretzels, and on weekdays there are lunch sets. For a roomier, more grown-up market with more seats, our Tokyo Christmas markets 2026 guide covers Azabudai Hills and seven others.

🚇 Hopping between Asakusa, Shibuya and Roppongi by subway in one evening? A Tokyo Subway Ticket covers the Ginza, Hibiya and Oedo lines these walks start from, so you don't stop at a ticket machine each time.

📅 Good to know before you go

  • Last entries: the Tokyo Metropolitan Government observatory and MAG8 both stop letting people in at 21:30.
  • Asakusa: the gates, pagoda and main hall are lit every day from sunset until about 23:00, but the hall is closed inside after 17:00.
  • Roppongi: the Christmas market ends on 25 December 2026; after that, Route 4 is just the street.
  • Weather: MAG8 may shorten its hours or close in bad weather, and haze hides the observatory view.

One more thing: the same sukiyaki house, at lunch prices

Asakusa Imahan's sukiyaki dinners start at about ¥15,000, but the 1895 house also opens at 11:30 for lunch, from about ¥3,000. At lunch it serves rice bowls, each limited to 20 a day: the "Hyakunen gyudon" (hundred-year beef bowl), a Meiji-style sukiyaki bowl and a steak bowl. So you can taste the place at midday and keep the evening free for the lit-up walk.
