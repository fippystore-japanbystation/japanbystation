Tenma & Tenjinbashisuji: Where Osaka Locals Actually Eat

Japan's longest shopping street, sushi at counter prices and standing bars after six. One stop from Osaka Station.

EatHalf-day routeWhere to stay

Most visitors eat in Dotonbori and never see this side of Osaka. One stop from Osaka Station, Tenma is where office workers drink after work and families do their daily shopping, under a roof that runs for about 2.6 km. If you have one free evening in Osaka and you care about food, we'd spend it here.

About a 4-minute read · facts checked 23 November 2026

Who Tenma suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Food-first travellers who want local prices and fewer tour groups
  • Couples and friends who want a night out without a long ride home
  • Anyone using Osaka Station for day trips: it's one stop away
  • Rainy days: the main street is covered end to end

Think twice

  • Light sleepers: the lanes north of JR Tenma stay loud late
  • Big-name luxury hotels: those are in Umeda
  • Very young kids at night: the busiest bars are small, smoky and standing-only

Getting there

JR Tenma Station is one stop from Osaka Station on the Osaka Loop Line, and a JR Pass covers it. By subway, the Sakaisuji Line stops at Minamimorimachi, Ogimachi and Tenjinbashisuji 6-chome, all on the shopping street. Tenjinbashisuji 6-chome is also on the Tanimachi Line, and some Hankyu trains from Kyoto run straight through to it, so check the departure board if you're coming that way.

Eat like a local

Tenjinbashisuji is said to be Japan's longest shopping street: about 2.6 km, roughly 40 minutes end to end if you never stop, which you will. Most shops are small, family-run and priced for people who live here.

  • Harukoma (sushi): the oldest sushi shop on the street and the one with the queue. Pieces are big and plates start at around ¥100. At the tables you write your order on a slip of paper, and the chefs work fast. It's open 11:00 to 21:30 and closed on Tuesdays. Go mid-afternoon for the shortest line. Sushi Masa, almost opposite, is the local alternative when the queue is too long.
  • Croquettes from the butchers: meat shops along the arcade fry korokke at the counter for a few hundred yen. This is the real walking snack here, more than takoyaki.
  • Standing bars (tachinomi) north of JR Tenma: the lanes called Ura-Tenma fill up after six. Order a drink and two or three small plates, then move on. Hopping is the point.
  • Takoyaki: yes, it's here too, usually cheaper and with shorter lines than in Dotonbori.
Cash tip: many small stalls and standing bars still prefer cash. Carry coins and ¥1,000 notes.

A half-day walking route

  1. 10:00 Start at Osaka Tenmangu Shrine, the shrine that gave Tenma its name. It's quiet in the morning and a short walk from Minamimorimachi Station.
  2. 10:30 Walk north up the arcade and snack as you go: croquettes, rice crackers, fruit.
  3. 12:00 Lunch at Harukoma, or any busy set-meal shop with locals waiting outside.
  4. 13:30 Keep going towards 6-chome, where the arcade gets calmer and more residential.
  5. 15:00 Back to your hotel for a rest. Tenma is at its best after dark.
  6. 18:30 Ura-Tenma, north of JR Tenma: two or three standing bars, then a late bowl of ramen.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Cherry blossom week (mid-April): the Japan Mint, about 15 minutes' walk from Temmabashi Station, opens its roughly 560 m cherry blossom lane for about one week. Entry is free but you must reserve online in advance (in 2026 it ran 9 to 15 April, with booking from mid-March). Osaka hotels get much more expensive that week; most offer free cancellation, so booking early costs nothing.
  • Tenjin Matsuri (24 and 25 July): one of Japan's biggest festivals, centred on Osaka Tenmangu. Hotels near the shrine fill first.
  • Osaka Museum of Housing and Living: its life-size 1830s Osaka street is closed for renovation until 31 December 2026, and it normally closes on Tuesdays. Check the reopening date before you plan around it.

Nearby, if you have more time

  • Osaka Castle: a few subway stops south-east and an easy morning add-on. Sort your castle tickets before you go →
  • Nakanoshima: riverside museums and a rose garden, a pleasant walk south of the arcade.
  • Kema Sakuranomiya Park: a long riverside cherry-blossom walk beside the Mint, where locals have their hanami picnics.

One more thing: write your sushi order down

At Harukoma's tables you don't need Japanese: you write your order on a slip of paper (at the counter you just tell the chef). Take a photo of the menu board while you queue, decide in the line, and you'll be eating within minutes of sitting down while everyone else is still reading.
