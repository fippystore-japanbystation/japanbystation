Food Near Namba Station: Eight Places From a Morning Cheesecake to Late-Night Skewers

Takoyaki, omurice, kitsune udon and Ura-Namba skewers, with the hours and closing days that catch people out.

MorningLunchDinner and late

Namba wakes up late. Hardly anything good opens before 9:30, then the area serves food until almost midnight. So plan a cheesecake or takoyaki morning, have omurice or kitsune udon for lunch, and spend the evening on the Sennichimae and Ura-Namba side streets, a few minutes from the gates. Each of these eight places had 4★+ on Google Maps when we checked. Hours and prices come from their own listings.

Who this list suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Staying around Namba or arriving on the Nankai line from Kansai Airport
  • Travellers who want Osaka classics without a long queue plan
  • Night owls: two picks serve until 22:30 or later

Think twice

  • Early trains: no pick here opens before 9:30
  • Halal or vegetarian diets: none of these lists it officially
  • Card-only travellers: four picks take cash or PayPay only
We suggest: omurice at Hokkyokusei for a quick sit-down lunch under Takashimaya, kitsune udon at Imai for the classic, and Ura-Namba skewers after dark. Arriving by train? Our guide to Namba's stations shows which one is which.

A sweet, slow morning before the shops open (from 9:30)

  • Rikuro Ojisan no Mise (りくろーおじさんの店), Namba main shop, 2 min from Midosuji Namba: the fluffy cheesecake Osaka gives as a gift, about ¥965 for a whole cake. You can watch them being made through the window of the ground-floor shop. Open 9:30–21:30, with occasional closing days. No cards.
  • Takoyaki Wanaka (わなか), Sennichimae main shop, about 5 min: takoyaki from ¥500, with a self-service counter to eat at. Opens 10:30, or 9:30 on Saturdays and holidays, until 21:00, every day of the year. No cards.

A lunch from Osaka's own recipe book

  • Hokkyokusei (北極星), Takashimaya Osaka B1, connected to Nankai Namba: omurice at a small eat-in counter in the food hall. Hokkyokusei was founded in 1922 and says its founder created omurice. About ¥1,000. Open 10:00–20:00 (last order 19:30). It closes when the store does.
  • Dotonbori Imai Honten (道頓堀 今井), Metro Exit 14, 5 min: our one Dotonbori pick. Kitsune udon is ¥930. The broth is a Hokkaido kombu dashi simmered for about an hour with Kyushu fish flakes. Open 11:30–21:30 (last order 21:00). Closed Wednesdays and the 4th Tuesday, except in December.
  • 551 Horai (551蓬莱), main shop, 2 min from Midosuji Namba: pork buns since 1945, about ¥330 each. Take them away and eat on the train. Open 11:00–21:30. Closed the 1st and 3rd Tuesdays.

Smoky side streets after dark, until 23:30

  • Ajinoya Honten (味乃家), Sennichimae side, about 6 min: upstairs on a side street. Order the Ajinoya Mix, an okonomiyaki with pork, squid, octopus, shrimp and two eggs, from about ¥1,000. Open 11:00–22:00. Closed Mondays. Cash or PayPay.
  • Osaka Yakiton Center (大阪焼トンセンター), Ura-Namba, 3 min from Nankai Namba: grilled pork skewers, with a standing floor at street level. About ¥2,000–3,000 a head. Open 15:00–23:30 (last order 22:45), with occasional closing days. PayPay only, no cards. Smoking is allowed and under-20s can't enter.
  • Meoto Zenzai (夫婦善哉), Hozenji Yokocho, 3 min: sweet red-bean soup in a lantern-lit alley, under ¥1,000. Retty reviewers say one order comes split into two small bowls, the "married couple" of the name. Open 10:00–22:30 every day.

📅 Good to know before you go

  • Closing days: Ajinoya closes Mondays, Imai Wednesdays (and the 4th Tuesday except in December), 551 Horai the 1st and 3rd Tuesdays.
  • Cash and PayPay: Rikuro and Wanaka don't take cards; Ajinoya takes cash or PayPay; Yakiton Center takes PayPay only.
  • Takashimaya hours: Hokkyokusei follows the store's closing days.
  • New Year: 551 Horai's main shop is closed on 31 December.

One more thing: the udon shop where kitsune udon is said to have begun

One stop north, about 5 minutes from Shinsaibashi Station, Usami-tei Matsubaya has served udon since 1893. The story goes that the founder put the fried tofu meant for inari sushi on a plain bowl of udon, and kitsune udon was born. A bowl is about ¥650. It closes early in the evening and on Sundays and holidays, and takes no cards. It also had 4★+ on Google Maps when we checked.
