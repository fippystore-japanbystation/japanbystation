Luggage at Tokyo Station: Coin Lockers, Staffed Storage for Several Days, and the Quiet Lockers

More than 900 lockers in one room, and only 40 of them take a big suitcase. Here's where your case can wait.

PricesWhere the big ones areIf they're full

Tokyo Station's biggest locker room, on B1 inside the gates by GRANSTA, has 728 small lockers, 152 medium and just 40 large. That is the whole problem in one number. A large locker costs about ¥900–1,000 a day, and by late morning the big ones are often gone. So book one ahead, or use the staffed counter that keeps bags for several nights at ¥420 a night, or send the case to your hotel before 11:00.

Who this page suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Arriving by Shinkansen or Narita Express before check-in
  • A side trip of a few nights while your big case stays in Tokyo
  • A last day of sightseeing before an evening train

Think twice

  • Leaving the case more than 3 days: lockers won't do, use the staffed counter
  • Late-night pick-ups: staffed counters close by 20:00–21:00
  • Hotels outside the 23 wards: same-day locker delivery won't reach you
We suggest: book a large Multi ecube locker the day before (¥500 plus the locker fee), or hand the case to the Baggage Storage Center if you'll be away for a night or more. Off the Narita Express? Try the quiet lockers on B4 first (see the end of this page).

Paying: most touch-panel lockers take Suica or PASMO, and your card becomes the key. Some older lockers want coins. Multi ecube lockers also take QR payments and credit cards. Lockers hold bags for up to 3 days, and on many of them a new day starts at 2:00.

Where to leave it, by where you're going

  • Getting off the Shinkansen? The lockers by the Yaesu North gate are the closest, on the same floor as the Shinkansen gates. They are also the first to fill, so book them if you can.
  • Changing to JR lines inside the gates? Go down to B1 by GRANSTA. It's the biggest locker space in the station, with over 900 lockers, but only 40 of them are large.
  • Heading out the Marunouchi side, towards the Imperial Palace? There are lockers inside the Marunouchi Central gate and outside the North gate, in the North free passage. Neither bank has large lockers in JR East's list.
  • Going to Disney or Makuhari on the Keiyo Line? The Keiyo underground passage has its own bank. JR East says it is often quieter because it's far from the other lines.

Lifts matter with a big case. The south transfer gate of the Tohoku and Hokuriku Shinkansen has no lift, and neither does the central transfer gate of the Tokaido Shinkansen. Use the north and south gates instead.

All full? Plan B, C and D

  1. Look Check live availability. Multi ecube's site shows which of its lockers are free right now, by size, with no sign-up. Booking a locker costs ¥500 and opens a month ahead.
  2. Hand it over Staffed storage. The Baggage Storage Center, outside the gates, keeps bags for several nights at ¥420 per piece per night (¥840 from the sixth night), 8:00–20:00, tel. 03-3231-0804. Same-day storage is ¥600 per piece and ends at 20:30. GRANSTA Cloak on B1, inside the gates, is open 8:30–21:00.
  3. Ask JR EAST Travel Service Center, just left of the Marunouchi North gate on 1F, 8:30–19:00. It also holds bags for the day.
  4. Send it To your hotel the same day. Book a Multi ecube delivery by 9:30, drop the case in the locker by 11:00, and it reaches your hotel by 18:00. It delivers to hotels in Tokyo's 23 wards and Urayasu (over 1,000 hotels across the service), at about ¥2,500–2,700 (current campaign price).

📅 Good to know before you go

  • Large lockers first: on busy days the suitcase-size lockers fill by late morning. Multi ecube bookings open a month ahead and cover up to 3 days.
  • Shinkansen with a big case: 161–250 cm in total needs a reserved seat with an oversized baggage area on the Tokaido Shinkansen. Book it with the ticket.
  • Several nights away: lockers stop at 3 days; the Baggage Storage Center keeps bags by the night.

One more thing: the lockers four floors down

When the B1 lockers are full, keep going down. On B4, inside the gates on the way to the Sobu Line (Rapid) platforms, there's a big bank of lockers by the Sobu underground lift. Multi ecube's own guide calls them the ones most likely to be free, simply because few people walk that far. The Narita Express uses those underground platforms too, so if you arrive on the N'EX the quiet lockers are on your way up.
