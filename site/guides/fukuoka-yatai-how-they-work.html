Fukuoka Yatai: How They Work, and Nakasu, Tenjin or Nagahama?

About 100 street stalls with ten seats each, built every evening and gone by morning. Here's how to get a seat, what to order and which area to pick.

How it worksWhich areaAn evening

For a first yatai night, we'd start in Tenjin: the most variety, a short walk from the station, and stalls like Kokinchan that are famous for yaki-ramen. Go to Nakasu if you want the classic picture of lanterns on the river, and to Nagahama if you want ramen with fewer people. Wherever you go, bring cash, arrive around 18:00 to 19:00, and plan to visit two or three stalls, not one.

Who yatai suit, and who they don't

Good fit

  • Solo travellers and pairs: groups of two or three are ideal, and solo is fine
  • Anyone who likes talking to strangers: you sit elbow to elbow
  • Food hoppers who want three small meals, not one big one

Think twice

  • Groups of five or more: there are only about ten seats per stall
  • Rainy nights: many stalls simply don't open
  • If you need a toilet nearby: stalls have none (ask staff for the nearest)

How a yatai works

Fukuoka has about 100 yatai (101 on the city's count in April 2026). Since 2013 they have been run under a city ordinance, Japan's first of its kind. A stall may only use the street from 17:00 to 4:00 next morning, setting up and packing away included, and everything, seats too, has to fit inside 3 m × 2.5 m (city rules).

  • Hours: some stalls open at 18:00, most at 18:30 to 19:00, and most close around midnight. There are no fixed holidays. Each stall decides, and many close in rain or storms (official guide).
  • Getting a seat: ask "two people, OK?" If there's a queue, join the end.
  • Ordering: check the prices, then order at least one drink and one dish per person. That's the polite minimum. Most stalls have no cover charge, but some bar-style yatai do.
  • Paying: pay when you leave. Most stalls are cash only, and only about 10% take credit cards. Some newer ones take QR payments like PayPay.
  • Manners: travel light, sit close, don't take food away, don't linger once you've finished, then try another stall.

Nakasu, Tenjin or Nagahama?

A · The classic scene

Nakasu

Lanterns on the Naka River

A row of about 20 stalls at the south end of Nakasu Island, about 5 to 8 minutes from Nakasu-Kawabata or Kushida Shrine station. Busiest 19:00 to 22:00. A newer cluster, Ura-Nakasu, has 10 stalls on Showa-dori, about 3 minutes north of the station.

B · Our first-timer pick

Tenjin

Most variety, near the trains

Stalls along Watanabe-dori and Showa-dori, right in the shopping district. Ramen, yaki-ramen and the occasional French stall. Kokinchan is about 2 minutes from Tenjin Station Exit 1.

C · The ramen row

Nagahama

Smaller, by the fish market

The old home of Nagahama tonkotsu ramen. It was down to two stalls by 2021, and seven new ones opened in June 2023. About a 9-minute walk from Akasaka Station.

Verdict: Tenjin for a first night, Nakasu for the photo and the buzz, Nagahama for ramen and a quieter seat. The answer changes if it's raining (stay where you can walk between several stalls quickly) or if you're four or more (go early, around 18:00, or split up).

What to order, and where

Tonkotsu ramen, yakitori and oden are the standards at almost every stall. Yaki-ramen, stir-fried ramen, is the one locals are talking about now. No yatai serves raw food.

  • Kokinchan (Tenjin): yaki-ramen ¥820, mentaiko tamagoyaki ¥750. Tabelog lists Mon–Wed 18:30–1:30, Fri–Sat to 2:00, closed Thursday and Sunday. No cards, but e-money is accepted.
  • Takechan fr Tsukasa (Seiryu Park, Nakasu): yaki-ramen and a mentaiko omelette made with tonkotsu stock. 18:00–1:00, irregular days off. Some menus are in several languages.
  • Tenjin Yatai Zonochan (Watanabe-dori): opened April 2025. Hakata ramen, bite-size gyoza and tonkotsu yaki-ramen. 18:15–24:00, cash only.
  • Mentai Chudoku (Nagahama): Fukuoka's first stall devoted to mentaiko (spicy cod roe). 18:00–24:00, PayPay only.
  • Yatai no Tamachan (Nagahama): yakitori, oden and Nagahama ramen. 18:30–24:00, weather permitting, QR payments OK.

An easy three-stall evening

  1. 18:00 Walk Watanabe-dori in Tenjin while the stalls finish setting up. It's the best time to get a seat.
  2. 18:30 Stall one: yaki-ramen and mentaiko tamagoyaki at Kokinchan (not Thursday or Sunday) or Zonochan, with one drink.
  3. 19:30 Walk about 15 minutes east to Nakasu and cross to the river.
  4. 20:00 Stall two: yakitori or oden on the riverside row, or try Ura-Nakasu on Showa-dori if the queues are long.
  5. 21:30 Stall three: a last bowl of tonkotsu ramen, then a short walk back to Nakasu-Kawabata.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Weather decides. Stalls have no fixed holidays and many close in rain or wind. With two nights in Fukuoka, you get a second chance.
  • Fridays and Saturdays have the longest waits (japan-guide.com). Arriving between 18:00 and 19:00 is the easiest way in.
  • New stalls are coming. The city is choosing operators for 23 more spaces across Nakasu, Tenjin and Nagahama, opening in April and May 2027.
  • Leave your luggage first. Stalls are tiny. Drop bags at the hotel (most hotels offer free cancellation, so booking early costs nothing).

One more thing: a light bulb tells you if a stall is open

Because yatai close for rain, wind or a day off at short notice, Fukuoka City fitted stalls with smart light bulbs. The official LINE account "FUKUOKA GUIDE" uses them to show which stalls are actually open tonight, and you can search the stalls in English by area or by food. Check it before you leave the hotel, not after a wet walk to a dark street. Landing at Fukuoka Airport's international terminal? There's a yatai concierge desk on 1F, open 8:00 to 21:30.
