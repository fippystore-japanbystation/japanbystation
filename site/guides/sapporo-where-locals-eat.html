Where Sapporo Locals Eat: Markets, Soup Curry and Ramen

Breakfast where the wholesale buyers go, soup curry in a basement and a 1 a.m. bowl of ramen.

Which marketSoup curryRamen

For a seafood breakfast, we'd go to the Curb Market, next to Sapporo's central wholesale market and about 20 minutes from Odori, rather than the better-known Nijo Market downtown. For soup curry, head to a basement off Odori (Garaku) or ride out to Kotoni, where people actually live. For ramen, do what Susukino does: finish the night with a bowl, in the famous alley or at Keyaki around the corner, open until 2 a.m.

Who this food plan suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Food-first travellers with two or three days in Sapporo
  • Early risers: market stock is best from 8:00 to 11:00
  • Winter visitors: soup curry and ramen are built for cold nights
  • Anyone happy to ride the subway two or three stops for a better meal

Think twice

  • Very tight budgets: market crab and seafood bowls aren't cheap
  • Late sleepers: the Curb Market is quieter and emptier by the afternoon
  • Spice-averse eaters: start soup curry at the mildest level

Nijo Market or Curb Market?

A · Curb Market · Our pick

60 shops · 6:00–17:00

Where the city's kitchen is

Next to Sapporo's central wholesale market, Hokkaido's biggest. About 60 stores selling fish, crab, vegetables, fruit and Hokkaido sweets, and about 20 restaurants (open from 7:00), most attached to fish shops, so you can eat what you pick. Tozai Line from Odori to Nijuyonken (about 10 minutes), then 7 minutes on foot.

B · Nijo Market

Downtown · 7:00–18:00

History on your way

Dating from the early Meiji period, with some shops over 100 years old, right by Bus Center-mae Station and a short walk from Odori. Restaurants open 6:00 to 21:00, so it also works for a late breakfast or an early dinner. Both markets are open every day.

C · Both, different days

2+ days

Breakfast at one, lunch at the other

Curb Market for an early seafood breakfast on a free morning, Nijo for a seafood lunch between sights downtown. Neither needs more than an hour or two.

Our verdict: if you can be on the subway by 8:00 one morning, go to the Curb Market. The city's tourism office says stock thins out after the busy 8:00–11:00 window, and the restaurants fill with local workers at lunchtime. Choose Nijo if you only have a spare hour downtown or you're going after 15:00. Nijo's restaurants stay open until 21:00, while the Curb Market closes at 17:00.
How to shop: almost every stall offers tastings, so try a few kinds of salmon roe before you buy. Live crabs can be boiled on the spot or shipped, and heavier crabs have more meat.

Soup curry: where Sapporo goes

Soup curry is Sapporo's own dish: a thin, spiced broth with big pieces of Hokkaido vegetables and chicken or pork, served with rice on the side. You choose the spice level, the rice size and a topping or two. Two toppings is plenty.

  • Garaku (central): in a basement at Minami 2-jo Nishi 2-chome, near Odori. The pork-bone and herb broth simmers for two days, with kelp and bonito added, and the shop closes when it runs out, so go early.
  • Okushiba Shoten (by Sapporo Station): in the basement of the Hokuren Building, Kita 4-jo Nishi 1-chome. Known for a rich shrimp broth, and the easiest pick with luggage or an early train.
  • Kotoni (residential): a few stops west of Sapporo Station on JR, or on the Tozai Line. Picante sits under the elevated JR Kotoni Station and cooks each curry in its own small pot. Sapporo Rakkyo, nearby, has a classic chicken, pork and tomato broth that suits first-timers. It closes on the third Wednesday of the month.

Ramen beyond the Ramen Alley

Ganso Ramen Yokocho in Susukino is a 42-metre alley lined with 17 miso ramen shops. It's crowded from morning to night, and it's also where Sapporo people end a night of drinking with a bowl. It's worth a look, but the shops around it run on the same late-night habit.

  • Keyaki: Minami 6-jo Nishi 3-chome, 3 minutes from Susukino Station, open 10:00 to 2:00 (last order 1:45), every day of the year. A good bet when the alley queues are long.
  • Order like a local: miso is the Sapporo style. Spicy miso and miso with char siu are the alley's most popular orders with overseas visitors.

A one-day eating route

  1. 7:30 Tozai Line to Nijuyonken and walk to the Curb Market. Seafood bowl at one of the fish-shop restaurants.
  2. 9:30 Shop for crab or roe while stock is fresh. Ask about shipping.
  3. 12:30 Back downtown. Light lunch, or a look round Nijo Market by Bus Center-mae.
  4. 17:30 Early dinner: soup curry at Garaku before the broth runs out.
  5. 22:30 Susukino after dark, then a miso ramen to finish, in the alley or at Keyaki.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • No closing days at the markets: both the Curb Market and Nijo Market list no regular closing days, so your market morning can go on any day of the trip. Individual shops still set their own hours.
  • Soup curry sells out: Garaku closes when its broth is gone and other shops close irregularly, so keep a second choice nearby.
  • Book early for winter weeks: Sapporo's big winter weeks fill hotels near Odori and Susukino first. Most hotels on the big booking sites offer free cancellation, so holding a room costs nothing.

Breakfast the way Sapporo does it

  • Arinko: a handmade onigiri shop since 1980. The tourism office suggests the "cheese katsuo" rice ball with tonjiru pork and vegetable miso soup. Take it to Odori Park for a picnic.
  • Sandria: a takeout-only sandwich shop since 1978, open 24 hours with more than 40 kinds. The double egg sandwich is the most popular.

One more thing: a free ride to breakfast, and a bath after

Two Curb Market restaurants, Kita no Ryoba and Kaisen-Shokudo Kitano Gourmet-tei, run a free shuttle from hotels and JR Sapporo Station if you reserve a day ahead, so you don't have to work out the subway at 7 a.m. And if you're still there after lunch, the market has its own neighbourhood bathhouse, Sakae-yu, which opens at 13:00. It's a sento, not a tourist spa, and a warm soak is a very Sapporo way to finish a cold market morning.
