Kyoto in December 2026: Last Leaves, Temple Markets and the New Year Bells

The lantern path you may have read about is gone. Here's what's actually on, with the 2026 dates from Kyoto's official listings.

CalendarRing a bellWhere to stay

If an older guide promised you the Arashiyama Hanatouro lantern path in December, skip it: Kyoto Prefecture ended it after 2021, and this year's bamboo light-up finished on 8 November. December in Kyoto is quieter and more local instead: radish stew at a temple on 7–8 December, the year's last flea markets at Toji (21st) and Kitano Tenmangu (25th), and on New Year's Eve, temples where you can take a turn on the bell yourself. For most visitors we'd plan around one market day and 31 December.

Early December: the last of autumn

Many of Kyoto's special autumn openings run until 6 December in 2026, among them Tofukuji's leaf-viewing opening and the evening light-up at Hogon-in in Arashiyama. Arrive in the first week and you still catch the end of the colour, with far fewer people than in late November. After that, the temples are back to their normal hours and much calmer.

7–8 December: radish stew at Senbon Shakado

Every year on 7 and 8 December, Senbon Shakado (Daihoon-ji) marks the day the Buddha reached enlightenment by simmering big pots of daikon radish with fried tofu and handing out bowls. The radish is blessed first, and the stew is said to keep illness away. It runs 10:00–16:00 and costs ¥1,000. The custom goes back to the Kamakura period, when monks wrote sacred characters on the cut radish. Take city bus 50 to Kamishichiken; it's a 3-minute walk.

The year's last temple and shrine markets

Kyoto has two big monthly markets, and their December editions are the busiest of the year, full of New Year decorations alongside the usual antiques, pottery, old kimono, plants and food.

  • Shimai-Kobo at Toji, 21 December (8:00–15:00). The last Kobo-ichi of the year. Toji is about 10 minutes' walk from Kintetsu Toji Station, one stop from Kyoto Station, so it's easy on a travel day.
  • Shimai-Tenjin at Kitano Tenmangu, 25 December (6:30–21:00). Stalls start opening around 6:30, but most are up by about 9:00. Look for shimenawa straw decorations and herring roe for the New Year table. City bus 50 stops at Kitano Tenmangu-mae.

31 December: watch the bell, or ring it

On New Year's Eve, temples strike their bells 108 times to clear away the year's worldly desires (joya no kane). Kyoto gives you two very different ways to be there.

  • Watch: Chion-in. One of Japan's three great bells, about 70 tonnes, struck by 17 monks with a 4 m log from 22:20. In 2026 watching is by advance application only, ¥3,000 per person (free for elementary school age and under). Can't get in? The trial ringing on 27 December at 14:00 is free to watch.
  • Ring it: Kodaiji. From 23:45; numbered tickets are handed out from 22:00 to the first 108 groups, one strike per group.
  • Ring it, no ticket: Shinnyo-do (from 23:45, four or five people strike together, free herbal tea while it lasts), Eikando (anyone in the queue between 23:00 and 24:30 gets a turn), Mibu-dera (from 23:30, about ten people per strike, no limit) and Chion-ji at Hyakumanben (23:30–0:30).
  • Early night instead: Myoman-ji rings a "year-sending bell" from 15:00 to 17:00, so you can ring out the year and still be at dinner by six.
Getting home: plan the trip back before you go. Kurama-dera also rings its bell at 23:30, but the official listing warns there is no night cable car.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Chion-in on 31 December is by advance application only (¥3,000). If you want to watch it, apply before you fly.
  • Kodaiji gives out only 108 tickets, from 22:00, one per group.
  • Fixed dates: daikon-daki is always 7–8 December, Shimai-Kobo the 21st, Shimai-Tenjin the 25th. Build your days around them, not the other way round.
  • Hotels: rooms near Gion go first for New Year's Eve; most listings offer free cancellation, so booking early costs nothing.

One more thing: Kyoto's oldest working bell

Hoon-ji, a small temple in Kamigyo, rings its bell from 23:45 on New Year's Eve, usually stopping at 108 strikes. The bell is an Important Cultural Property and, according to the city's listing, the oldest bell still in use in Kyoto. It's a world away from the crowds at Chion-in.
