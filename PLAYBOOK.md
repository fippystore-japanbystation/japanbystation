# Japan by Station: production playbook

> READ STRATEGY.md FIRST (goal, channel roles, content mix, engagement). It wins over any rule below that conflicts with it.

Ling runs **Japan by Station**: japanbystation.com (Japan-only English travel guides) and Instagram **@japanbystation**.
Each Reel answers ONE traveler question and gives the solution. Viewers comment a KEYWORD and n8n DMs them the guide page.
**The guide page must be live (and its keyword in keywords.json) before its Reel is queued** (except SCENIC Reels, section 3).

## 0. Session setup (every fresh session)
```
git clone -q -b studio https://github.com/fippystore-japanbystation/japanbystation.git ~/jbs/studio   # add_repo first if git is refused
cd ~/jbs/studio && python3 jbs.py setup      # clones main + media into ~/jbs, downloads the Kokoro voice model
python3 jbs.py status                        # what's queued, which keywords exist
```
Always finish with `python3 jbs.py save "what you did"` so the next session has your new sources (no Cloudflare build).

## 1. Voice and facts (non-negotiable)
- NEVER repeat a topic. Before writing anything, read the DONE list in TOPICS.md and the existing site/guides and reel/specs. Add every new guide/Reel to DONE.
- Professional team voice: "we suggest", "we'd take", "our pick". Never "Honestly?", no overly friendly AI phrasing, no hype.
- Never invent team names, credentials or personal stays. We build guides from official sources.
- Every time, price, opening day and distance comes from a source you found this session (operator, airport, city tourism bureau such as osaka-info.jp / kyoto.travel, the venue itself). If only blogs say it, write "about" or leave it out. Put sources in the guide's `sources` field with the check date.
- Explain WHY (minutes saved, price, fewer changes, who it suits). Say who it does NOT suit.
- No affiliate or commission wording inside videos or captions. No fake urgency.
- EVERGREEN TOPICS ONLY (Ling, 24 Nov, after the "Hakone Ropeway Closed: is the day still worth it?" page): never build a guide, FB post or Reel around a one-day event or disruption ("closed today", "service suspended this morning", "on the day we checked…"). Ling: we make GENERAL, new topics; "X is closed, is the day still worth it?" is not practical. If a brief is framed as a disruption (closure, bad weather, cancelled train), turn it into the general everyday decision behind it (e.g. "Gora to Lake Ashi: bus or ropeway?") and mention disruptions only as one line of advice. Planned multi-week closures may appear only as a short "Good to know before you book" line. EXCEPTION (Ling, 25 Nov): SEASON pages, Reels and posts about multi-week seasonal events (Christmas markets, winter illuminations, New Year, light-ups) ARE wanted, with exact dates ("13 Nov–25 Dec 2026") from the official site; title them with the year; after the event, add a line "2026 has ended; dates for next year usually appear in …".
- NOT ALWAYS DOTONBORI / NAMBA (Ling, 23 Nov; guides AND Reels). Too many Osaka pieces end with "stay in Namba, walk to Dotonbori". Every Osaka piece must include at least one less obvious area as a real option, framed as "besides Namba / Dotonbori, consider X, because …" with 1–2 researched whys (e.g. Tenma, Kitashinchi, Nakazakicho, Tanimachi, Kyobashi, Fukushima, Bentencho, Shin-Osaka, Kitahama), matched to who it suits. Do not use Dotonbori as the reference point in more than one answer per piece. Same idea in Tokyo: not every answer is Shinjuku or Shibuya.

## 2. Website guides
FOOD BLOG (Ling, 24 Nov; TOPICS.md lines marked "FOOD", F01…): a lighter blog-style page about food that is UNIQUE, DIFFERENT, worth trying. Not the DEEP TEMPLATE. Rules:
- 5–7 dishes or snacks. About two thirds not mainstream (what locals eat, a regional oddity, the dish visitors walk past); one or two well-known classics so readers recognise the page. Never a list of "the best".
- Each dish: what it is in one plain sentence; why it's different or special (history, how it's made, how locals eat it); where to try it: one well-known place AND one smaller local place, each with area/nearest station, price level, closing day if known; how to order/eat it (one tip). 2–4 sentences per dish, warm, specific, no hype.
- LINK OUT (Ling: "refer other links"): link the sources inline, like a blog: the shop's own site or its Tabelog page, the city/prefecture tourism page, a museum or association page. 1–2 outside links per dish, rel="noopener" target="_blank". Also link our related station/area guides.
- 600–900 words, answer-first lede ("If you only try one unusual thing in X, make it …"), 3–5 photos of the dishes or the food street (Pexels only; if no photo of that exact dish, show the area or the market, never a different dish), a short FAQ (2–3), sources with check date. City = the region's city value. One UPPERCASE keyword (e.g. MONJA).
- VARY THE FORMAT (Ling, 24 Nov): food pages must not all look the same. Rotate one of these per page (check the last 2 food pages and pick a different one; note the format in TOPICS.md DONE):
  A. "Eat it differently": the unusual WAY locals eat something familiar (dip it, pour broth on it, eat it for breakfast, the order you're meant to eat it in), then where to try.
  B. "Reversed": start from the place (a market, a station, a street) and what to eat there in order, instead of dish by dish.
  C. "Classic vs local": each pair = the famous version and the one locals prefer, side by side.
  D. "Would you try it?": the strangest-sounding dishes first, with why they're actually good; ends on the safe classic.
  E. "One dish, deep": one regional dish told properly (story, how it's made, 2–3 places, how to order), for dishes with enough to say.
  Official sources (tourism boards, prefecture pages, the shop's own site) are welcome as links. The point is engagement: show something different and special people can try in that area.
- DEPACHIKA series (Ling, 24 Nov, a favourite: "Isetan's basement food market and the other malls' basement food halls, the refined things there — I want these written"): TOPICS.md lines D00–D10. Same FOOD BLOG rules and format rotation. Focus on WHAT'S THERE AND WHAT'S FUN, not directions (Ling: no exits or how-to-get-in; readers figure that out and can ask in the comments): 5–7 counters/items worth it (the refined or seasonal things: wagashi, fruit, bento, gift boxes, the store's own signature item) with price level; DISCOUNTS (whether and roughly when evening markdowns happen, any regular sale days); FREEBIES (free tasting, samples, small gifts with purchase, if the store or reliable sources say so); and the TOP-FLOOR restaurant floor (is there one, what kinds of food, one or two worth it). One less-known detail regulars know. Store sites and floor guides are the best sources; link them. Every D page links to the D00 hub and the station guide. Great material for Reels and FB WONDER/PLAY posts.
- FB posts for a food page: both are PLAY or WONDER posts ("Would you try this?", "Guess what this costs", the story behind one dish); food posts are our best fit for STRATEGY.md.

DEEP TEMPLATE (Ling, 23 Nov; EVERY new guide, briefs included): copy `site/guides/tenma-tenjinbashisuji-local-guide.html`. Ling: "not surface level", a real 3–5 minute read (about 700–1,100 words), quality over speed. A place may be covered again from a new angle (Ling: one place can be told more than once) — link the related guides both ways.
  Order (use the sections that fit the question; brief guides keep their A/B/C answers and verdict inside this frame):
  1. Hero + lede that ANSWERS FIRST (who should go / what to pick) and why, in 2–3 sentences; byline "About a N-minute read · facts checked <date>".
  2. "Who it suits, and who it doesn't" (`.fit` yes/no boxes).
  3. Getting there (exact lines/stations, which pass covers it).
  4. Eat like a local: named places with what to order, price level, closing day, queue tip; at least one photo (`<figure class="photo">` with alt text + caption).
  5. A half-day route with times (`ol.route`, `span.t`) when it's an area guide.
  6. Where to stay: three bases as cards, each = who it suits + one downside + a link whose text is UNIQUE and evocative ("Wake up quiet, be in Kyoto by mid-morning →"), never "See hotels near X", never the word "affiliate".
  7. A "hot" experience box (`.hotbox`) where one really exists (food walk, sell-out tickets) — check on Klook/osaka-info that it exists.
  8. "📅 Good to know before you book" (`.bookbox`): SOFT FOMO from real facts only (annual fixed dates, reservation-only spots, real closures, "most hotels offer free cancellation"); never "book now", never invented scarcity.
  9. Nearby, if you have more time; 10. ONE MORE THING; 11. one-line affiliate disclosure at the very bottom (`p.disclose`); 12. Keep planning links.
  Every <img> has descriptive alt text; hero_alt is used for og:image:alt (build does it). Photos: Pexels ids that really show that place (look first). FAQ 3–4 with direct answers.
One file per guide: `site/guides/<slug>.html`. The build picks it up automatically (home "Latest posts", /guides index, sitemap, llms.txt, JSON-LD Article + Breadcrumb + FAQPage, keywords.json).
Header block (JSON, must parse):
```
<!--meta
{"title":"…","desc":"meta description, ~150 chars","city":"Kyoto","cat":"Where to stay","tag":"coral|mint|sky|sun|lilac|pink",
 "hero":PEXELS_PHOTO_ID,"hero_alt":"…","card":"one-line summary for cards","date":"YYYY-MM-DD","keyword":"UNIQUEWORD","order":0,

```
Body: copy the structure of an existing guide (e.g. `site/guides/osaka-local-markets.html`): hero → lede (answer first) → byline with check date → `band` sections (`band plain|namba|umeda|tennoji`) with `card noimg` fact cards → `tipbox` "Our suggestion" → booking CTA → "Keep planning" links.
- Images: `src="pexels:ID:WIDTH"` (Pexels photo id). Pick photos that really show that place; Tokyo photos never on Osaka pages.
- IMAGES MUST DISPLAY (Ling, 23 Nov): the build only makes `.jpeg` URLs, and some Pexels photos are PNG only (e.g. 35834190 broke a live hero). Before using any id, load `https://images.pexels.com/photos/ID/pexels-photo-ID.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=300` in the browser (new Image() onload/onerror); reject ids that fail. After deploy, check every image on each new page plus /, /guides and the city page loads (no broken images), and fix + redeploy at once if any is broken.
- Affiliate links only inside content, `rel="sponsored noopener" target="_blank"`: Booking.com search `https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=<Area>%2C+<City>` (Travelpayouts Drive script converts it), Klook search `https://www.klook.com/search/result/?query=…` for tours/passes/transfers. A page with no affiliate link is fine.
- More approved programs (Ling, 23 Nov; already active on Travelpayouts, the Drive script converts plain official links): Airalo / Yesim (Japan eSIM — https://www.airalo.com/japan-esim , https://yesim.app/), Welcome Pickups / Kiwitaxi (airport transfers — https://www.welcomepickups.com/ , https://kiwitaxi.com/), Radical Storage (luggage storage — https://radicalstorage.com/), Go City (https://gocity.com/), Tiqets (https://www.tiqets.com/). Put ONE where the reader is deciding that exact thing (eSIM in arrival guides, transfer in airport guides, storage in early-arrival / last-day guides). Booking.com, Agoda, Trip.com etc. are still under review: keep the Booking.com search links (they start earning once approved).
- HONESTY about affiliate links: they do NOT make anything cheaper for the reader (same price as booking direct). Never write "cheaper/best price/save" for a link unless the guide shows a same-date price comparison proving it. Frame them as convenience (book in English, pay in your currency, skip the ticket queue, free cancellation where the page says so).
- DESTINATION-FIRST DIRECTIONS (Ling, 24 Nov): on the website, exits/gates are always written as "Going to X? Use the Y exit" — never a list of what each exit leads to. Recommend ONE named hotel per traveller type with the reasons (near the gate you'll use, English-friendly, free extras…), and for food give the famous spot AND a niche local pick with its source. Station guides follow the STATION HUB TEMPLATE in section 8. Every guide needs something no AI summary gives (UNIQUE OR DON'T PUBLISH, section 8).
- ONE MORE THING (Ling, 23 Nov; EVERY guide, new and old). Each guide ends (before "Keep planning") with ONE extra that a quick Google search or AI answer would not give, in `<section class="wrap sec onemore">` with `<h2>One more thing: …</h2>`, 2–4 sentences + a small source line. Example: site/guides/nakazakicho-vs-umeda-hotel.html (Yamatatsu croquettes outside Nakazakicho Exit 2). Rotate the type:
  - FOOD: a place locals actually go (butcher croquettes, an old kissaten, a standing bar, the shotengai bakery). Must be backed by Tabelog / Retty / Google Maps reviews or a local tourism site; name it, say where (exit / metres), payment quirks, what to order. Say "popular with locals" only if the source says so.
  - A VIEW / SCENE: where to stand or what time to go to see something most people miss (a bridge, a rooftop, a river walk at dusk).
  - A LOCAL HABIT: how people who live there use the place (morning shotengai, sento, a neighbourhood shrine).
  - A SMART TIP: the same experience, easier or cheaper (with the number and why).
  - A LESS-DONE ACTIVITY: something few visitors do there (a workshop, a morning sento, a ferry, a hike from the station). Later (Ling, 23 Nov) these may link to a bookable GetYourGuide/Klook activity, only via approved programs; GetYourGuide is NOT approved yet, so no GetYourGuide links until Ling says so.
  HONEST TONE (Ling, 23 Nov): if it's good, just say it's good; no forced "but". Mention a downside only when reviewers really flag one that matters (e.g. "cash only", "20-minute queue at lunch", "some find it too greasy"). Attribute it ("Tabelog reviewers say…"), never invent opinions.
  It must be EVERGREEN: no dated events, festivals or limited menus (they expire). Never "locals told us" / "our favourite" (we don't claim visits). Facts checked this session; add the source to the `sources` field.
  STATUS: all existing guides got one on 23 Nov 2026 (osaka-where-to-stay is in build.py ONEMORE_OSAKA). Never reuse a place already used in another guide (grep "One more thing" site/guides/*.html). If a page somehow lacks one (grep -L onemore site/guides/*.html), add it before writing new briefs.
- Keyword: one short UPPERCASE word, unique (build asserts). It is what viewers comment.
- SEO/GEO: answer in the first paragraph, 3–4 FAQ entries with direct answers, real numbers, internal links to related guides.
- Deploy: `python3 jbs.py deploy-site "Add <slug>"` (builds, copies to main, pushes → live in ~1 min). **Batch several guides per deploy** (Cloudflare free plan ≈500 builds/month; budget ≤4 site deploys/day).
- Verify one new page in the browser after deploy.

## 2b. Facebook text posts (Ling, 23 Nov — test)
- FACEBOOK = JAPAN FIRST (Ling, 24 Nov, final; see STRATEGY.md 2–4): posts are about what makes Japan fun, special or easier at a station/area, written to be enjoyed on Facebook itself. Hotels are never the topic (we have no real hotel photos; hotel-centred posts look weak); at most one soft line like "we filtered a few good-value hotels near X on the site". Mix per STRATEGY.md 3. Posts marked "rewrite" in site/fbposts are hotel-centred and wait to be rewritten to this angle (build.py drops them from the queue until "rewrite" is removed).
- STYLE (24 Nov): write every Facebook text post to FB-STYLE.md (style profile learned from high-performing Japan-travel FB posts). - PERFORMANCE (24 Nov): before writing, read PERF.md (what hooks/topics are winning on IG/FB) and COMPETITORS.md (formats that outperform); lean on winners, keep ~20% experiments. See section 9.
For EVERY new guide, also write TWO Facebook posts as `site/fbposts/<id>.json` → `{"id":"fb-<keyword-lower>-1","guide":"<slug>","photo":<pexels id that shows that place>,"order":<next number>,"text":"…"}` (second one `-2`). build.py turns them into fb-queue.json; n8n posts the next one 6 times a day (Ling 24 Nov 16:54: 6 FB text posts + 7 Reels a day is the right total for a new Page; slots 08:10, 10:50, 13:20, 16:05, 19:10, 21:40 Malaysia time + 0–10 min at random) with its designed image. Keep at least 12 unposted posts in the queue (2 days). New posts come from the website producer (2 per new guide, ~15 guides a day); hotel-centred posts are rewritten on 27 Nov.
- Same topic and facts as the guide, but its own angle (FACT VARIETY in section 3 applies; each point gets 2–3 sentences with colour and a reason, like the Reel elaborations); may say we took our time and checked every fact against official listings (true). Example of the approved emotional style: site/fbposts/fb-tenma-1.json. Ling (23 Nov): detailed (250–400 words), English, a small team that knows the place ("we'd", "our take"), warm, specific, never salesy.
- HOOK = the first 1–2 sentences, ONE paragraph, 150–250 characters, no line break inside it. Facebook only shows this before "See more", so it must carry the payoff (a number, a surprising fact, a clear benefit) and end with 👇. Bad: "Second trip to Osaka? Stay where Osaka actually lives." Good: "We priced the same three nights on both sides of Osaka Station. Walking 12 minutes saved ¥9,474. Here's when that walk is worth it, and when it isn't 👇"
- LAYOUT: a BLANK LINE between every paragraph and every point (no stacked bullets). Section headers as one line "🚇 HOW TO GET THERE" (emoji + CAPS), no ━━━ lines. Each point = its own 1–2 sentence paragraph, starting with an emoji or 1️⃣. Link on its own line after "👉 …:". Example: site/fbposts/fb-nakazaki-1.json.
- FB FORMATS (25 Nov, project lead after research; see STRATEGY.md 3–5): short PHOTO-FIRST posts beat long text and link posts on every page we checked (a stunning photo + one line got 1.4K likes; link/long posts 1–6), and on our Page text posts reach almost no one (0–18 views) while visuals travel further. So for every guide:
  · Post 1 = PLACE CARD (short): ONE beautiful, real photo of that place (Pexels; "photo" id only, no designed text image, so the photo stays clean), and 2–4 short lines: what the place is known for or why people love it (snow, quiet mornings, a view, a food), one concrete detail, an ending that is EITHER an easy question ("Would you go in winter or autumn?", "Have you been?") OR a soft "more places like this on our page" / "follow Japan by Station for one Japan place a day" (Ling, 25 Nov; alternate). No link in the text (the link goes in the guide post); 3–5 hashtags.
  · Post 2 = the PRACTICAL post (the existing long style: hook, 2–3 options with whys, our take, link, question, designed image).
  · About 1 post in 5 may instead be GUESS WHERE (Ling, 25 Nov: rotate the style, and favour places most people don't know, not the mainstream ones). Rotate: "Where in Japan is this? 🤔" · "Most visitors never find this place. Any guesses?" · "Not Kyoto. Not Kanazawa. Where is it?" · "One clue: it's 1 hour from Osaka. Guess?" · a close-up detail first ("Guess the town from this street") · "Have you been here?" Answer after 6 blank lines ("Answer: <place> — <one line on why it's special>"). Use a well-known place at most 1 time in 3.
  Keep "order" alternating short / practical. PERF.md compares the two formats (experiment E3); shift the mix by results.
- Insider, lesser-known first: lead with the places and habits regulars know. Dotonbori / Namba / Shibuya crossing / Kabukicho only as a reference point and at most once in any 3 posts; never "the best / must-see" hype.
- CLICKS (Ling, 24 Nov): many people click from Facebook to the site, so give them more chances, with FUN ENGAGEMENT that makes them want to click, not a sales push. Right after the hook, ONE playful click line with the link, e.g. "🤔 Guess first, then check the answer 👉 <url>", "🎯 Bet you can't guess the cheapest option 👉", "👀 One of these surprised even us 👉", "🍱 Hungry yet? The spots, times and prices 👉", "📌 Save this for your trip (the full guide is here) 👉". Use guessing games, A-or-B picks, "which one would you choose?", small challenges, surprise facts; vary them every post. Then the link again at the end with its one-more-thing teaser and a fun question to answer in the comments. "Full guide" is allowed sometimes, but not as the only reason to click. No money talk, no "book now"; we are not monetizing yet: the goal is high-quality content people enjoy and want to open.
- HASHTAGS (Ling, 24 Nov): 10–15 per post on the last line, CamelCase: 2–4 for the exact place (#Tenma #Tenjinbashisuji), 2–3 for the city/region (#Osaka #OsakaTravel #Kansai), 2–3 for the topic (#WhereToStay #JapanFood #JapanTrains), then broad ones (#JapanTravel #TravelJapan #JapanTrip #JapanTravelTips #VisitJapan #JapanGuide). No unrelated tags.
- Every post ends with the guide URL (https://japanbystation.com/<slug>) introduced by its ONE MORE THING, not "full guide" (e.g. "👉 The croquette stop outside the station + the route:") and a question or "save this". No affiliate links, no "cheaper/best price", emojis as bullets only. End with the hashtag line above.
- IMAGE (Ling, 23 Nov; 25 Nov: PLACE CARD and GUESS WHERE posts use the clean Pexels photo instead, via "photo" only): the footer on fbimg.py now reads "Read more on japanbystation.com". Every PRACTICAL post gets its OWN designed image, never a plain photo and never the same photo twice. Make it with `python3 fbimg.py <photo.jpg> "<title 2–3 short lines split by |>" "<PLACE, CITY>" site/fbimg/<id>.jpg [focus 0–1]` and add `"image":"fbimg/<id>.jpg"` to the post JSON. The title is the post's promise in 3–7 words ("Walk 12 minutes, save ¥9,474?"). Get the photos like Reel footage: in the built-in browser on a pexels.com tab, fetch the chosen images (images.pexels.com …?w=1600) into ONE bundle file and download it, then split it with device_bash in Downloads/jbs_media/fb and device_stage_files it (method in section 3). Look at the finished image before using it. Mix in neon / night scenes often.
- NO FOLDER ACCESS? (Ling, 24 Nov: do this, keep running) If no Downloads folder is connected, do NOT leave posts in fbposts-pending. In the built-in browser (any pexels.com tab) render the photo as a CSS background (cover, focus via background-position) on a div 819.2x1024 CSS px, then capture it in 4 tiles with Claude_Browser__computer zoom region [0,0,480,600] scale 1, moving the div left/top by 0 / -409.6 and 0 / -512 CSS px (1 CSS px = 1.1719 zoom px on Ling's pane; check with a 100 px grid if the pane size changes). The zoom PNGs land in the session's tool-results folder; stitch them to 960x1200 (2x2 of 480x600), save to fb/src/<id>.jpg, run fbimg.py, look at a contact sheet, then put the post in site/fbposts with "image" and deploy. Example run: fb-onsen/fukuoka/chitose (24 Nov).
- Photo choice: an eye-catching, clearly Japanese Pexels photo that LOOKS at you (colour, lanterns, food, people, a famous station); it may differ from the exact place, but stay in the same region (Kansai photo for Kansai, Tokyo for Tokyo; never Mount Fuji on an Osaka post). Look at it before using it.
- Emotion: a line or two of feeling (why this moment is worth it), but never claim a stay or visit we didn't make.

## 3. Reels (approved style; do not change without Ling)
- SEASON / TIMELY REELS (Ling, 25 Nov; Tokyo Cheapo's two biggest Reels, ~7–9× their usual, were a dated list of illuminations and an "is back" event): for SEASON guides (TOPICS X lines) make list Reels: "Osaka has 2 Christmas markets this year", each spot = name + one thing that makes it special + its date range on the card ("until 25 Dec", "from 4 Dec"); dates ARE allowed on cards and in the caption for these Reels only (the no-dates rule still holds for all others). If tickets are sold (Klook/KKday) the ending can say "tickets and the full list: link in bio" (no affiliate wording, never "cheaper" unless the guide shows a verified price difference). Make them as soon as the page is live; they beat the normal queue order.
- COVER + HOOK MUST STOP THE SCROLL (Ling, 25 Nov: "make the covers more fun"): besides the STRANGER TEST, every cover title uses one of: a surprising fact ("Osaka's Christmas market is free"), a contrast ("Not Kyoto: Japan's quietest snow town"), a number ("3 Christmas markets, 1 night in Tokyo"), "most people miss…", "is back / opens this week", or a playful question ("Would you eat this at 7 a.m.?"). Max ~6 words, the city or Japan in it. Rotate the type; never the same type twice in a row. The first spoken line says the same promise.
- SCENIC REELS (25 Nov, project lead after research; replaces the rejected "life in Japan" idea): about 2 of the 7 daily Reels show ONE beautiful place and why people love it. Evidence: on Facebook a single stunning real photo + one line ("Where in Japan is this?") got 1.4K likes / 62 comments on a 401K page, while link and long-text posts on similar pages got 1–6; on IG, a small Osaka creator's best Reel (~8× median) was one visually striking temple near Osaka; our own best saves came from Reels with a clear payoff. Ling rejected phrases (pronunciation risk), habits, Japanese-only places and jobs (low engagement).
  Format: open on the most striking shot with a one-line hook in the first second ("This is what Shirakawa-go looks like under snow", "The quietest side of Kyoto at 7 a.m."), then 3 quick things that make it special (what it's known for, the best season/time, the feeling or the less-known detail: fewer people, snow, a view, a local food there), then EITHER a soft question ("Winter or autumn: when would you go?") OR "more places like this on our page / follow for one Japan place a day" (Ling, 25 Nov; alternate). 20–30 s, footage-led, few words. Ideas and done list: SCENIC-IDEAS.md.
