Who the one-day plan suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • First visits on a Kansai–Kyushu trip with one day to spare
  • JR Pass holders: train and JR ferry are both covered
  • Early risers: the museum is quietest before 8:30

Think twice

  • You want both a walk to the torii and the floating view: that needs the right tide day, or a night on the island
  • You plan to hike Mount Misen too: give Miyajima a full day
  • You hate early starts: the plan works best from 7:30

Getting to Miyajima: three ways

A · JR train + JR ferry

≈45 min · ≈¥620

Our pick

JR Sanyo Line from Hiroshima to Miyajimaguchi takes about 25–30 minutes (about ¥420), then a 5-minute walk to the pier and a 10-minute ferry (¥200) that runs about every 15 minutes. Both are covered by the JR Pass; pass holders just buy the ¥100 visitor tax at the terminal.

B · Hiroden tram, line 2

70–75 min · ¥240

Cheap, slow

A flat ¥240 fare, paid when you get off, but the trip through the city takes about 70–75 minutes. Worth it only if your hotel is near the tram line and you'd rather watch the city go by than save time.

C · Boat from Peace Park

45 min · ¥2,400

Straight from the A-Bomb Dome

The World Heritage Sea Route leaves Motoyasu Pier, 150 m south of the dome: 17 sailings a day, ¥4,400 return, with the last boat back from Miyajima at 17:30. Book by the day before; IC cards aren't accepted and the ¥100 tax is extra.

Our verdict: JR train plus JR ferry for almost everyone. The boat from Peace Park (C) wins only when you want to go straight from the museum to the island without backtracking and don't mind ¥4,400 return. The tram (B) is for slow days, not one-day plans.

The tide check that decides your afternoon

The Miyajima Tourist Association publishes a yearly tide table and puts it simply: below 100 cm you can walk out to the Great Torii, and above 250 cm the shrine and torii look like they're floating. Low tide alone isn't enough; some days it never drops below 100 cm. The table uses Hiroshima Port levels, and Miyajima runs slightly earlier. Pick the day that has both a sub-100 and an over-250 window in daylight, and plan the island around those times rather than the clock.

The shrine is open from 6:30; in late autumn it closes at 17:30, in December at 17:00, and entry is ¥300. After sunset it's lit until 23:00, but you can't go inside.

Eat like a local

  • Okonomi-mura (Hiroshima city): 23 okonomiyaki counters stacked on floors 2 to 4 of one building in Shintenchi, about 3 minutes from Hatchobori tram stop. It grew from the post-war food stalls on this square. Each counter keeps its own hours, so it suits a late dinner after the island.
  • Anagomeshi Ueno (Miyajimaguchi): conger eel over rice, the dish this coast is known for, a minute from JR Miyajimaguchi Station. The diner runs from about 10:00 to 19:00 (last order 18:30) and the bento counter from 9:00; it closes when sold out, so take a bento for the ferry if the line is long. Budget about ¥2,000–3,000.

A one-day route

  1. 7:30 Peace Memorial Museum, booked online for the early slot, then the Peace Park and A-Bomb Dome.
  2. 10:00 Tram or taxi back to Hiroshima Station, JR to Miyajimaguchi (about 25–30 minutes).
  3. 11:00 Anagomeshi bento, then a JR ferry between 9:10 and 16:10 for the route past the torii.
  4. 12:00 Itsukushima Shrine and the torii, timed to your tide window.
  5. 15:00 Omotesando shopping street and the deer.
  6. 18:30 Back in town for okonomiyaki at Okonomi-mura.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • The tide table comes first: choose your Miyajima date from the Tourist Association's table, then book the hotel around it.
  • The 7:30 museum slot needs an online reservation, as does the last hour of the day. The museum closes 30–31 December and for a period in mid-February.
  • The Peace Park boat sells out ahead: it can be booked up to the day before, not on the day online.
  • Most Hiroshima hotels on booking sites show a free-cancellation date, useful if you move the day to catch a better tide.

One more thing: the ferry that sails past the torii

Both ferries take 10 minutes and cost ¥200, but JR runs its sailings from Miyajimaguchi between 9:10 and 16:10 on a route that passes close to the Great Torii before docking. Sit or stand on the right-hand side facing forward for the view; it's the first, and often best, look at the torii, and it costs nothing extra.

Quick answers

Can you do Hiroshima and Miyajima in one day?

Yes. Start at the Peace Memorial Museum, which opens at 7:30 (book the 7:30–8:30 entry online), then take the JR Sanyo Line to Miyajimaguchi (about 25–30 minutes) and a 10-minute ferry. You'll be on the island by late morning with the afternoon for the shrine.

Is the tram or the JR train better for Miyajima?

JR for speed: about 25–30 minutes to Miyajimaguchi and covered by the JR Pass. The Hiroden tram (line 2) costs a flat ¥240 but takes about 70–75 minutes through town. Use it only if you're already near the tram line and in no hurry.

When can you walk to the Miyajima torii?

When the tide is below about 100 cm, according to the Miyajima Tourist Association. The torii looks like it's floating above about 250 cm. Check the association's tide table (it uses Hiroshima Port, and Miyajima is slightly earlier). Some days never drop below 100 cm.

Is there a fee to visit Miyajima?

Yes, a ¥100 visitor tax per person, collected with the ferry fare or IC card tap. JR Pass holders buy it separately at the terminal. Itsukushima Shrine itself costs ¥300.
