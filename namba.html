Osaka · Namba

Things to Do Around Namba

Got an afternoon in Namba? Everything here is a short walk from the station, and we give you the minutes to each.

Namba is the easiest part of Osaka to explore on foot. Leave Namba Station by Exit 14 and the canal, the food streets and the shopping arcades are all within a few minutes. Here is how it fits together.

One more thing: the lion that swallows bad luck

Ten minutes west of the Namba crowds, in a quiet residential street, is Namba Yasaka Shrine, the local guardian shrine of Namba. Its stage is a 12 m lion's head with a gaping mouth, and the lion is said to swallow evil spirits and bring luck, which is why students and shop owners come to pray here. It's free, and early morning is the quiet time to see it.

Namba Yasaka Shrine, 2-9-19 Motomachi, Naniwa-ku; about 6–10 min from Namba Station. Open daily from early morning to about 17:00, free. Sources: JR West (New Golden Route), GLTJP (supervised by the shrine), Japan Cheapo.

Quick answers

Are Namba and Dotonbori the same place?

Not quite. Namba is the wider district around Namba Station, and Dotonbori is the canal street inside it, 3–5 minutes on foot from Exit 14 of Osaka Metro Namba Station. Kuromon Market, the Shinsaibashi-suji arcade and the Doguyasuji kitchen street are all part of the same walkable area. Not sure which Namba you're at? Here are the four Namba stations and their exits.

What is Namba famous for?

Street food and neon. Namba is where you eat takoyaki along the Dotonbori canal under the giant moving signs, graze at Kuromon Market (about 150 shops, best before noon) and shop the covered Shinsaibashi-suji arcade. A quieter local favourite is Namba Yasaka Shrine, with its 12-metre lion-head stage, free to visit.

How far is Kuromon Market from Namba Station?

About 8 minutes on foot. Kuromon is a covered market of about 150 shops selling grilled seafood, wagyu skewers and fruit, and it is at its best before noon. For more to eat right by the gates, see food near Namba Station.
