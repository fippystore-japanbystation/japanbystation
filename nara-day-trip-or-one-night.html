Nara: Day Trip or One Night? Deer, Trains and a Quiet Evening

💡 The quick answer

A day trip is enough for Nara's big three: Todaiji's Great Buddha, Kasuga Taisha and the deer park are all a walk from Kintetsu-Nara Station, and in winter they close around 17:00.

Which train, what closes at five, and what you only get if you stay after the day-trip crowds go home.

A day trip is enough for Nara's big three: Todaiji's Great Buddha, Kasuga Taisha and the deer park are all a walk from Kintetsu-Nara Station, and in winter they close around 17:00. Stay one night if you want what a day trip misses: sunset from the free hillside veranda of Nigatsudo, Naramachi's old streets in the evening, and the park again the next morning, unhurried. Our pick for a first visit: Kintetsu from Osaka or Kyoto, out early, and one night if you're moving between the two cities anyway.

Who a night in Nara suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Travellers moving Kyoto → Osaka (or back): Nara sits between them on Kintetsu
  • Photographers who want sunset and early-morning light in the park
  • Anyone who'd rather walk slowly than rush four temples into one afternoon

Think twice

  • Night owls: the temples close by about 17:00 and evenings are quiet
  • Short trips with one base: a day trip from Osaka or Kyoto covers the highlights
  • Rail Pass holders on a tight budget: Kintetsu isn't covered

Getting there: Kintetsu or JR?

  • From Osaka-Namba (Kintetsu): about 40 minutes to Kintetsu-Nara, ¥680. Trains also stop at Tsuruhashi, which makes Osaka's Korean food district an easy base (see living like a local in Osaka).
  • From Osaka Station (JR): about 50 minutes on the Yamatoji Rapid to JR Nara. The new JR limited express "Mahorobago" has also linked Osaka and Nara since March 2025.
  • From Kyoto (Kintetsu): about 35 minutes by limited express (about ¥1,280 with the seat charge) or about 46 minutes by express (about ¥760). The sightseeing train Aoniyoshi takes about 33–36 minutes for ¥1,490 per person in pairs, but generally doesn't run on Thursdays.
  • From Kyoto (JR): about 45 minutes on the Miyakoji Rapid, covered by the Japan Rail Pass.

Why Kintetsu wins for most: Kintetsu-Nara is about 7 minutes' walk from Kofukuji and about 15 from Todaiji. JR Nara is a longer walk west of the park, roughly 15–20 minutes further.

Three ways to do Nara

A. Day trip: the big three, done by five

Arrive by 9:00, walk Kofukuji → the deer park → Todaiji (¥800) → Kasuga Taisha, lunch in between. From November to March the Great Buddha Hall runs 8:00–17:00 (7:30–17:30 from April to October), Kasuga Taisha's grounds 7:00–17:00 and its paid inner area 9:00–16:00 (¥700). Kofukuji's five-storey pagoda is under a long repair and covered, so don't plan your photos around it.

B. One night: sunset, Naramachi, an easy second morning

Do the day as above, but end it at Nigatsudo, the hall on the hillside above Todaiji, for sunset. Nara's own evening walking course goes from there down to Sarusawa Pond and ends at The Bar at Nara Hotel (18:00–23:00). Next morning: Isuien Garden (9:30–16:30, ¥1,200, closed Tuesdays) and Naramachi's old townhouses, then a train on to Osaka or Kyoto.

C. Nara between Kyoto and Osaka

If you're changing bases anyway, send big luggage ahead or use lockers, ride Kintetsu from Kyoto, spend the day in Nara, and carry on to Osaka-Namba in the evening on the same railway. You see Nara without an extra night or a round trip.

Our verdict: one night if you're moving between Kyoto and Osaka anyway or love photography; a day trip if you have one base and three or four nights. It changes for Rail Pass holders: then JR, and a day trip is the better use of the pass.

The deer: timing and the rules

The July 2025 count found about 1,465 deer, the most since 1953. They are wild and protected, not pets. Nara's visitor bureau asks you to feed them only the deer crackers sold in the park (¥200), one at a time, to keep bags and maps out of reach, and to open your hands to show you've run out. Stags can be aggressive, especially in autumn, and about 200 fawns are born each year at the Rokuen enclosure, where mothers and fawns stay until about mid-July; give any mother deer extra space in early summer. The bureau also runs early-morning deer-calling events at Tobihino in some seasons.

Eat like a local

  • Kakinoha-zushi at Izasa-chaya (near Todaiji): pressed sushi wrapped in persimmon leaves, Nara's signature. 11:00–17:00 (last order 16:00), irregular closing days. A good lunch between Todaiji and Kasuga.
  • Nakatanidou (5 minutes from Kintetsu-Nara): famous for its high-speed mochi pounding; a yomogi (mugwort) mochi is ¥200, cash only. Listings disagree on hours and closing days, so treat it as a daytime stop.
  • An evening drink at The Bar at Nara Hotel: 18:00–23:00 (last order 22:30), the final stop of the city's own evening walk. The hotel opened in 1909.

The one-night version, with times (winter)

  1. 9:00 Kintetsu-Nara. Kofukuji (9:00–17:00), then into the park.
  2. 10:30 Todaiji's Great Buddha Hall (¥800).
  3. 12:00 Kakinoha-zushi at Izasa-chaya.
  4. 13:30 Kasuga Taisha and its lantern paths; the inner area closes at 16:00.
  5. 16:00 Check in, then up to Nigatsudo for sunset (about 16:45–17:00 in winter).
  6. 18:00 Down past Sarusawa Pond, dinner, a nightcap at The Bar at Nara Hotel.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Lantern nights: Kasuga Taisha lights about 3,000 lanterns on 14 and 15 August and on Setsubun in February every year, and the city runs evening light-ups of Ukimido and Sarusawa Pond from July to September. Worth knowing if your dates fall on them.
  • No Aoniyoshi on Thursdays: plan the sightseeing train for another day.
  • Free cancellation: most Nara hotels offer it, so holding a room while you decide costs nothing.

Nearby, if you have more time

  • Naramachi Koshi-no-ie: a free traditional townhouse, 9:00–17:00, closed Mondays.
  • Fushimi Inari: on the JR line back to Kyoto. See Fushimi Inari: dawn, afternoon or after dark?

One more thing: sunset from Nigatsudo's veranda

Nigatsudo, the hall on the hillside above the Great Buddha, looks out over the whole city. Listings say it is open at all hours with no ticket, and the city's official evening walk starts here for the sunset and the night view. It's the reason we'd stay the night.

Quick answers

Is one day enough for Nara?

For Todaiji, Kasuga Taisha and the deer park, yes: they sit within walking distance of Kintetsu-Nara Station and close around 17:00 in winter. Stay a night if you want Nigatsudo at sunset, Naramachi in the evening, or a slower second morning for Isuien Garden and the Kofukuji museum.

Kintetsu or JR to Nara?

Kintetsu for most people: Kintetsu-Nara is about 7 minutes' walk from Kofukuji and about 15 from Todaiji, and trains from Osaka-Namba take about 40 minutes for ¥680. JR suits Japan Rail Pass holders (the pass doesn't cover Kintetsu), but JR Nara Station is a longer walk from the park.

How much are deer crackers in Nara?

¥200 for a pack. Feed the deer only these crackers, one at a time, keep bags and maps out of reach, and show empty hands when you're done. Stags can be aggressive in autumn, and mothers protect their fawns from about mid-May to July.

What is there to do in Nara at night?

The temples themselves close by about 17:00, so evenings are quiet. Nara's official evening course climbs to Nigatsudo for sunset and the night view, walks down to Sarusawa Pond and finishes at The Bar at Nara Hotel (18:00–23:00). In summer and on a few fixed festival nights, lanterns and light-ups add more.
