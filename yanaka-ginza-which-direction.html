Yanaka Ginza: Which Way Should You Walk With Your Parents?

You want an easy browse through old-town Tokyo without ending the outing on a staircase. The direction you walk makes the difference.

From NipporiFrom SendagiStep-free

Start at JR Nippori and finish at Sendagi. From Nippori you reach the Yuyake Dandan steps first and walk down them into the shopping street. GO TOKYO calls them the staircase "leading down" to the street. Then stroll the 170 m street and carry on to Tokyo Metro Sendagi Station, which has a lift down to the platform. If you walk the other way, the same steps come at the very end, going up. If anyone in your group should avoid stairs completely, start and finish at Sendagi and turn round before the steps.

Routes and facilities from the Yanaka Ginza association, GO TOKYO, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, Tokyo Metro and Toei · checked 23 November 2026

Answer A · Start at Nippori

Down the steps first, then the whole street

about 5 min to the street

Leave JR Nippori on the west side. GO TOKYO says to take the North West Exit and follow the main road along the edge of Yanaka Cemetery towards Yanaka Ginza. The association puts the street 5 minutes on foot from Nippori. The descent starts at Yuyake Dandan, the staircase at the top of the street. GO TOKYO says the shops begin about 1 minute below it. One thing to watch: the Tokyo government's accessible-route guide notes that the street at the top of Yuyake Dandan slopes toward the stairs, so take the first step slowly. At the bottom, Yanaka Ginza runs for about 170 m with about 60 shops. Keep walking straight to Yomise-dori and turn towards Sendagi Station. The association puts Sendagi 5 minutes from the street too.

Steps

Down

At the start, not the end

Yuyake Dandan sits between the Nippori side and the street. The top of the steps slopes towards them.

Suits: parents who can manage one flight of outdoor steps going down, and anyone who wants the classic view from the top first. Not for: wheelchairs, strollers, or anyone who can't use stairs at all. See Answer C.

Answer B · Start at Sendagi

The same street in reverse ends with a climb

steps come last

Starting at Tokyo Metro Sendagi (Chiyoda Line) is easy at first. GO TOKYO lists the Dokanyama-side exit as 3 minutes from the street, and the association says 5 minutes. You walk along Yomise-dori and enter Yanaka Ginza from its lower end. The catch is at the far end. To get to Nippori from there you go up Yuyake Dandan, with tired legs and shopping bags. That climb is the end of the outing this brief is trying to avoid. Reverse order makes sense only if you plan to come back the same way to Sendagi, or if lunch near Sendagi is fixed for the start of the day.

Suits: an out-and-back visit from Sendagi, or a group that wants to finish at JR Nippori for the Yamanote or Keisei lines and doesn't mind the climb. Not for: parents who tire easily at the end of a walk.

Answer C · Step-free

Sendagi lift, Yomise-dori, turn round before the steps

no stairs

For a stroller, a wheelchair or anyone avoiding stairs, use Sendagi in both directions. Tokyo Metro lists a lift from platforms 1 and 2 to a dedicated lift gate. The Tokyo government's accessible-route guide places it between Exits 1 and 2, and the ecomo barrier-free guide places it near the Dangozaka-shita bus stop. From there, walk Yomise-dori into Yanaka Ginza, browse to the foot of Yuyake Dandan, and come back the same way. The government guide warns that Yomise-dori has no sidewalk, so keep to the side. Its alternative from Nippori starts at the East Exit, which JR serves with a lift from the North Gate. That route passes Kannonji Temple and Yanaka Cemetery, where the road down to Yomise-dori runs downhill for about 300 m. It is longer, on narrow roads with cars. The guide also mentions the Taito City "Megurin" loop bus from Yanaka Cemetery towards Yanaka Ginza and Yomise-dori.

Suits: strollers, wheelchairs, and anyone who wants a short, flat outing that finishes at a station with a lift. Not for: seeing the view from the top of Yuyake Dandan. You see the steps from below. Our verdict

We suggest: JR Nippori → down Yuyake Dandan → Yanaka Ginza → Yomise-dori → Tokyo Metro Sendagi. Plan to sit down for lunch near Sendagi around 11:30–13:30. The answer changes if anyone can't use stairs at all. Then start and finish at Sendagi with the lift and turn round at the foot of the steps. If you must finish at Nippori for a JR or Keisei train, accept the climb, or take the longer step-free Nippori East Exit route. On Wednesdays and Thursdays our lunch pick is closed, so choose another place on the street.

One more thing: breakfast in a 1938 coffee house

Start at the Nezu end and you pass Kayaba Coffee, a kissaten (old-style coffee house) that opened in 1938 in a wooden house from the 1910s. Order the egg sandwich and the "Russian", coffee mixed with cocoa, the way regulars have for decades. There's tatami seating upstairs if your parents want to sit on the floor the old way.

Kayaba Coffee, 6-1-29 Yanaka, Taito-ku; between Nezu and Nippori stations. Opens at 8:00; hours vary by source, so check before you go. Busy at weekends. Sources: Time Out Tokyo, LIVE JAPAN, Taito Historical and Cultural Society (taireki.com).

Quick answers

Should I start Yanaka Ginza from Nippori or Sendagi?

From Nippori, if your group can manage steps going down. GO TOKYO describes Yuyake Dandan as the staircase leading down to the shopping street, so starting at Nippori puts the steps at the beginning, going downhill. Walk through the 170 m street and finish at Tokyo Metro Sendagi Station. Starting at Sendagi puts the same steps at the very end, going up.

How long is Yanaka Ginza and how far is it from the station?

The shopping street association describes it as a short street of about 170 m with about 60 shops. It says the street is 5 minutes on foot from Nippori Station and 5 minutes from Sendagi Station.

Is there a step-free way into Yanaka Ginza?

Yes. The simplest is to start and finish at Sendagi Station, which has a lift from the platform to a dedicated lift gate. Walk along Yomise-dori into Yanaka Ginza and turn back before the Yuyake Dandan steps. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government's accessible walking course also comes from the East Exit of Nippori Station via Yanaka Cemetery. It says the road from the cemetery to Yomise-dori goes downhill for about 300 m. The roads are narrow, and Yomise-dori has no sidewalk.

What time do the shops on Yanaka Ginza open?

There are no street-wide hours. The association says each shop is run separately, so opening hours and holiday closures vary by shop. GO TOKYO also lists no fixed opening hours.
