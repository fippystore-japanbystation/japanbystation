Answer A · The card everyone should have

Suica or PASMO, card or phone

no deposit for visitor cards

The card shortage is over: regular Suica and PASMO went back on sale on 1 March 2025, with a ¥500 deposit. Visitors have no-deposit options. Welcome Suica comes loaded with ¥1,000 to ¥10,000, works for 28 days, and is sold at JR East Travel Service Centers (Narita, Haneda Terminal 3, Tokyo, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ueno and more) and Welcome Suica machines at the airport stations; leftover balance is not refunded and top-ups are cash only. The TOURIST PASMO (since May 2026) is sold at airport stations, also for 28 days. On an iPhone, add Suica or PASMO directly in Apple Wallet and top up with your own card; JR East's Welcome Suica Mobile app lasts 180 days.

Answer B · Why prices differ

JR lines vs Tokyo Metro and Toei

three companies, three fares

Tokyo's trains belong to different companies, and each charges its own fare. The green JR Yamanote Line is the backbone: a loop of 30 stations taking about an hour, linking Shinjuku, Shibuya, Tokyo, Ueno and Shinagawa. Since JR East's fare revision on 14 March 2026 its minimum fare is ¥155 by IC card; Tokyo Metro and Toei start at ¥178. The same trip can differ: Shinjuku to Tokyo Station is ¥253 on JR (14 minutes) but ¥209 on the Marunouchi Line (18 minutes). Shinjuku to Asakusa is about ¥272 on Toei lines but ¥387 if you mix JR and the Ginza Line.

Answer C · The visitor pass

Tokyo Subway Ticket (24 / 48 / 72 h)

¥1,000 / ¥1,500 / ¥2,000

Prices rose on 14 March 2026 to ¥1,000, ¥1,500 and ¥2,000 (children half). It covers every Tokyo Metro and Toei subway line, not JR, and is sold to visitors from abroad and people living outside the Kanto area, at Narita and Haneda airports, Toei tourist information centres, many hotels and electronics stores, and online. The maths: the 72-hour ticket works out at about ¥667 a day. With typical subway rides of ¥209–¥272, three rides a day roughly breaks even and four clearly wins. The 24-hour ticket needs about four or five rides in a day.

Our verdict

We suggest: everyone taps an IC card; it is slightly cheaper than paper tickets and works on every line. Add the 72-hour Tokyo Subway Ticket only if you are staying on a subway line (Asakusa, Ginza, Ueno) and plan three or more subway rides a day, roughly a full sightseeing day across town. Skip it if your hotel is by a JR station and your days run along the Yamanote Line, or if you plan day trips out of Tokyo on those days.

One more thing: Tokyo's last tram

Your IC card also works on the Tokyo Sakura Tram (Toden Arakawa Line), the only one of Tokyo's old city tram lines still running. It rattles 12.2 km through backstreets between Minowabashi and Waseda, past Oji and Asukayama, for a flat fare. You tap on as you board at the front and get off at the back, and it shows you a side of Tokyo the subway never will.

Flat fare ¥170 (IC ¥168), children ¥90 (IC ¥84); Toden 1-Day Pass ¥400. 30 stops; services every few minutes. Source: Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Transportation (Toei).

Quick answers

Can tourists buy a Suica card in 2026?

Yes. Regular Suica and PASMO cards went back on sale on 1 March 2025 (¥500 deposit). Visitors can also buy Welcome Suica (no deposit, valid 28 days) at JR East Travel Service Centers and airport stations, the TOURIST PASMO at airport stations, or add Suica or PASMO to Apple Wallet on an iPhone.

Is the Tokyo Subway Ticket worth it?

Only if you ride the subway a lot. Since 14 March 2026 it costs ¥1,000 for 24 hours, ¥1,500 for 48 hours and ¥2,000 for 72 hours. Single subway rides cost ¥178–¥300 or so by IC card, so the 72-hour ticket (about ¥667 a day) pays off at roughly three typical rides a day. It does not cover JR lines such as the Yamanote.

Why does the same trip cost different amounts?

Tokyo has three main operators: JR East, Tokyo Metro and Toei, and each charges its own fare. Shinjuku to Tokyo Station is ¥253 by IC card on JR but ¥209 on the Marunouchi subway line. Changing between companies adds a second fare (Metro and Toei give a discount on joint trips).

Can I use Suica on Android?

Only on phones with Osaifu-Keitai, which in practice means phones sold in Japan, so most Android users should carry a physical card. iPhone users can add Suica or PASMO directly in Apple Wallet (Suica on iPhone 7 or later, PASMO on iPhone 8 or later). Either way, the card works on JR, subways, private railways and buses.
