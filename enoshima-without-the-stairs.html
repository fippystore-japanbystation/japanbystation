Enoshima Without the Stairs: Escalator, Boat or Stay on the Shore?

💡 The quick answer

Yes, Enoshima works for someone who walks happily but hates long stair climbs, as long as you plan it in one direction.

What the paid escalator really covers, the boat that saves the climb back, and a flat shore day with the island in view.

The three plansEatWhere to stay

Yes, Enoshima works for someone who walks happily but hates long stair climbs, as long as you plan it in one direction. Our pick: ride the Escar escalators up (¥500), walk on across the top and down to the rocks at Chigogafuchi, then take the little Benten-maru boat back to the bridge, so you never climb back over the island. If the sea is rough and the boat isn't running, keep it to the lower island and the shore instead.

Who this plan suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • Parents or partners who can walk an hour on the flat but tire on long stairways
  • A half day from Tokyo with sea views and a seafood lunch
  • Clear winter days, when Mount Fuji shows best from the coast

Think twice

  • Wheelchairs and strollers on the island itself: the Escar asks you to fold strollers, and the way down is stairs
  • Anyone who can't manage stairs going down either: stick to the shore plan
  • Stormy days: the boat stops and the island's back side is all on foot

Getting there from Tokyo

Take Odakyu from Shinjuku to Katase-Enoshima. The Enoshima-Kamakura Freepass (¥1,640 for adults from Shinjuku) covers the round trip plus a day of Odakyu local-area and Enoden rides. Add the Romancecar for a reserved seat (¥750 each way; sample trains take about 61–69 minutes). From Katase-Enoshima it is about a 5-minute walk to the start of Benten Bridge, a flat 389 m walkway to the island. Katase-Enoshima Station is all on one level, which helps. The Enoden's Enoshima Station and the Shonan Monorail are each about 10 minutes further away.

Three ways to do Enoshima with fewer stairs

A. Lower island only: the bridge, the shops, a long lunch

Walk the bridge and stroll the shop street at the bottom of the island. This is where most of the seafood restaurants are. Make lunch the goal, then turn back at the foot of the shrine steps. You get the sea on both sides, Fuji on clear days from the bridge, and no climbing at all. The downside: you miss the views from the top and the caves on the far side.

B. Upper island with the Escar (and the boat back)

The Escar is a set of three paid escalators: the first stops near Hetsunomiya shrine, the second near Nakatsunomiya, the third at the Samuel Cocking Garden and the Sea Candle lookout tower. All three cost ¥500 for adults (¥250 children), 8:50 to 19:05, and replace stone steps that take about 20 minutes to climb. The catch the signs don't shout about: the Escar only goes up. Coming down is by stairs.

So plan it one way. From the garden, the path on to Okutsunomiya and down to the rocks at Chigogafuchi is on foot, with stone steps and no escalator; the Iwaya Caves (¥500) are close by. From Chigogafuchi the Benten-maru boat runs back to the foot of Benten Bridge in about 10 minutes, about ¥400 for adults, cash. Kanagawa Prefecture's own tourism page suggests it for the way back to save energy. It has no timetable and stops in rough seas, so phone 0466-22-4141 before 10:00 on the day.

C. Stay on the shore: flat, with the island in view

Kanagawa's tourism board checked the route from Katase-Enoshima Station to Enoshima Aquarium and found it usable by powered wheelchair, with only slight curbs and slopes. Pair the aquarium (¥2,800 adults, 9:00 or 10:00 to 17:00 depending on the season, ¥200 off with your rail ticket) with a walk along Katase West Beach, one of the official Fuji viewpoints.

Our verdict: plan B with the boat, on a calm day, for most people who simply dislike climbing. Choose A if going down long stairways is also hard on the knees. Choose C for wheelchairs, strollers, or a rough-sea day.

Eat like a local: shirasu, near the bridge

Shirasu, tiny whitebait, is the local catch here, served on rice raw or boiled.

  • Tobicchi Honten (lower island, right after the bridge): 11:00–21:00 (last order 20:00), no fixed closing day, about ¥1,000–3,000 a head. Tabelog reviewers order the shirasu-don set (about ¥1,490). On weekends and holidays it hands out numbered tickets before opening. No credit cards. Reviewers note raw shirasu is off the menu from about January to mid-March.
  • Enoshima-tei (upper island): sea-view tables, cards accepted, no reservations, open to 17:30 on weekdays and 19:00 at weekends. A good reason to sit down after the Escar.
  • Enoshima Koya (mainland, 3 minutes from Katase-Enoshima): 8:00–20:00 (last order 19:00), cards accepted. Listings name the makanai-don, fish namero with sesame sauce, as the dish to order. Handy for plan C, and for an early lunch.

A stair-light half day (plan B)

  1. 9:30 Phone the Benten-maru (0466-22-4141) to check it is running today. If not, switch to plan A or C.
  2. 10:30 Arrive at Katase-Enoshima, walk the 389 m bridge, browse the shop street.
  3. 11:15 Escar up all three sections (¥500). The garden is free until 17:00; the Sea Candle is ¥800.
  4. 12:30 Lunch with a view at the top.
  5. 13:30 On foot to Okutsunomiya and down to Chigogafuchi; the Iwaya Caves if you have the energy.
  6. 14:30 Benten-maru back to the bridge, then the train home, or the Enoden to Kamakura.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Winter is Fuji season: Fujisawa's tourism site calls winter the clearest time for Fuji from Enoshima, and lists "Diamond Fuji" sunsets around early April and early September.
  • Winter illumination: the Sea Candle garden's "Shonan no Hoseki" lights ran 22 November 2025 to 28 February 2026; the garden charges ¥500 after 17:00. Check this season's dates.
  • Romancecar front seats: the panoramic seats on GSE trains sell out first. Most hotels offer free cancellation if you want to lock in a weekend.

Nearby, if you have more time

  • Kamakura: the Enoden runs on to the Great Buddha and Hasedera. Our Kamakura from noon guide times it.
  • Hakone: the other classic Odakyu trip. See Gora to Lake Ashi: bus or ropeway?

One more thing: the flat Fuji view most people walk past

Fujisawa's tourism office lists the breakwater promenade at Katase Fishing Port, on the mainland just west of the bridge, as a Fuji viewpoint. It is flat and away from the island crowds, and it looks across the water toward the mountain on clear winter afternoons. It's a good last stop for anyone whose legs are done for the day.

Quick answers

Is Enoshima OK for someone who doesn't like stairs?

Yes, with a plan. The bridge and the shopping street at the bottom of the island are walkable, the paid Escar escalators (¥500) carry you up to the Samuel Cocking Garden, and the Benten-maru boat can bring you back from Chigogafuchi so you don't climb back over the island. The Escar only goes up, so without the boat you come down by stairs.

Does the Enoshima Escar go down?

No. The Escar runs upwards only, in three sections to the Samuel Cocking Garden and Sea Candle. The way down is by stairs. It costs ¥500 for adults and runs 8:50 to 19:05.

How do I avoid the climb back from Iwaya Caves?

Take the Benten-maru boat from Chigogafuchi back to the foot of Benten Bridge, about 10 minutes and about ¥400 for adults. It has no timetable and stops in rough seas, so phone 0466-22-4141 before 10:00 on the day to check it is running.

How do I get to Enoshima from Shinjuku?

Odakyu to Katase-Enoshima. The Enoshima-Kamakura Freepass (¥1,640 from Shinjuku) covers the round trip and the Enoden for a day; the Romancecar takes about 61–69 minutes and adds a ¥750 reserved-seat charge each way.
