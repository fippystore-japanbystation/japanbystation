Shinjuku, Tokyo · Things to do

Two Hours Before Dinner in Shinjuku: Garden, Skyline or Side Streets?

The clock decides this one. Check when your two hours start before you pick.

Shinjuku GyoenFree observatoryShinjuku-sanchome

From October to mid-March Shinjuku Gyoen stops letting people in at 16:00 and closes at 16:30, so the garden only works if your two hours start by about 14:00. Later than that, take the free Tokyo Metropolitan Government observatory, open to 22:00. If it rains, stay in Shinjuku-sanchome, where a big bookstore connects straight to the subway.

Checked 23 November 2026 · hours from the Ministry of the Environment, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and GO TOKYO

Answer A · For an early start

Shinjuku Gyoen

last entry 16:00 (Oct to mid-Mar)

Enter at Shinjuku Gate, a 5-minute walk from Shinjuku-sanchome Exit E5 or 10 minutes from the South Exit of Shinjuku Station. The autumn leaves usually peak from late November to early December, and the best viewpoints are close to that gate: the Japanese Garden for maples, and the Formal Garden with its plane trees. We suggest staying at the Shinjuku Gate end rather than crossing the whole garden, so you are not rushing to leave at 16:30.

Suits: a free afternoon that starts by about 14:00, on a dry day that isn't a Monday. Not for: a late afternoon in winter.

Answer B · For the skyline, any time until 21:30

Tokyo Metropolitan Government observatory

free · 202 m

Allow about an hour: roughly 10 minutes on foot from the JR Shinjuku West Gate, 20 to 30 minutes in the lift queue (the official estimate), time at the windows, and the walk back. After 17:30 only the South Observatory is open, except on days the South one is closed. When it gets dark, the building's free TOKYO Night & Light projection plays on the outside wall until 21:45, so you can watch it on your way to dinner.

South Observatory

9:30–22:00

Last entry 21:30

Closed 1st and 3rd Tuesday. North closes 17:30 and is closed 2nd and 4th Monday.

Plan for

≈1 hr

Suits: a late afternoon or early evening, dinner on the west side, and anyone who wants sunset and city lights for free. Not for: a rainy or very hazy day, when there's not much to see.

Answer C · For an unhurried walk, and for bad weather

Shinjuku-sanchome side streets

3 min from JR East Exit

Walk out of the JR East Exit and head along the east side toward Shinjuku-sanchome. The one stop we'd make is Kinokuniya Books Shinjuku Main Store, about 3 minutes from the JR East Exit and connected directly to Exit B7 of Shinjuku-sanchome Station. It is open 10:00 to 21:00 every day, so you can browse right up to dinner. If the rain starts, use the subway concourse and come up close to your restaurant.

Kinokuniya

10:00–21:00

Open every day

3-17-7 Shinjuku. Direct from Shinjuku-sanchome Exit B7.

Suits: rainy days, a dinner booking on the east side, and anyone who'd rather wander than queue.

Our verdict

We suggest: if your two hours start by 14:00 on a dry day, Tuesday to Sunday, take Shinjuku Gyoen, especially during the autumn leaves in late November and early December. If they start later, or it's a Monday, go up the free observatory and stay for the projection after dark. If it rains, stick to Shinjuku-sanchome and Kinokuniya. From mid-March the garden stays open until 18:00, so then it also works for a 15:30 start.

One more thing: the shrine behind Golden Gai

Between the side streets and dinner, slip into Hanazono Shrine, Shinjuku's guardian shrine, which was already old in the Edo period. Its red gates sit between Yasukuni-dori and the back of Golden Gai, so you walk from neon into lanterns and quiet in a few steps. It's dedicated to Inari, the deity of business, and entertainers have long come here to pray for success.

Hanazono Shrine, 5-17-3 Shinjuku; next to Exit E2 of Shinjuku-sanchome Station. Grounds open 24 hours, free. Sources: Time Out Tokyo, GLTJP, Tokyo Cheapo.

Quick answers

What time does Shinjuku Gyoen close?

From 1 October to 14 March the garden is open 9:00 to 16:30 with last entry at 16:00. From 15 March to 30 September it closes at 18:00 (last entry 17:30), and from 11 June to 31 July at 19:00. It is closed on Mondays (or the next weekday if Monday is a holiday) and from 29 December to 3 January. Adult entry is ¥500.

Is the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatory free?

Yes. Both 45th-floor observatories are free, 202 m up. The South Observatory is open 9:30 to 22:00 (last entry 21:30); the North Observatory closes at 17:30 unless the South one is closed that day. The South Observatory is closed on the 1st and 3rd Tuesday, the North on the 2nd and 4th Monday. The official site says to expect a 20 to 30 minute wait for the lift.

How long is the walk from Shinjuku Station to the observatory?

The Tokyo Metropolitan Government says about 10 minutes from the JR Shinjuku West Gate. Tochomae Station on the Oedo Line is right under the building.

What should I do in Shinjuku if it rains?

Stay on the east side around Shinjuku-sanchome. Kinokuniya Books Shinjuku Main Store (10:00 to 21:00, open every day) connects directly to Exit B7 of Shinjuku-sanchome Station, so you can browse until dinner without an umbrella.
