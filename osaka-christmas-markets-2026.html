Tenshiba, Tennoji: the big free one

The Christmas Market in Osaka Tenshiba fills the lawn and the Tenshiba i:na area at the entrance of Tennoji Park, 20 November–25 December. Hours: 11:00–21:00 until 18 December, 11:00–21:30 from 19 December, last orders 30 minutes before. It goes ahead in rain (there are some tent seats) and closes only in severe weather.

  • Getting there: a short walk from Osaka Metro Tennoji (Midosuji and Tanimachi lines), JR Tennoji, Kintetsu Osaka-Abenobashi and the Hankai tram's Tennoji-ekimae stop.
  • The mug (¥1,000): when you order a hot drink, choose a paper cup or the 2026 mug. In the mug every hot drink is ¥100 cheaper, and refills cost only the drink. A two-mug set is ¥1,900 pre-ordered online (¥2,000 at the venue); stocks are limited.
  • Paying: every stall takes cash and many take only cash. No cash? The information booth sells meal tickets by card or e-money that work at all food and shop stalls.
  • Santa: the Santa House at Tenshiba i:na is a free photo spot; on set days in late November and December Santa is in (photos ¥1,500 each).

What to eat and drink (2026 stall list)

Fifteen food and drink stalls, each with a theme. Our short list from the official menu:

  • Glühwein: Glühwein imported from Germany at Christmas café & Bar (¥800), honey-and-apple Glühwein at MAISEL & FRIENDS (¥800), mixed-berry Glühwein at Lecker Glühwein Haus (¥900), which pours six different kinds.
  • Something different: Hiroshima oysters at Berlin Haus: oyster cream risotto (¥1,500) or oyster-and-mushroom ajillo (¥1,300).
  • Warming: beef goulash (¥1,400) or goulash in a bread bowl (¥1,600) at Gulasch Pizza Haus; an Angus beef pie (¥1,100) at Köln Haus.
  • To share: München Haus's four-sausage platter (¥2,400), or a Paulaner Weissbier (¥1,300).
  • Sweet: Santa, reindeer and snowman crêpes (¥1,500 each), Christmas churros (¥900), and a Christmas French toast (¥1,400) at Santa's Markt Kitchen. Brezeria sells German-baked butter pretzels (¥600) to take home.

Quietest time: weekday afternoons. You can't reserve seats and saving tables with bags isn't allowed, so on December weekend evenings come by late afternoon.

Umeda Sky Building: the tree and the light shows

This year's theme is THE STARLIGHT TOWN, the story of a black cat called Noël. The Wonder Christmas Marche fills Wonder Square, the plaza at the foot of the building, 13 November–25 December: stalls 12:00–22:00 (last orders 21:30), tree lit 12:00–23:00. Entry is free. JR Osaka is about 7 minutes on foot.

  • The tree and its shows: about 25 m and about 130,000 LEDs, one of Japan's largest LED-mapping trees. From 1 to 25 December the main 6-minute story show runs on the hour and half hour, 17:00–21:30.
  • What to eat and drink: ten huts, A to J. Champagne and small plates at Jacquart Champagne Terrace; the official Glühwein & Wurst Haus (house Glühwein, sausages, pretzels, sugared almonds); a long waffle "tree" and smoked turkey at M's Kitchen; a cheese soup in bread at Cheese Melt House; a 40 cm churro and a fresh-chocolate hot chocolate at Schick, which also bakes 100 doughnut pretzels a day, first come, first served.
  • The mug: ¥900 each, in mint (the town by day) or grey (by night), sold at the market service centre.
  • Passes: Trial Pass ¥1,600 (a Glühwein and a sausage); Enjoy-It-All Pass ¥3,200 for one or ¥4,600 for two (adds both mugs); Premium Pass ¥5,000 / ¥8,000 (adds the Kuchu Teien observatory). Collect them at booth C.

Hankyu Umeda: the indoor one

Hankyu's flagship store turns its 9th-floor event hall and Festival Square (Shukusai Hiroba) into a market from 18 November, themed "Tie the Magic". About 120 brands take part, led by Käthe Wohlfahrt with wooden ornaments and advent calendars from its Rothenburg shop, plus European sweets, eat-in dishes and character-shaped gelato made only for the market.

  • The tree: an 8 m mirror-ball tree with a light-and-music show on the hour, 11:00–19:00, and a sweet scent sprayed over the hall during each show. The last show on 25 December is at 16:00.
  • Merry-go-round: ¥350 a ride, by time slot on eplus only (not sold in the store).
  • Santa from Rovaniemi: 19–20 December at 11:00, 15:00 and 17:00, ¥2,200 per group, booked on eplus. There's also an icing-cookie workshop most days.
  • Mind the dates: the event hall closes for good at 17:00 on 20 December and partly closes on 1 December.
  • Getting there: about 2 minutes from Midosuji Line Umeda, 3 from Hankyu Osaka-umeda or JR Osaka's Midosuji gate.

Which one is for you

Our picks

  • The full market night: Tenshiba. Free, the most stalls, and the lights with Abeno Harukas behind them.
  • One evening near Osaka Station: Umeda Sky Building, timed for the 17:00–21:30 shows in December.
  • Small children or rain: Hankyu Umeda, indoors, with the merry-go-round.
  • Ornament shopping: Hankyu (Käthe Wohlfahrt and about 120 brands).

Think twice

  • Hankyu after 20 December: only the Festival Square part is left.
  • Tenshiba without cash: get meal tickets at the information booth first.
  • Umeda Sky passes: the Trial Pass is just a drink and a sausage; buy one only if that's what you'd order anyway.

One December evening, two markets

  1. 15:00 Tenshiba in daylight: walk the stalls, buy the mug and a Glühwein while tables are easier to find.
  2. 16:30 Midosuji Line from Tennoji to Umeda, one straight ride north.
  3. 17:00 Hankyu Umeda 9F for ornaments and the mirror-ball tree (last show of the day at 19:00; event hall until 20 Dec).
  4. 18:30 Walk to Umeda Sky Building (about 7 minutes from JR Osaka) and catch a light show; they run every half hour until 21:30.
  5. 20:30 Dinner in Takimi-koji downstairs, or up to the observatory if you still have energy.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • All three end on 25 December; Hankyu's event hall ends earlier, on 20 December.
  • Hankyu's merry-go-round and Santa are sold only on eplus, by time slot; Santa is there only on 19–20 December.
  • Tenshiba's mug sets and workshops are pre-sold on KKday in limited numbers (linked from the official site).
  • Most hotels offer free cancellation, so holding a December room near Tennoji or Umeda costs nothing if plans change.

One more thing: your market receipt works upstairs

Keep the Tenshiba receipt. A same-day market receipt of ¥1,000 or more (workshops excluded) gets you 10% off the Abeno Harukas observatory next door, and ¥100 off the Abeno Harukas Art Museum. It works the other way too: buy an observatory ticket at the 16th-floor counter and you get a ¥100 voucher to spend at the market. Glühwein first, then the city lights from the top.

Quick answers

Is the Osaka Christmas market free in 2026?

Yes. The Christmas Market in Osaka Tenshiba (Tennoji Park, 20 Nov–25 Dec 2026) is free to enter; you pay for food, drinks and the mug. The Wonder Christmas Marche at Umeda Sky Building and Hankyu Umeda's 9th-floor market are also free to walk into.

How much is the Tenshiba Christmas market mug?

¥1,000 in 2026. Hot drinks bought in the mug are ¥100 cheaper than in a paper cup, and refills cost only the drink (still ¥100 off). A two-mug set is ¥1,900 when pre-ordered online instead of ¥2,000, and you collect it at the information booth.

Can I pay by card at Osaka's Christmas markets?

At Tenshiba every stall takes cash, but many take only cash; the information booth sells meal tickets by card or e-money that work at every food and shop stall. Hankyu is a department store, so cards are fine there.

What are the opening hours of the Tenshiba Christmas market?

11:00–21:00 from 21 November to 18 December, and 11:00–21:30 from 19 to 25 December (opening day 20 November: 16:00–21:00). Last orders are 30 minutes before closing. It goes ahead in rain but closes in severe weather.
