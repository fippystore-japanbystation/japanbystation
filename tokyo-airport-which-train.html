Tokyo · Getting there

Narita or Haneda: Which Train Gets You to Your Tokyo Hotel Fastest?

Pick the line by where you sleep, not by which train sounds fastest.

HanedaNaritaBus or taxi

From Haneda, take Keikyu if you stay in Shinagawa, Asakusa or Yokohama, and the Tokyo Monorail for Hamamatsucho, Tokyo Station and the east side. From Narita, take the Skyliner (about ¥2,470) for Ueno and Asakusa, and the Narita Express (¥3,140–3,330) for Tokyo Station, Shibuya and Shinjuku. The Limousine Bus wins with heavy bags to a hotel it stops at, and after the last train.

Answer A · Landing at Haneda

Keikyu or Tokyo Monorail

11–34 min to most bases

Keikyu runs from Terminal 3 to Shinagawa in 11 minutes, Yokohama in 23 and, with through trains onto the Toei Asakusa Line, to Asakusa in 34 minutes without a change (about ¥599 by IC card). For Shinjuku, change at Shinagawa to JR: about 45 minutes and ¥580 in all. The last train toward Shinagawa leaves Terminal 3 at 00:08 and the first at 05:26.

The Tokyo Monorail reaches Hamamatsucho in 13 minutes on the fastest service (¥519 by IC card), then it is about 7 minutes to Tokyo Station on the JR Yamanote Line. On weekends and holidays its Monorail & Yamanote Line Discount Ticket covers any Haneda terminal to any Yamanote station for ¥590.

Answer B · Landing at Narita

Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner

¥2,470–3,330

The Skyliner runs from Terminal 2·3 to Nippori in about 36–50 minutes and Keisei Ueno in about 41–57 minutes (Terminal 1 adds about 3 minutes), for ¥2,470. Ueno is 5 minutes from Asakusa on the Ginza Line. Keisei also sells a discounted Skyliner ticket online for visitors. Heading to Asakusa directly with no reserved seat? The Access Express runs there without a change for about ¥1,276 by IC card, but slower.

The Narita Express (N'EX) reaches Tokyo Station in 53 minutes at its fastest (¥3,140 reserved), then continues to Shinagawa, Shibuya and Shinjuku (¥3,330). Visitors with a non-Japanese passport can buy the N'EX Tokyo Round Trip Ticket: ¥5,200 for the trip in and out within 14 days, including JR trains in the Tokyo area to your hotel station.

Answer C · Big bags, late arrivals, groups

Airport Limousine Bus, or a taxi

door drop-off

The Limousine Bus stops at the doors of big hotels, so there are no stairs or transfers with luggage. Haneda to Shinjuku is ¥1,400 and about 40–55 minutes in daytime traffic, with stops at hotels such as Keio Plaza, Hilton Tokyo and Park Hyatt; the last regular bus leaves Terminal 3 at 23:40. After the trains stop, late-night buses leave Terminal 3 at 00:20, 01:00, 01:40 and 02:20 for Shinjuku and Ikebukuro (¥2,800). From Narita the bus to Shinjuku costs ¥3,600 and takes about 2 hours, so it only beats the train if your hotel is on its list.

Taxis: Haneda has fixed fares to central wards, about ¥8,300 to Shinjuku in the daytime plus tolls and more from 22:00 to 5:00, which is reasonable for three or four people after midnight. Narita's fixed fares are ¥29,000–32,500 to central Tokyo plus tolls, a group-only choice.

Suits: travellers with large suitcases whose hotel is a bus stop, and anyone landing at Haneda after midnight. Not for Narita arrivals in a hurry: the bus is slower than N'EX or Skyliner.

Our verdict

We suggest: fly into Haneda if you can, because Keikyu or the Monorail gets you to most bases in under 45 minutes for under ¥600. From Narita, pair the line with the area in our first-trip area guide: Skyliner for Asakusa and Ueno, N'EX for Tokyo Station and Shinjuku (round trip ticket if you also fly out of Narita). Take the Limousine Bus when your hotel is on its route with heavy bags, and after 00:08 at Haneda; a taxi only makes sense at night with three or four people sharing.

One more thing: a temple town 10 minutes from Narita

If you land at Narita with a few hours to spare, or you're flying out late, ride one or two stops to Narita Station instead of going straight to Tokyo. From there, an 800 m street of old wooden shops grilling eel leads to Naritasan Shinshoji, a temple founded in 940. Have unaju (eel on rice) on the way; it is what this town is known for.

About 10–15 min by Keisei or JR from the airport to Keisei-Narita / JR Narita, then about 10 min on foot along Omotesando. The temple is free. Sources: JAL Guide to Japan, JNTO (japan.travel), All About Japan.

Quick answers

What is the fastest way from Haneda to Shinjuku?

By train, Keikyu to Shinagawa (11 minutes from Terminal 3) and then JR, about 45 minutes and about ¥580 by IC card. The Limousine Bus (¥1,400) takes about 40–55 minutes in daytime traffic but stops at major Shinjuku hotel doors.

Narita Express or Skyliner?

Match it to your hotel. The Skyliner is quicker to Ueno and Nippori (about 41–57 minutes to Ueno, ¥2,470). The Narita Express goes to Tokyo Station (fastest 53 minutes, ¥3,140) and on to Shibuya and Shinjuku (¥3,330). Visitors with a non-Japanese passport can buy the N'EX Tokyo Round Trip Ticket for ¥5,200.

What if I land at Haneda after midnight?

The last Keikyu train toward Shinagawa leaves Terminal 3 at 00:08. After that, late-night Limousine Buses leave Terminal 3 at 00:20, 01:00, 01:40 and 02:20 for Shinjuku and Ikebukuro (¥2,800), or take a taxi.

How much is a taxi from Narita to Tokyo?

Narita's fixed taxi fares to central Tokyo run from ¥29,000 (Chiyoda, Chuo, Taito) to ¥32,500 (Shinjuku, Shibuya, Minato), plus tolls, with a surcharge from 22:00 to 5:00. It only makes sense for a group sharing, or very late.
