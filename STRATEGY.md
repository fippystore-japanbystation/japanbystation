# Japan by Station: strategy (North Star)

Owner: Ling. Project lead: Claude (Ling, 24 Nov: "you are head of the Japan by Station project — plan and think through the whole logic").
EVERY producer (website, Reels, Facebook, Pinterest, SEO, performance) reads this file first. When a detailed rule in PLAYBOOK.md conflicts with this file, this file wins. Ling's words are the direction, not a script to follow 100%.

## 1. The goal
Reach many people and earn **followers and likes** by giving **unique, informative details** about Japan, **without pushing**.
- First: follows + engagement (likes, comments, saves, shares) on Instagram and Facebook.
- Second: visits to japanbystation.com. Nice to have, never forced.
- Not now: selling. No "book now", no hard sell, no hotel promotion on social.

## 2. Who does what
| Channel | Job | Hotels? |
|---|---|---|
| Reels (IG + FB) | Show why Japan is fun and how to do it well, in 30–45 s. Reach + follows. | Minor role only: the area, a room type, value by area. Never a video about one hotel. |
| Facebook text posts | A small, specific, interesting thing about Japan people enjoy reading and reacting to. Engagement + follows. | Not the topic. At most one soft line: "we filtered a few good-value hotels near X on the site". We have no real hotel photos, so hotel-centred posts look weak. |
| Website | The depth: full guides, hotel shortlists, activities, maps, prices, sources. | Yes. This is where hotel picks live. |
| Pinterest | Long-life search traffic to the website. | Guides of any kind. |

## 3. What a social post is about (content mix, Facebook + Reels)
Lead with **Japan itself**: what's fun, special, surprising or useful, at a specific station or area.
- ~40% WONDER: a unique local detail most visitors miss (a market where they cook the fish you buy, a torii that only floats at high tide, a food only one area does well).
- ~30% SMART TRAVEL: one practical thing that makes the day easier (which train, which exit, last bus, luggage trick, a mistake to avoid).
- ~20% PLAY: easy engagement: A-or-B picks, guess the price, "which would you choose", "tag who you'd go with", "what's your must-eat in X?".
- ~10% STAY, LIGHT: the area to base yourself in and why (food, mood, transport), never a hotel sales pitch; the hotel shortlist is "on the site".
Keep ~20% of all posts as experiments; let PERF.md move the mix.

## 4. How a post earns the click (without pushing)
The post must be worth reading on its own: deliver the interesting part IN the post. Then one light reason to go further: "we filtered X spots / hotels / time slots on the site", "the full route and map are here", or a guess-then-check game. Never tease the answer to force a click. No money talk.

## 5. Engagement is part of the job
- End most posts with a question people can answer in one word or a pick (A or B, which city, what would you eat first).
- Soft follow line sometimes (not every post): "Follow for one Japan station tip a day."
- Comments: reply to real comments within a few hours with something useful (plan, see Roadmap). Never argue, never spam.
- Post times are spread (n8n random delay); no bursts.

## 6. Quality bar (unchanged)
Facts from sources checked this session; no invented personal stays; evergreen topics; not always Dotonbori/Shinjuku; licensed images only (if we can't use it, we don't); clean captions.

## 7. What we measure (daily, PERF.md)
North Star: **new followers per day** (IG + FB) and **likes/reactions per post**. Then saves, shares, comments, reach. Website clicks are tracked but are not the goal.
