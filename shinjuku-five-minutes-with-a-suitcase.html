Shinjuku, Tokyo · Getting there

"Five Minutes from Shinjuku Station": How Long Is It with a Suitcase?

Shinjuku has more than one station and dozens of exits. We traced one real "5 minutes" hotel from the airport train, the subway and the airport bus.

Airport trainSubwayAirport bus

"Five minutes" starts at one exit, not at your train. Hotel Gracery Shinjuku says 5 minutes from JR Shinjuku's East Exit, but with a suitcase from the Narita Express the lift route comes out at the south gates, about 1 km and 15–20 minutes from reception. The Narita Limousine Bus to Tokyu Kabukicho Tower stops about 200 m away: slower to Shinjuku, but almost no walk.

Answer A · Narita Express, platform to reception

From the N'EX platform

≈1 km with lifts

Our sample N'EX left Narita Terminal 1 at 15:14 and reached Shinjuku at 16:41 (87 minutes, ¥3,330). It stops at platforms 5–6, and JR East's own note says the step-free route from there leads only to the New South, Koshu-kaido and Miraina Tower gates, at the south end of the station.

The hotel is at the north end, in Kabukicho. From the Busta Shinjuku side the street walk is about 970 m, roughly 13 minutes at a normal pace, before you even count the walk along the platform and through the gates. The route planner allows 12 minutes just from the JR platforms to Seibu-Shinjuku Station, which the hotel says is 3 minutes from its door. With a suitcase we would budget 15–20 minutes, then a lift: reception is on the 8th floor.

Answer B · The advertised exit, and the lift that gets you there

East Exit, or Metro Exit B11

≈450–470 m on the street

The advertised exit: from the JR East Exit plaza the street walk to the hotel is about 470 m, about 6 minutes. That is the "5 minutes", and it holds if you already stand at the East Exit.

The nearest step-free alternative: Tokyo Metro's Marunouchi Line station at Shinjuku lists a lift from the platform to its East Gate, and lifts from the East Gate up to Exits B11 and A6, from 6:00 to 23:00 only. From Exit B11 the street walk is about 450 m, along Kabukicho's Sakura-dori. Useful if you came in on the Marunouchi Line from Tokyo Station or Ginza; check which side your train arrives on, as the lift listed serves the platform towards Ikebukuro.

Answer C · When a closer bus stop is worth a slower ride

Narita Limousine to Tokyu Kabukicho Tower

¥3,600 · ≈200 m walk

The Airport Limousine runs from Narita straight to Tokyu Kabukicho Tower, which the hotel says is about a 2-minute walk away (about 190 m on the street). On our sample timetable the bus left Terminal 1 at 15:40 and was due at 17:35: 115 minutes for ¥3,600, against 87 minutes and ¥3,330 by N'EX.

So the bus saves about 800 m of walking with your bags and every lift hunt, at the cost of about 30 minutes more on board and ¥270 more, and only if a departure fits your landing. If none does, the hotel suggests the buses to Busta Shinjuku, but from there it is the same 970 m walk as the train.

Suits: big suitcases, travellers with children or limited mobility, anyone landing tired. Not for: tight schedules, or when the next bus is more than about half an hour away.

Our verdict

We suggest: before booking, check which exit the "minutes" are counted from and match it to your airport transfer. For a Kabukicho hotel like Hotel Gracery Shinjuku with a big suitcase, take the Narita Limousine to Tokyu Kabukicho Tower when a departure is within about 30 minutes of clearing customs; otherwise take the N'EX and budget 15–20 minutes from platform to reception. If your hotel is near the South Exit or Busta Shinjuku instead, the N'EX's lift route ends right there and the train wins outright.

One more thing: Memory Lane, once the bag is gone

Once the suitcase is at the hotel, walk back to the West Exit and duck into Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane). It started as a black market among the rubble around 1946 and is now a lane of tiny counters grilling yakitori and simmering nikomi stew, barely wide enough for two people. It's two minutes from the station and the best place to feel you've arrived in Tokyo.

Quick answers

What does 'five minutes from Shinjuku Station' mean?

It is measured from one exit, not from your train. Hotel Gracery Shinjuku, for example, says 5 minutes from JR Shinjuku's East Exit; the street walk from the East Exit plaza is about 470 m. From the Narita Express platform the step-free route comes out at the south gates, about 1 km away.

Which platform does the Narita Express use at Shinjuku?

Platforms 5 and 6. JR East says the step-free route from these platforms goes through the New South, Koshu-kaido and Miraina Tower gates, and you cannot change directly to most other JR platforms from them.

Is the airport bus better than the train to Shinjuku with luggage?

It depends on the stop. The Narita Limousine Bus to Tokyu Kabukicho Tower costs ¥3,600 and took 115 minutes on our sample timetable, against 87 minutes and ¥3,330 by Narita Express, but it drops you about 200 m from Kabukicho hotels instead of about 1 km.

Is there a step-free subway exit for Kabukicho?

Tokyo Metro lists lifts at its Marunouchi Line Shinjuku Station from the East Gate to Exits B11 and A6, from 6:00 to 23:00. From Exit B11 it is about 450 m on the street to Hotel Gracery Shinjuku.
