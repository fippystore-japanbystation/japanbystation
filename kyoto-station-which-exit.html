Kyoto Station has two faces. The Karasuma side (north, the Central exit) is where the city buses, Kyoto Tower, the taxi rank and the Karasuma subway are. The Hachijo side (south) is the Shinkansen side, with Kintetsu trains to Nara and the airport buses. Know which side you need before you tap out, and you skip the long walk across the building with your suitcase.

Which exit for where you're going

  • Going to Kiyomizu-dera, Gion, Kinkaku-ji or Ginkaku-ji by bus? Use the Central (Karasuma) exit. The city bus terminal sits right in front, at the foot of Kyoto Tower.
  • Going to Nara or Toji by Kintetsu? From the Shinkansen, use the Shinkansen Central exit: the Kintetsu gates are right in front. From JR local trains, use the West exit and turn left.
  • Catching the airport bus (Kansai or Itami)? Use the Hachijo exit on the south side. Highway and hotel shuttle buses leave from there too.
  • Taking the Karasuma subway from the Shinkansen? Leave by the Hachijo exit and go underground to the subway's South gate. With a big suitcase, the tourism office suggests the Underground East route instead.
  • Changing from the Shinkansen to a JR line? Use the Central transfer gate (escalators and lifts) or the East transfer gate for the Nara Line to Fushimi Inari and Uji.
The trap: the "Shinkansen Central exit" and the local-line "Central exit" are different gates on different sides. The Shinkansen one leads you south towards Kintetsu and Hachijo, not to the bus terminal.

The buses: what to know before you queue

City buses cost a flat ¥230 for adults inside the city zone. On Saturdays, Sundays and holidays the EX100 and EX101 sightseeing express buses (¥500) leave from stop D1, in front of Kyoto Tower: EX101 goes to Kiyomizu-dera (get off at Gojozaka), EX100 also stops at Gion, Heian-jingu and Ginkaku-ji. Regular routes: 86, 106 and 206 for Kiyomizu and Gion, 205 for Kinkaku-ji, 5 for Ginkaku-ji, 28 for Arashiyama. The Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass is ¥1,100.

The tourism office's own way round the crowded Kiyomizu buses: the subway two minutes to Kujo (¥220), then bus 202 or 207 to Gojozaka.

Lockers and same-day luggage delivery

  • JR Central smart lockers (Hachijo side, 1F): 72 lockers, ¥400 / ¥500 / ¥700 a day by size, cashless. Drop a bag by 11:00 and they deliver it to your Kyoto hotel around 18:00 (from ¥1,500).
  • Crosta Kyoto (B1): the hands-free travel counter; same-day delivery to your hotel if you hand the bag in by 13:00.
  • Kintetsu side: 45 coin lockers, ¥400–1,000 by size, 100-yen coins.

In peak weeks the lockers fill early, and the city asks visitors not to bring big suitcases onto buses. Sending the bag ahead is the calmer option.

Eat around the station, two layers

What most visitors go for: Kyoto Ramen Koji on the 10th floor of the station building, 11:00–22:00 (last order 21:30), with ten shops from Hokkaido to Fukuoka. From the JR West gate, walk into Isetan and take the south lift to 10F. For bento and sweets, JR Kyoto Isetan's B1 and B2 food floors and Porta's underground restaurants (11:00–22:00) are right there.

Dig a little deeper: about 5 minutes' walk east of the station, Honke Daiichi-Asahi has served Kyoto-style soy-sauce ramen since 1947. It opens in the early morning, has over 7,000 Tabelog reviews with a 3.73 score, and closes on Thursdays. Its listed hours differ between sources, so check before an early visit.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • Kansai Airport bus: ¥2,800, about 85–90 minutes, 4:30 to 21:10 from the Hachijo side. The operator says the KIX line needs a reservation.
  • EX sightseeing buses run only on weekends and holidays (plus Obon and New Year).
  • The rooftop garden closes from 27 November 2026 until about winter 2031 while the new station concourse is built.
  • Most hotels offer free cancellation, so booking a station hotel early for cherry blossom or autumn weeks costs nothing if plans change.

Nearby, if you have more time

  • Kyoto Tower: 10:00–21:00 (last entry 20:30), ¥1,000 for adults, across the road from the Central exit.
  • Toji: the five-storey pagoda, about 15 minutes' walk from the Hachijo exit.
  • Fushimi Inari: two stops on the JR Nara Line.

One more thing: the view 45 metres up

The station building's rooftop garden closes from 27 November 2026 for about five years, but the Skyway on the 10th floor stays open: a 185 m glass corridor 45 m above the concourse, open 10:00–22:00 and lit from 17:00. Walk up the 171-step Grand Staircase (lit with about 15,000 LEDs after dark) and you get Kyoto Tower and the city lights before your train.

Quick answers

Which exit at Kyoto Station is for the city buses?

The Central exit on the Karasuma (north) side. The city bus terminal is right in front of it, at the foot of Kyoto Tower. The EX100 and EX101 sightseeing express buses leave from stop D1 there.

Which side of Kyoto Station is the Shinkansen on?

The Hachijo (south) side. The Kintetsu line to Nara is right in front of the Shinkansen Central exit, and airport limousine buses leave from the Hachijo exit. Note that the local-line 'Central exit' and the 'Shinkansen Central exit' are different gates.

How do I get from Kyoto Station to Kansai Airport by bus?

Airport limousine buses leave from the Hachijo (south) side. To Kansai Airport it costs ¥2,800 for adults and takes about 85–90 minutes; buses run from 4:30 to 21:10, and the operator says the KIX line needs a reservation.

Where can I leave luggage at Kyoto Station?

JR Central's smart lockers on the Hachijo side (from ¥400 a day, cashless) also offer same-day delivery to your hotel if you drop the bag by 11:00. The Crosta Kyoto counter (B1) takes bags for same-day hotel delivery until 13:00, and coin lockers are spread around the station.
