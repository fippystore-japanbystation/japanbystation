It's 8 a.m. in Tokyo. Who Will Take Your Suitcase?

💡 The quick answer

With an 8 a.m. arrival, book a staffed hotel whose FAQ says it keeps bags before check-in, and drop them on the way from the airport.

Why we say this ↓

Check-in is hours away. We read a hotel's luggage rules, an apartment listing and JR East's locker list to see which booking lets you sightsee hands-free.

If you land early, we suggest booking a hotel with a 24-hour front desk that says in its FAQ it keeps bags before check-in: you drop them at 8 a.m. and walk away. A self check-in apartment usually cannot take them; the one we checked opens its doors at 16:00. Then a station locker is the fallback: a large one at a JR station costs about ¥900–1,000 a day, and Ueno alone has hundreds.

Answer A · A staffed hotel

Drop the bags at the front desk

Free · 24-hour desk

Hotels with a staffed desk often state this in their FAQ. Sotetsu Fresa Inn Tokyo-Toyocho's own FAQ, as an example: the front desk is open 24 hours, and staff keep luggage before check-in and after check-out, limited to those two days. So an 8 a.m. arrival is fine; you are not asking a favour.

Read the small print on two points. The same FAQ says it cannot take luggage that is too large or in large quantities, so a family with four big cases should ask first. And it will hold parcels you courier ahead for up to one week before check-in, handy if you want to send a case on from your first stop.

Answer B · An apartment stay

Self check-in: check for a real drop-off point

Door opens 16:00

Apartments win on space (see our room-size comparison), but mornings are their weak spot. The Kinshicho flat we priced, Brillia ist Kinshi Park, uses smart check-in with an unmanned desk, check-in runs 16:00–22:00, and luggage storage is not in its facilities list. Land at 8 a.m. and that is eight hours before anyone can let you in.

Some hosts do offer a drop-off room or a partner shop. We suggest you ask in a message before booking, and only count on it if the answer names a place and its opening hours. "We'll try" is not a drop-off point.

Answer C · Station storage

A large locker where your day ends

≈¥900–1,000 · first to last train

Put the case in a locker at the station nearest to where you will sleep, so you collect it on the way in. JR East gives its usual locker prices as S ¥500, M ¥700–800 and L ¥900–1,000, available from the first to the last train. By its size guide, L fits a larger suitcase and LL a larger suitcase plus shopping.

Ueno as an example, where the Keisei airport trains from Narita end: JR East lists 21 locker sites around the station, 13 of them in JR Ueno. Outside the Shinobazu gate are 20 LL and 56 L lockers; inside, the Shinobazu gate passage has 119 L and the Park gate passage 100 L. Busy stations fill up, so JR East's Multi ecube lockers can be reserved up to a month ahead, at ¥100–200 more for M and L in busy periods.

Our verdict

We suggest: with an 8 a.m. arrival, book a staffed hotel whose FAQ says it keeps bags before check-in, and drop them on the way from the airport. It costs nothing and you collect them at the desk. Book the apartment only if the host names a drop-off point; otherwise budget about ¥1,000 for an L locker at the station nearest the flat. The answer changes with oversized or many cases (ask the hotel first, or use LL lockers), and if you arrive after about 16:00, when even the self check-in flat is open.

One more thing: send the suitcase ahead

Before you think about lockers, consider not carrying the suitcase at all. Airport counters with the Hands-Free Travel logo (JAL ABC, Yamato and others) can deliver your bag to your hotel the same day if you drop it off in the morning, so you go straight into the city with a day bag. JAL's same-day service from Haneda Terminal 3 costs ¥4,500 a bag. It delivers to hotels only and may slip to the next day at peak times.

Look for the Hands-Free Travel counters in the arrival lobbies at Narita, Haneda and Kansai. Same-day cut-off times and prices vary by company and airport. Sources: JNTO (japan.travel) Hands-Free Travel, JAL Same-day Delivery Service.

Quick answers

Will a Tokyo hotel keep my luggage before check-in?

Most staffed hotels will, but check the FAQ. Sotetsu Fresa Inn Tokyo-Toyocho, for example, says its front desk is open 24 hours and keeps bags before check-in and after check-out on those days, but not luggage that is too large or in large quantities.

Can I leave bags at a Tokyo apartment before check-in?

Often not. The flat we checked in Kinshicho has smart check-in with an unmanned desk, check-in from 16:00 to 22:00, and no luggage storage in its listed facilities. Ask the host in writing for a drop-off point before you book.

How much is a large coin locker at a Tokyo station?

JR East puts its usual locker prices at ¥500 for S, ¥700–800 for M and ¥900–1,000 for L, per its own guide to Tokyo Station lockers. Reservable Multi ecube lockers can cost ¥100–200 more for M and L in busy periods.

Where are the big lockers at Ueno Station?

JR East lists 13 locker sites inside JR Ueno. Outside the Shinobazu gate there are 20 LL and 56 L lockers; inside, the Shinobazu gate passage has 119 L lockers and the Park gate north–south passage 100 L lockers. They work from the first to the last train.
