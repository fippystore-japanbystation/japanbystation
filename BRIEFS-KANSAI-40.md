CHANNEL STYLE
Help independent travelers decide what to book, where to go, and what a place is actually like. Write in conversational English. Talk to someone planning a trip who has a real constraint: money, time, luggage, an early flight, a late dinner, or the last train home.

FORMAT FOR EACH REEL
1. Opening cover: Use the two lines provided. The slash separates line one from line two. Open on a visual that makes the question immediately clear.
2. Voiceover: Aim for 70–95 words. Start with the most useful finding. Give exactly three researched answers based on A, B, and C below. Give each answer one concrete detail and say who it suits.
3. Verdict: Finish with one practical recommendation. If the right choice changes with the time, price, or day of the week, say exactly when it changes.
4. Visuals: Suggest three shots, map views, or comparisons that help prove the answer.
5. Research notes: Supply source links and the date checked. Keep these outside the voiceover.
6. Affiliate fit: Name the specific stay, activity, rental, or transport booking that fits the recommendation. Use only supplied, approved affiliate URLs. Leave the link blank when none fits.

RESEARCH RULES
The A/B/C points below are options to investigate. Verify them before turning them into recommendations. For room prices, use the same stay dates, occupancy, and total-price basis. For routes, count the walk, transfers, waiting time, and return journey. For food, check last orders. For a quiet hotel, look for evidence about the actual street or room. If a key detail cannot be verified, flag that Reel as “needs research.” A finding such as “go earlier,” “skip the upgrade,” or “this budget only buys a capsule on these dates” is a valid answer.
