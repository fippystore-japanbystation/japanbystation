# Facebook text-post style profile (learned from high-performing Japan travel posts)
Method (Ling, 24 Nov; idea from Hao's "Social Post" skill, applied to OTHER pages, not a personal voice):
collect real FB text posts from Japan-travel pages and creators with their reactions / comments / shares, compare each post with that page's usual numbers, and extract what the winners do: first line, length, line breaks, emoji, list vs story, question, link placement, tone. Patterns only; never copy wording.
The Facebook writer (PLAYBOOK 2b) applies this profile to every new FB post. The profile is only "v0 / early" until it rests on ≥ 20 sampled posts from ≥ 5 pages.

## Profile v1 (26 Nov; 46 samples / 14 pages + our own 22 posts) — the Facebook writer uses THIS from 27 Nov
Evidence level per rule: [P] = seen on ≥ 2 pages, clearly above page usual · [E] = early, 1 page or weak · [OWN] = our page (so far only tells us about reach).
1. ONE idea per post, built on a strong photo. Almost every winner is photo (or short video) + caption; bare text and link cards lose. [P]
2. First line = the hook, and it must be one of these four (rotate them):
   a. CONTRAST that takes the reader's side against a pain: crowds/overtourism/queues → a quieter or better place. (JapanTravel 22.1K and 347; Japan Cheapo page-high) [P]
   b. NEWS YOU CAN USE / TIMELY: "is back", "starts", "this December", with what + where + when inside the first 2 sentences. (Tokyo Cheapo 2.7K/495 shares, 202/111 shares, 106; Japan Travel Tips FAQ 14 shares) [P]
   c. A SPECIFIC surprising fact or a pointed question answered at once ("did you know there's a second one?", "can a machine replace this craft?"). (JapanTravel 680, 83; Japan Objects 2×) [P]
   d. A dated seasonal LIST (spots + dates) — the most-shared format. (Tokyo Cheapo 493 shares; 14 shares) [P]
3. Sentence 2–3 delivers the practical payoff IN the post (when / where / how much / how long). No teasers. [P]
4. Length: 2–5 short paragraphs, roughly 40–120 words before any link; line breaks between paragraphs; 0–2 emoji. [E — winners are short, but length was not measured on every sample]
5. End with an easy question people can answer in a word or a pick, or invite a local tip ("where would you go instead?"). Comments come from insider alternatives. [E]
6. Link: at most one, AFTER the value, framed as "full route / dates / map here". The 70% no-link posts give the whole answer. [P for "teaser + link loses"; link placement itself unproven]
7. NEVER: generic openers ("Planning a trip to Japan?", "Ready for…?", "Discover…", "Unlock the best…"), vague teasers ("this is going to be popular"), hype ("DON'T MISS OUT"), "comment KEYWORD", selling a service or booking plug, bare URL, mood-only captions. [P — 0–7 reactions on 7 pages]
8. First-person mini-story openers: MIXED (Tokyo Cheapo 198 ≈ 4× vs JapanTravel's "one stop early" 17, below usual). Use only when the story has a concrete payoff in line 2. [E, unproven]
9. Hashtags: winners use 0–3; long hashtag blocks appear on low performers. Cut our -2 posts' 12–15 hashtag tails to ≤ 3. [E]

## Profile v0 (early, 4 samples)
1. First line = a human, specific hook (humour, news, a season "this week"), never a generic "Planning a trip to Japan?".
2. Sentence 2 gives the practical payoff (when / where / how much), so the post is worth saving or sharing.
3. Short: 2–5 short paragraphs before the link; one idea per post.
4. Practical "this week / this season" lists earn shares; insider alternatives earn comments → end with a question that invites a local tip ("Where would you go instead?").
5. Link posts with only a teaser underperform: the post itself must deliver value, the link is "more detail".

## Profile v0.1 additions (24 Nov run 2, 18 samples / 9 pages — still early)
6. "News you can use" wins shares: a rule, system, date or event that changes NOW ("is back", "starts", "just announced"), with what/where/when in the first 2 sentences (Tokyo Cheapo 106 reactions; Japan Travel Tips FAQ 14 shares). Seen on 2 pages.
7. Dead formats: bare URL with no text, one-line jokey teasers, "comment KEYWORD" lead magnets, selling a service (0–7 on 3 pages).
8. A question as the first line works when it is specific and answered right away ("What's special about…?" + answer) — 1 page, weak evidence.
