Sakai: Can You Watch a Knife Being Made Without Booking?

Sakai has made knives for professional kitchens for centuries. Here is what you can see on a weekday afternoon without planning ahead.

Free demoBook aheadWalk-in museum

Yes, if you go on a Friday. Sakai Traditional Crafts Museum holds a free sharpening demonstration by a knife craftsman every Friday from 13:30 to 15:00, with no booking. Sharpening is the step that gives a Sakai blade its edge. Watching the forging itself is different: the workshops listed by Sakai's tourism bureau ask for a booking 10 to 15 days ahead. On other weekdays, the museum's free knife exhibit and its shop of 24 Sakai makers are what you can see without planning.

Answer A · No booking, Friday afternoon

Watch a craftsman sharpen a blade

Fri 13:30–15:00 · free

The museum says a knife craftsman demonstrates sharpening (togi) right in front of visitors every Friday from 13:30 to 15:00. On Saturdays and Sundays there are re-sharpening demonstrations instead, from 11:00 to 12:00 and 13:00 to 15:00. Entry is free and you just walk in. Cancellations are posted on the museum's website, so check it on the morning you go.

Answer B · Plan ahead

A forge visit or a sharpening class

10–15 days' notice

Sakai's tourism bureau lists knife workshops that accept visitors by reservation. Sasuke, a forge run by a fifth-generation knife and scissor maker, takes bookings up to 15 days ahead (open 10:00–19:00, closed irregularly, in principle on Thursdays). Fujii Hamono Seisakusho, which does every stage from forging to fitting the handle in-house, takes bookings up to 10 days ahead and closes on Sundays. The museum also runs occasional hands-on sharpening classes. The next one listed is Thursday 24 December 2026, 14:00 to 15:30, for ¥2,000. You sharpen two of your own knives with a certified craftsman, so it suits people who travel with knives or plan to buy one first.

Answer C · Any open day

The free knife museum and shop

10:00–17:00 · free

On days without a demonstration, Sakai Traditional Crafts Museum still shows how a Sakai knife is made. Its knife museum, "Cut", covers the history, production steps and tools with models and real examples. Downstairs, the shop stocks knives from 24 Sakai makers, more than anywhere else in one place, according to the museum. Makers take turns helping on the shop floor, and English-speaking staff are on hand. If you buy a knife, the museum also re-sharpens knives from ¥1,600.

Our verdict

We suggest: go on a Friday and arrive by 13:30 for the free sharpening demonstration, then browse the exhibit and the 24-maker shop before it closes at 17:00. You don't need to book anything. If you can't make a Friday, weekend re-sharpening demos start at 11:00 and 13:00. If you want to see the forging itself, book a workshop at least two weeks before your visit. Quick answers

Can I watch a Sakai knife being made without a reservation?

Yes, one step of it. Sakai Traditional Crafts Museum (Sakai Denshokan) holds a free sharpening demonstration by a knife craftsman every Friday from 13:30 to 15:00, and re-sharpening demonstrations on Saturdays and Sundays (11:00–12:00 and 13:00–15:00). You just walk in. The museum posts on its website if a session is cancelled.

Can I visit a Sakai knife forge?

Usually only with a booking. The Sakai tourism bureau lists Sasuke (open 10:00–19:00, closed irregularly, in principle Thursdays) as taking reservations up to 15 days ahead, and Fujii Hamono Seisakusho (closed Sundays) up to 10 days ahead. Contact them well before your trip.

How do I get from Namba to Sakai Traditional Crafts Museum?

Take a Nankai Line local from Namba to Sakai Station (15 minutes, 8 stops), then walk about 1.1 km (16 minutes). Google Maps puts the whole trip at about 35 minutes and ¥290. The museum is free, open 10:00–17:00, and closed on the third Tuesday of the month and from 29 December to 3 January.

Is there a knife-sharpening class in Sakai?

The museum runs occasional sharpening classes taught by a certified Sakai knife craftsman. The next one listed is Thursday 24 December 2026, 14:00–15:30, ¥2,000 per person, 16 places, first come first served. You bring two of your own knives, an apron and a towel, and apply by email, phone or at the shop.
