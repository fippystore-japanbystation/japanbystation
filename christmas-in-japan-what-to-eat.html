Christmas in Japan tastes of strawberry shortcake: a light sponge, whipped cream and strawberries, bought from a convenience store or a department store and eaten on Christmas Eve. Around it sit Japan's own Christmas foods: Christmas karaage and bone-in roast legs of local chicken, a Christmas sushi set, and shanmeri for the kids. We suggest a small cake you can buy on the day, a jidori roast leg from a konbini or a karaage shop, and a Christmas market for the Japanese twists on Glühwein food.

Who this suits, and who it doesn't

Join in if

  • You're in Japan on 22–25 December and want to eat what families here eat.
  • You have a hotel room or rental flat to take a cake and chicken back to.
  • You like convenience-store and depachika food as much as restaurants.

Skip it if

  • You want a restaurant Christmas dinner on the night: special courses are sold by reservation.
  • You're travelling between cities on the 24th with no fridge for a cream cake.

The Christmas cake: strawberries, cream, order ahead

The Japanese Christmas cake is a light sponge with whipped cream and strawberries, the Japanese-style "shortcake". Sizes are in gō: 3-gō feeds one or two, 4-gō (about 13 cm) two to four, 5-gō (about 15 cm) four to six. Most whole cakes are sold by pre-order and collected around 19–25 December.

The most Japanese one: Lawson's matcha cake made with Morihan, the old Kyoto tea house (4-gō size, ¥4,500): green tea through every layer instead of strawberries.

The traveller's cake: from 15 December, Lawson sells 3-gō mini cakes, strawberry ¥896 and chocolate ¥950, with no reservation. It's the easy way to have a Japanese Christmas cake in a hotel room.

Department stores: the showpiece cakes

Depachika cakes are the ones people queue to pick up. Isetan Shinjuku opened its 2026 Christmas cake orders on 2 October, and several of its own cakes are for 24 December pick-up only. If you want one, order soon and plan your evening around the store pick-up. On the day, depachika and cake shops still sell slices and small cakes, which is all most travellers need. Chicken, the Japanese way: karaage and jidori legs

Chicken is the other half of a Japanese Christmas table, and the most local versions come from Japan's own chains and its regional breeds:

  • Christmas karaage: Karayama, the karaage chain, sells a Christmas Box on 22–25 December only: three bone-in "Christmas chicken" pieces, six of its signature crispy thigh karaage, chicken-breast sticks, herb potatoes and three small fried sweet buns. ¥2,689, or ¥2,489 if you book in store or by phone by 20 December. The bone-in Christmas chicken alone is ¥313.
  • A jidori roast leg: FamilyMart's Christmas roast leg is Awaodori, a local chicken breed from Tokushima, ¥1,890.
  • The everyday version: 7-Eleven's charcoal-grilled roast chicken leg, ¥650.16, for when you just want a chicken leg on the 24th.

Christmas sushi and a bottle of shanmeri

Japanese Christmas tables mix in Japanese food. FamilyMart sells a Christmas sushi set (¥2,790) by pre-order alongside its cakes and chicken.

To drink, there's shanmeri: a sweet sparkling drink in a champagne-style bottle that pops when opened, so children can toast with the adults; in a HugKum survey of parents of 3–12-year-olds, more than half buy it for Christmas at least sometimes. It appears in supermarkets and convenience stores every December, in white (muscat), rosé, red and gold, often in character-printed wrappers. One thing to know: bottles not made for children can contain a little alcohol, so check the label.

Christmas markets, with Japanese twists

Japan's Christmas markets are German in style, but look for the dishes you'd only find here:

  • Tokyo Christmas Market: a matcha "snow dome" au lait, Hokkaido venison steak, and currywurst under a "snowfall" of Hokkaido cheese.
  • Osaka, Tenshiba: Hiroshima oysters at Berlin Haus, as a cream risotto (¥1,500) or an oyster-and-mushroom ajillo (¥1,300).
  • The classics, too: Glühwein (from ¥800 at Tenshiba, ¥100 less in the ¥1,000 mug), stollen at Skytree Town and Roppongi Hills, and Kinderpunsch and hot chocolate for non-drinkers.

A Christmas Eve plan that needs no restaurant booking

Restaurants sell their Christmas courses by reservation (Umeda Sky Building's 39th-floor restaurants, for example, run them on 23–25 December from ¥17,000 a person, for two or more). Here's an evening that works without one:

  1. By 20 Dec Book a Karayama Christmas Box or single Christmas chicken at the shop near your hotel, for pick-up on the 24th.
  2. Afternoon Christmas market for Glühwein and the Japanese-twist dishes while it's light; most are free to enter.
  3. Early evening Collect the chicken, then a Lawson mini cake and a bottle of shanmeri on the way back.
  4. Night Eat in your room, then go out for the illuminations, which run late.

📅 Good to know before you book

  • 30 Nov–1 Dec: the convenience-store early discounts end (7-Eleven app 30 Nov, Lawson and FamilyMart 1 Dec).
  • 20 December: last day for Karayama's early-booking price; the box itself is sold only on 22–25 December, while stocks last.
  • Isetan Shinjuku: several of its own cakes are for 24 December pick-up only.
  • Keep it cold: cream cakes need a fridge; ask your hotel if your room has one before you order a whole cake.

One more thing: the cake that turns 30

7-Eleven's "Christmas Kamakura" turns 30 this Christmas, and for many people in Japan it simply is the convenience-store Christmas cake. For the anniversary it was reworked: lighter whipped cream, a strawberry sauce with more fruit, and a bavarois made with an egg bred for confectionery. About 15 cm across, for four to six, ¥3,240; order by 30 November on the 7NOW app for 10% off.

Quick answers

What do Japanese people eat at Christmas?

A Christmas cake, usually a white sponge cake with whipped cream and strawberries, and chicken, often bone-in roast legs or fried chicken, eaten on Christmas Eve or Christmas Day. Children often get shanmeri, a sweet sparkling drink in a champagne-style bottle. Convenience stores and department stores pre-sell all of these for pick-up around 19–25 December.

What is a Japanese Christmas cake?

Usually a round sponge cake layered and covered with whipped cream and topped with strawberries, the Japanese-style shortcake. Sizes are given in gō: 3-gō feeds one or two, 4-gō two to four, 5-gō (about 15 cm) four to six. Convenience stores and department stores take pre-orders from autumn.

Can tourists buy a Christmas cake without pre-ordering?

Yes, in small sizes. Lawson sells 3-gō mini cakes for one or two (strawberry ¥896, chocolate ¥950) from 15 December with no reservation, and cake shops and depachika sell slices and small cakes on the day, though the popular whole cakes are sold by pre-order.

What is shanmeri?

A sweet, non-alcoholic-style sparkling drink sold in a champagne-like bottle that pops when you open it, so children can toast with the adults. Common flavours are white (muscat), rosé, red and gold; some bottles not made for children may contain a little alcohol, so check the label.
